# Draft: Sleep, Stress, and the Brain

**Status:** Research-backed production draft; not yet rendered or published.

## Editorial frame

This 60-second, vertical Hinglish educational Reel explains that sleep is an active brain process and that poor sleep and stress can reinforce one another. It is designed for a general adult audience and avoids diagnosis, treatment instructions, miracle claims, supplement recommendations, and individual medical advice.

## Approved public claims

The National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke explains that sleep supports learning, memory, concentration, and quick responses, and that the brain remains active during sleep. It also notes that most adults need 7–9 hours of sleep, while individual needs vary.[1] The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute states that sleep deficiency can interfere with learning, focus, reactions, decision-making, emotional regulation, and coping with change.[2] Harvard Medical School describes a two-way relationship between sleep and mood: inadequate sleep can increase irritability and vulnerability to stress, while stress can make sleep harder.[3]

> This Reel is educational only. Persistent sleep problems, severe daytime sleepiness, or mental-health concerns deserve evaluation from a qualified clinician.

## Voiceover script — Hinglish, approximately 55–60 seconds

**0–6s — Hook:** “Aap jab sote ho, aapka brain off nahi hota. It is still working.”

**6–16s — Core idea:** “NIH ke according, sleep ke dauran brain learning, memory aur next-day focus ko support karta hai. Isliye sleep sirf rest nahi—brain maintenance bhi hai.”

**16–29s — Stress loop:** “Problem tab shuru hoti hai jab stress sleep ko disturb karta hai. Aur poor sleep aapko next day zyada irritable, unfocused aur stress-sensitive feel kara sakti hai.”

**29–42s — Practical framing:** “Most adults ke liye 7 to 9 hours common range hai, but everyone is different. Perfect number chase karne ke bajaye, aaj ek small routine protect karo.”

**42–54s — Low-risk habit:** “Same wake-up time, bedtime se pehle bright screens aur caffeine ko limit karna, aur ek calm wind-down routine—ye sleep-friendly habits ho sakti hain.”

**54–60s — Safety close:** “Agar sleep problem regularly chal rahi hai, ya din mein unusual tiredness hai, clinician se baat karna best next step hai.”

## Visual direction

Use a calm, photorealistic science-explainer style with no identifiable patient, clinician, or celebrity. The video should show a dimmed bedroom, a minimal abstract brain-and-memory visualization, a non-alarming stress-loop graphic, and a warm sunrise routine. Avoid medical charts, hospital scenes, diagnosis labels, medication, supplements, and text rendered inside generated image assets. Add final on-screen captions during editorial assembly only.

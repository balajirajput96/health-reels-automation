# REEL-0054 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel exploring "Theory of Mind and the Mentalizing Network"—the cognitive and evolutionary capacity to infer and model the unseen mental states, beliefs, desires, and intentions of other people, mediated by the temporoparietal junction (TPJ) and medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC). It attributes findings to David Premack, Guy Woodruff, and Uta Frith.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults interested in social psychology, empathy, and interpersonal communication. The takeaway is that human social connection relies on 'Theory of Mind'—our ability to realize that others hold different perspectives, knowledge, and beliefs than our own.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: how you can instantly tell someone is hiding a surprise just from their subtle gaze and tone.
2. The Definition: introducing 'Theory of Mind'—mentalizing others' thoughts, beliefs, and emotions.
3. The False-Belief Test: the developmental milestone (around age 4) where children recognize that someone can hold a false belief.
4. The Brain Circuit: temporoparietal junction (TPJ) and medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) driving perspective-taking.
5. Empathy vs Mentalizing: distinguishing cognitive perspective calculation from emotional resonance.
6. Real-World Conflict Resolution: asking "What do they know and feel that I don't see?" to resolve misunderstandings.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is social neuroscience education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic perspective-taking diagrams (Person A model -> Mental projection bubble -> Person B perspective / TPJ activation), lower-third caption-safe area, and no clinical autism diagnosis.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive science to peer-reviewed mentalizing literature (Premack & Woodruff, Baron-Cohen, Frith & Frith, Saxe). Use "Theory of Mind," "mentalizing network," and "perspective taking." Do not diagnose neurodevelopmental disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

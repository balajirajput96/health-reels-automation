# REEL-0122 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Psychological Reactance: Why Being Told What to Do Triggers Rebellion, Autonomy Defense, and How to Influence Ethical Decisions"—how when an individual perceives their behavioral freedom is threatened or constrained, an automatic motivational state (reactance) arises that compels them to do the exact opposite to restore perceived control. It attributes findings to Dr. Jack Brehm and social psychologists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is leaders, parents, managers, communicators, and anyone trying to persuade others or overcome self-sabotage. The takeaway is that demanding compliance creates resistance; giving autonomy and framed choices ("You are free to choose...") dissolves reactance and boosts compliance by up to 40%.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why telling someone "You must not do this" makes them want to do it twice as badly.
2. Jack Brehm's 1966 Theory: discovering Psychological Reactance—the brain's defensive reaction to perceived loss of autonomy.
3. The Autonomy Threat Alarm: the amygdala and prefrontal cortex interpreting forced commands as a loss of freedom, generating rebellious drive.
4. The "Romeo & Juliet" Effect: external prohibition artificially increasing the perceived value and desire for the restricted choice.
5. The "But You Are Free" Hack: research showing adding the phrase "लेकिन फैसला पूरी तरह आपका है" (But the choice is entirely yours) doubles persuasion success.
6. The Two-Choice Method: offering two acceptable options rather than an ultimatum to preserve the feeling of autonomous control.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute reactance research to peer-reviewed literature (Brehm, Guéguen, Dillard, Shen). Use "Psychological Reactance," "Autonomy Preservation," "Choice Architecture," and "Persuasion Psychology."

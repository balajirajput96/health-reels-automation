# REEL-0055 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination Theory (SDT)"—how genuine, sustained drive originates not from external rewards or punishment (extrinsic), but from fulfilling three fundamental psychological needs: Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness. It attributes findings to Edward Deci and Richard Ryan.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, creators, managers, and parents struggling with burnout or carrot-and-stick incentives. The takeaway is that lasting drive flourishes when people experience choice (autonomy), growth (competence), and social purpose (relatedness).

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why external cash prizes or pressure often destroy the pure joy and curiosity of doing something you love.
2. The Overjustification Effect: Edward Deci's soma puzzle experiments demonstrating how extrinsic rewards crowd out internal drive.
3. Self-Determination Theory (SDT): introducing Deci & Ryan's framework for human motivation.
4. Pillar 1 — Autonomy: feeling in control and having choice over how tasks are performed.
5. Pillar 2 — Competence: experiencing meaningful mastery, progress, and effective skill growth.
6. Pillar 3 — Relatedness: connecting with others and feeling that your work contributes to a shared purpose.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is motivational psychology and behavioral science education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic Self-Determination triad diagrams (Three interconnected glowing pillars: Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness), lower-third caption-safe area, and no life-coaching hype.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute motivational science to peer-reviewed Self-Determination Theory literature (Deci & Ryan, Pink). Use "intrinsic motivation," "Self-Determination Theory," "autonomy, competence, and relatedness." Do not make absolute financial or career guarantees.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

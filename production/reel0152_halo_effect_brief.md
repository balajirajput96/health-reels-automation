# REEL-0152 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Halo Effect & Attractiveness Stereotype: Cognitive Spillover, First Impressions, and The 'What is Beautiful is Good' Cognitive Bias"—how the human brain uses a single positive trait (such as physical attractiveness, polite speaking, or neat dressing) to automatically and subconsciously infer that the person is also intelligent, honest, capable, and trustworthy. It attributes findings to Dr. Edward Thorndike and Dr. Solomon Asch.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is job interviewees, leaders, consumers, and general citizens. The takeaway is that the brain is biased towards physical and superficial polish; be aware of the halo effect so you are not deceived by charisma, and use neat presentation ethically to create strong first impressions.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a person who dresses sharp and smiles politely is automatically judged by your brain to be smarter and more honest than they actually are.
2. Edward Thorndike's 1920 Military Study: discovering commanding officers rating attractive soldiers higher in intelligence and leadership across the board.
3. The 'What is Beautiful is Good' Stereotype: psychological cognitive spillover from one positive attribute into unrelated domains.
4. Courtroom & Job Market Impact: research proving polished defendants receiving 20% lighter sentences and higher starting salaries for identical resumes.
5. The Reverse Horns Effect: how a single awkward trait or untidy appearance creates an unfair negative bias across all areas.
6. The Mindful Calibration Rule: in professional evaluation, separate appearance from objective skill; for yourself, maintain clean, professional presentation.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute social psychology and cognitive bias research to peer-reviewed literature (Thorndike, Asch, Dion, Berscheid). Use "The Halo Effect," "Cognitive Spillover," "Attractiveness Stereotype," and "First Impressions."

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

# REEL-0080 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel exploring "The Peak-End Rule and Duration Neglect: How the Brain Evaluates Experiences"—how the human memory system reconstructs past experiences not as an arithmetic average of all moments, but almost entirely based on two singular snapshots: the most intense moment (the Peak) and the final moment (the End), while largely ignoring the total duration (Duration Neglect). It attributes findings to Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman, Barbara Fredrickson, and cognitive behavioral science.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, event planners, physicians, managers, travelers, and general adults. The takeaway is that memory is an editing storyteller; ensuring that any challenging workout, study session, work meeting, or vacation concludes on a high, satisfying note transforms how you remember and anticipate that experience in the future.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a 7-day wonderful vacation ruined by a 2-hour flight delay at the very end is remembered as a "terrible trip."
2. The Concept: Daniel Kahneman's Peak-End Rule—the remembering self vs the experiencing self.
3. The Colonoscopy & Cold Pressor Experiments: longer procedures ending with mild discomfort were remembered as less painful than shorter ones ending abruptly.
4. Duration Neglect: the brain doesn't sum total minutes of pleasant or unpleasant time; it averages the Peak and the End.
5. The Storyteller Brain: memory exists to guide future decisions, compressing narratives into high-salience emotional markers.
6. The Practical Application: always end difficult workouts, long study sessions, or difficult conversations with a positive, pleasant conclusion.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive heuristics and behavioral psychology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic Peak-End Rule curve comparisons (Experience curve -> Peak point + Final End point vs Flat timeline), lower-third caption-safe area, and no business consulting promises.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral science to peer-reviewed literature (Kahneman, Fredrickson, Redelmeier, Schreiber). Use "Peak-End Rule," "Duration Neglect," "remembering self vs experiencing self," and "cognitive heuristics." Do not promise financial or memory recall guarantees.

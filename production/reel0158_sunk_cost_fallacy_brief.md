# REEL-0158 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Sunk Cost Fallacy: Loss Aversion, Escalation of Commitment, and The Psychology of Throwing Good Time After Bad"—how human loss aversion makes people continue investing time, money, and emotional energy into failing projects, bad relationships, or awful movies simply because they have already spent resources on them that can never be recovered. It attributes findings to Nobel laureates Dr. Richard Thaler and Dr. Daniel Kahneman.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is entrepreneurs, students, investors, and general decision makers. The takeaway is that past costs are gone forever; make decisions strictly based on future value, not past expenditures.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why forcing yourself to finish a terrible movie in a theater just because you bought the ticket wastes two things: your money and your precious time.
2. The Sunk Cost Fallacy Discovery: Nobel laureate Richard Thaler defining the irrational human urge to honor past unrecoverable costs.
3. Loss Aversion & Ego Defense: the amygdala feeling emotional pain at acknowledging a loss, driving escalating commitment to bad ventures.
4. The Concorde Fallacy Historical Example: British and French governments pouring billions into supersonic jets long after knowing they were commercially unviable.
5. Sunk Cost vs Opportunity Cost: every hour spent trying to save a dead project is an hour stolen from a brilliant new opportunity.
6. The Clean Slate Question: ask yourself: 'If I had not invested a single rupee or hour into this yet, would I start it today?' If no, walk away immediately.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral economics to peer-reviewed literature (Thaler, Kahneman, Tversky, Arkes, Blumer). Use "The Sunk Cost Fallacy," "Escalation of Commitment," "Loss Aversion," and "Clean Slate Thinking."

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

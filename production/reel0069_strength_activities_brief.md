# REEL-0069 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining that a weekly movement routine can include muscle-strengthening activity in addition to aerobic movement. The narrative separates WHO/CDC public-health guidance from systematic-review evidence and avoids individual exercise prescriptions.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults who may think movement means only walking or running. The takeaway is that strengthening is one component of a broader routine, but exercise choice, intensity, progression, and safety should be adapted to the individual rather than copied from a short video.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific visual references animated with deterministic motion if official visual quotas permit. Native video generation is optional and must only be attempted if the authorized quota is available. If image-generation quota remains exhausted, do not use unrelated visuals; record a blocked preproduction state instead.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: an inclusive adult moving between a brisk walk and a gentle resistance movement in a clean, neutral setting.
2. Guideline context: a calm symbolic scene with major muscle groups represented through full-body movement, without generated text or fake guideline graphics.
3. Individual variability: several adults using different accessible movement formats, avoiding comparison or body-shaming.
4. Evidence boundary: a neutral research-notebook or study-table visual contrasted with a non-guarantee visual cue, without fabricated numbers or claims.
5. Options: bodyweight, resistance band, and light free-weight objects shown safely and without brand labels.
6. Progression: a gradual, controlled movement sequence with rest and technique emphasis, not an intense workout demonstration.
7. Safety: an adult choosing a seated or supported alternative and consulting a qualified professional when symptoms or health context require it.

## Visual constraints

Use consistent realistic editorial documentary visuals with natural light, inclusive adult representation, neutral clothing, uncluttered backgrounds, lower-third caption-safe space, and no logos, fake study screenshots, diagnostic imagery, or text-bearing AI graphics. Do not portray pain, injury, fear, or unsafe exertion.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, use readable multiline blocks, remain bottom-centered, and avoid covering faces, hands, or exercise form. Do not add unverified numeric claims or extra exercise instructions in captions.

## Evidence and safety gates

Do not claim that strength activities guarantee better balance, cognition, mood, weight, longevity, disease prevention, or metabolic health. Attribute the broad twice-weekly major-muscle-group recommendation to WHO and CDC. Describe the systematic-review findings as evidence from structured resistance-training research, not as a guarantee for informal movements. Avoid individualized loads, repetitions, exercise selection, or a command to exercise through pain.

## Disclosure

If produced, state that visuals are AI-generated reference images animated with deterministic motion. Record any official quota limitation. The package is general education only, not diagnosis or individualized prescription, and remains unpublished until separate editorial review.

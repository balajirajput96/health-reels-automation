# REEL-0078 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining that reducing one practical environmental barrier may make a chosen routine easier to repeat. It distinguishes context-and-repetition research from expert commentary and avoids fixed habit timelines, health guarantees, or blame.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults who want to make a self-chosen routine easier to start. The takeaway is to simplify one step, place a needed item near the cue, and revise the setup when it is unsafe, expensive, or inaccessible.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals animated with deterministic motion if official visual quotas permit. Native video generation is optional and may be used only if its official quota is available. If image generation remains exhausted, do not use unrelated visuals; record a blocked preproduction state.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: an adult notices a routine stalled by scattered items and too many setup steps; no moralizing or “lazy” framing.
2. Habit context: ordinary repeated action in a stable context, represented without brain rewiring imagery.
3. Friction: a small practical barrier such as a missing item or long setup; keep the scene realistic.
4. Example: a water bottle placed in a visible location the night before; no health claim or hydration prescription.
5. Evidence boundary: varied people and routines shown over time without a 21-day or fixed-count graphic.
6. Flexible design: the person changes the setup for access, cost, weather, pain, caregiving, or safety; no blame.
7. Safety: inclusive representation of differing abilities and circumstances, with the routine framed as optional and self-chosen.

## Visual constraints

Use a consistent realistic editorial documentary style with soft natural light, inclusive adult representation, accessible environments, lower-third caption-safe space, and no logos, fake study screenshots, timers, streak counters, weight-loss visuals, or medical exercise/diet prescriptions. Materials should look ordinary and affordable.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, use readable multiline blocks, remain bottom-centered, and avoid adding fixed timelines, “healthy for everyone” claims, or moral judgment about consistency.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute context-and-repetition findings to Gardner, Lally, and Wardle. Label the APA Wendy Wood interview as expert commentary. Use “may help” and “variation.” Do not claim that friction reduction guarantees automatic habits, weight loss, or health improvement. The reel is general behavior-science education, not medical advice.

## Disclosure

If produced, state that visuals are AI-generated reference images animated with deterministic motion. Record any official quota limitation. The package remains unpublished until separate editorial review.

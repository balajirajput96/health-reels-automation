# REEL-0073 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining affect labeling as a reflection skill: pausing to put a tentative, specific word to an emotion. It distinguishes a small fMRI laboratory finding from a practical self-reflection cue and avoids treatment claims.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults who sometimes feel emotionally overloaded. The takeaway is to notice and name a feeling without demanding a perfect label, while remembering that this is not a guaranteed calming method or substitute for mental-health care.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals animated with deterministic motion if official visual quotas permit. Native video generation is optional and may be used only if its official quota is available. If image generation remains exhausted, do not use unrelated visuals; record a blocked preproduction state.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: an adult pauses at a quiet desk or window, noticing a heavy moment without dramatic distress.
2. Definition: close-up of a journal page with blank space and a pen, but no generated text or labels.
3. Study finding: neutral research-lab imagery with an adult viewing abstract emotion images on a screen; no fake fMRI screenshots or invented numbers.
4. Brain boundary: tasteful conceptual brain-and-language visual with soft regions of light, not a literal diagnostic scan and no certainty arrows.
5. Practical cue: an adult writes a tentative feeling word or speaks quietly to themselves; use blank or unreadable notebook text so captions carry the approved words.
6. Reflection, not suppression: the person pauses, breathes normally, and chooses a response rather than erasing the emotion; avoid “before/after calm” claims.
7. Safety: a person closes the journal and reaches toward a professional-support context such as a phone or counseling-room doorway, without crisis imagery.

## Visual constraints

Use a consistent realistic editorial documentary style with natural light, inclusive adult representation, neutral clothing, uncluttered backgrounds, lower-third caption-safe space, and no logos, fake study screenshots, diagnostic imagery, brain-scan labels, religious claims, or text-bearing AI graphics. Avoid portraying strong emotion as dangerous or implying that one technique works universally.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, use readable multiline blocks, remain bottom-centered, and avoid adding brain-region claims, symptom labels, or therapeutic instructions that are absent from the approved script.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute the neuroimaging finding to the specific small Lieberman et al. study and state that it suggests a possible pathway. Do not claim that naming emotions shuts off the amygdala, cures anxiety or depression, prevents distress, or replaces therapy. Present the practical cue as optional reflection, not diagnosis or treatment.

## Disclosure

If produced, state that visuals are AI-generated reference images animated with deterministic motion. Record any official quota limitation. The package remains unpublished until separate editorial review.

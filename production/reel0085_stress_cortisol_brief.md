# REEL-0085 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Stress Response: HPA Axis, Cortisol Cascade, and Why Chronic Stress Shrinks the Hippocampus"—how the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis orchestrates the acute stress response through CRH, ACTH, and cortisol release, and how chronically elevated cortisol suppresses hippocampal neurogenesis, impairs prefrontal executive function, and biases the amygdala toward threat hypervigilance. It attributes findings to Bruce McEwen, Robert Sapolsky, and stress neuroscientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experiencing workplace burnout, relationship conflict, financial anxiety, and chronic low-grade stress. The takeaway is that the difference between healthy acute stress (sharpening memory and alertness) and toxic chronic stress (damaging hippocampus and immune function) lies entirely in recovery—and recovery practices (exercise, sleep, social bonding) literally prevent cortisol-induced brain damage.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why some stress makes you sharper and more focused, while constant stress makes you forgetful, anxious, and physically ill.
2. The Acute HPA Cascade: Threat -> Hypothalamus -> CRH -> Pituitary -> ACTH -> Adrenal Glands -> Cortisol + Adrenaline.
3. Acute Stress Benefits: short cortisol burst enhancing attention, immune activation, and memory encoding for important events.
4. Chronic Stress and the Hippocampus: sustained cortisol suppressing BDNF, reducing hippocampal volume (up to 8% smaller in chronic stress).
5. Amygdala Hyperactivation: chronic stress increasing amygdala reactivity, leading to threat hypervigilance, anxiety, and impaired decision-making.
6. Recovery as Neuroprotection: regular aerobic exercise, 7–9 hours sleep, and secure social bonding lowering allostatic load and restoring HPA regulation.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclaimer.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute stress neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (McEwen, Sapolsky, Lupien, Meaney). Use "HPA axis," "cortisol," "hippocampal neurogenesis," and "allostatic load." Do not diagnose anxiety disorders or PTSD.

# REEL-0058 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "Cognitive Stress Appraisal: Challenge vs Threat Mindset"—how the subjective interpretation of high-demand situations determines physiological cardiovascular profiles (threat: vasoconstriction, elevated vascular resistance, high cortisol vs challenge: ventricular dilation, high cardiac output, adaptive arousal). It attributes findings to Richard Lazarus, Jim Blascovich, and Wendy Berry Mendes.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults facing public speaking, exams, deadlines, and high-pressure performance. The takeaway is that bodily arousal (fast heart rate, alertness) is not automatically toxic damage; interpreting arousal as preparation ("Challenge") rather than danger ("Threat") transforms cardiovascular efficiency.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why your pounding heart before a big presentation can either freeze you in fear or supercharge your performance.
2. The Concept: Cognitive Appraisal Theory by Richard Lazarus—how thoughts shape bodily response.
3. The Threat State: perceiving demands as exceeding personal resources, causing blood vessel constriction and panic.
4. The Challenge State: perceiving demands with sufficient coping resources, dilating blood vessels and boosting oxygen to the brain.
5. Cardiovascular Difference: Challenge state mirrors aerobic exercise physiology; Threat state triggers toxic vascular clamp.
6. Arousal Reappraisal: telling yourself "My body is giving me energy and oxygen to succeed" instead of "I am having an anxiety attack."
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is psychophysiology and stress science education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic Biopsychosocial model diagrams (Demands vs Resources scale -> Threat vasoconstriction vs Challenge vasodilation), lower-third caption-safe area, and no medical hypertension claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute psychophysiology to peer-reviewed stress appraisal literature (Lazarus & Folkman, Blascovich & Mendes, Jamieson). Use "stress appraisal," "challenge vs threat mindset," and "arousal reappraisal." Do not diagnose panic or cardiovascular disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

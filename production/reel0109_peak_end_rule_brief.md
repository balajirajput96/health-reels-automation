# REEL-0109 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Peak-End Rule: How the Brain Evaluates Experiences by Peak Intensity and Final Moments, Duration Neglect, and Memory Hacking"—how humans remember past events not by the average of every moment, but almost exclusively by the most emotionally intense point (the peak) and how it concluded (the end). It attributes findings to Daniel Kahneman, Barbara Fredrickson, and cognitive psychologists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is professionals, creators, managers, and individuals wanting to improve their relationships, workouts, and memory of life events. The takeaway is that duration doesn't matter nearly as much as how an experience finishes—always engineer a positive, high-energy conclusion to any meeting, workout, or day.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a great 7-day vacation with a terrible final day is remembered as a bad trip, while a difficult day with a great ending feels wonderful.
2. Daniel Kahneman's Colonoscopy & Ice Water Studies: discovering that total duration of pain or pleasure is largely ignored (duration neglect) in retrospective memory.
3. The Peak-End Rule Defined: memory constructing an evaluation using only two data points: the emotional peak (highest high or lowest low) and the finale.
4. The Experiencing Self vs Remembering Self: the experiencing self living moment-to-moment; the remembering self writing the narrative and deciding future actions.
5. Duration Neglect: why 60 minutes of boring presentation with an inspiring 2-minute climax is rated higher than 30 minutes of steady content.
6. The Finale Hack: finishing your workouts on an enjoyable exercise, ending meetings on praise, and closing your day with a positive reflection.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute peak-end rule research to peer-reviewed literature (Kahneman, Fredrickson, Redelmeier, Varey). Use "Peak-End Rule," "Experiencing vs Remembering Self," "Duration Neglect," and "Cognitive Evaluation."

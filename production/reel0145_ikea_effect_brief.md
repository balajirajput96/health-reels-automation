# REEL-0145 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The IKEA Effect: Effort-Justification Bias, The Labor-Love Link, and The Psychology of Sunk Cognitive Investment"—how humans disproportionately ascribe high value and emotional attachment to products, projects, and ideas that they personally helped create or assemble, valuing self-assembled items up to 63% higher than pre-assembled identical goods. It attributes findings to Dr. Michael Norton, Dr. Daniel Mochon, and Dr. Dan Ariely (Harvard / Duke University).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is entrepreneurs, project managers, teachers, parents, and self-improvers. The takeaway is that people do not value effortless handouts; involve others in the creation process and invest personal effort to build deep commitment and lasting fulfillment.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a simple wooden table you assembled yourself with screws feels 5 times more valuable to you than an expensive designer table.
2. The Norton, Mochon & Ariely Harvard Study: testing consumer valuation of self-built IKEA storage boxes versus pre-assembled boxes.
3. The 63% Valuation Premium: subjects willing to pay 63% more money for items they assembled with their own hands.
4. Effort-Justification Cognitive Bias: the human brain linking invested biological labor directly with intrinsic emotional worth.
5. The Cake Mix Historical Insight: General Mills instant cake mixes failing in the 1950s until they required bakers to crack a fresh real egg.
6. The Co-Creation Habit: in business, parenting, and team projects, give people the autonomy to build solutions themselves rather than handing them finished answers.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral economics to peer-reviewed literature (Norton, Mochon, Ariely, Festinger). Use "The IKEA Effect," "Effort-Justification Bias," "Labor-Love Link," and "63% Valuation Premium."

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

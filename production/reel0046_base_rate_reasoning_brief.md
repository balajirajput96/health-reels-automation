# REEL-0046 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "Base Rate Neglect and Bayesian Reasoning"—how focusing exclusively on specific anecdotal evidence or isolated positive test cues causes people to ignore underlying baseline probabilities (prior base rates). It attributes findings to Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, and modern cognitive decision science.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults evaluating medical screenings, diagnostic probabilities, and everyday statistical claims. The takeaway is that interpreting any specific signal requires factoring in the overall rarity or frequency of the event in the general population.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a 99% accurate test for a rare disease (1 in 10,000) does not mean a 99% chance of having it.
2. Prior Probability: introducing the 'Base Rate'—the fundamental frequency of an event in a population.
3. Diagnostic Signal vs Base Rate: distinguishing true positive hits from unavoidable false alarms in rare contexts.
4. Natural Frequencies: translating abstract percentages (0.01%) into tangible counts (1 in 1,000 people).
5. Bayesian Integration: combining prior base rates with new specific evidence to calculate actual posterior probability.
6. Practical Thinking: asking "How common is this naturally?" before panicking over sensational single test results.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive decision science and probability education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic Bayesian decision trees and frequency dot arrays (1 blue dot in 1,000 grey dots), lower-third caption-safe area, and no medical diagnosis.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute decision science to peer-reviewed Bayesian reasoning and base-rate studies (Kahneman & Tversky, Gigerenzer). Use "prior probability," "base rate," and "natural frequencies." Do not diagnose medical conditions.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

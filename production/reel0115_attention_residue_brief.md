# REEL-0115 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Attention Residue & Context Switching: The Cognitive Tax of Quick Phone Checks, Working Memory Fragmentation, and Re-focus Latency"—how switching between tasks (e.g. checking a quick WhatsApp message while writing) leaves a sticky residue of attention on the previous task, reducing cognitive performance by up to 40% and requiring over 20 minutes to restore full deep focus. It attributes findings to Dr. Sophie Leroy, Dr. Gloria Mark, and Cal Newport.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone constantly checking their phone every 5 minutes while studying or working. The takeaway is that quick checks are not "zero-cost"—every switch leaves cognitive glue on your prefrontal cortex; batching communication preserves cognitive bandwidth.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why checking your phone for just 5 seconds completely destroys the next 20 minutes of your deep work.
2. Sophie Leroy's Attention Residue: discovering that when you switch from Task A to Task B, your attention does not follow immediately—part of your brain remains stuck on Task A.
3. The Cost of Sticky Attention: working with only 60-70% of your cognitive bandwidth because the residue occupies active working memory slots.
4. Gloria Mark's 23-Minute Recovery Rule: research showing it takes an average of 23 minutes and 15 seconds to return to the original level of deep focus after an interruption.
5. Multitasking Myth: the brain cannot process two cognitively demanding tasks in parallel—it rapidly context-switches, depleting glucose and neurotransmitters.
6. The Monotasking Batch Protocol: working in 45-minute single-task sprints with notifications completely silenced, then batching all replies into designated 15-minute windows.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive attention research to peer-reviewed literature (Leroy, Mark, Newport, Ophir). Use "Attention Residue," "Context Switching," "Cognitive Tax," and "Working Memory Fragmentation."

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

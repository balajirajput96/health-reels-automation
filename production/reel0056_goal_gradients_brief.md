# REEL-0056 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "The Goal Gradient Effect and Proximity Acceleration"—how perceived and actual closeness to a finish line accelerates effort, task completion speed, and persistence, and how artificial progress markers (endowed progress) manipulate subjective distance. It attributes findings to Clark Hull, Ran Kivetz, and behavioral economics.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, runners, project managers, and consumers completing loyalty programs. The takeaway is that humans naturally accelerate effort as the goal nears; structuring large projects with frequent visible milestones activates this proximity drive.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why runners sprint fastest during the last 100 meters and why finishing a 10-stamp loyalty card speeds up near the end.
2. The Concept: Clark Hull's 'Goal Gradient Hypothesis'—effort intensifying with spatial/temporal proximity to the reward.
3. Behavioral Evidence: Kivetz & Urminsky studies on loyalty cards and task persistence accelerating near completion.
4. Endowed Progress: how pre-stamping 2 bonus slots on a 12-stamp card makes completion 2x faster than a blank 10-stamp card.
5. Project Structuring: breaking long multi-month goals into distinct micro-finish lines to maintain steady acceleration.
6. Context Boundaries: recognizing when too many fake markers create fatigue or backfire under low-autonomy conditions.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is behavioral decision science education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic progress bar diagrams (Start line -> 80% mark -> Acceleration flame icon -> Finish line reached), lower-third caption-safe area, and no business consulting claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral science to peer-reviewed goal gradient literature (Hull, Kivetz, Urminsky, Nunes & Dreze). Use "Goal Gradient Effect," "endowed progress," and "proximity acceleration." Do not make unconditional productivity guarantees.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

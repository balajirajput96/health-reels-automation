# REEL-0065 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "False Memories and the Misinformation Effect: Why the Brain Rewrites the Past"—how human memory is not a pristine video recording but a constructive, malleable simulation susceptible to post-event misinformation, leading questions, and source confusion (the Deese-Roediger-McDermott DRM paradigm). It attributes findings to Elizabeth Loftus, Henry L. Roediger III, and cognitive psychology.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is general adults, law students, eyewitnesses, and family members arguing over past memories. The takeaway is that high confidence in a memory does not prove historical accuracy; human memories are reconstructed dynamically every time they are recalled.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: how you can vividly and confidently remember an event from your childhood that your parents prove never actually happened.
2. The Concept: Constructive Memory Theory by Elizabeth Loftus—memories are reconstructed jigsaw puzzles, not fixed recordings.
3. The Misinformation Effect: how subtly altering a single word ("smashed" vs "hit") changes estimated car speeds and creates false recollections of broken glass.
4. The DRM Paradigm: hearing "bed, awake, tired, dream, snore" causing 80% of participants to falsely remember the word "sleep."
5. Source Monitoring Errors: confusing an imagined scenario, dream, or family story with a genuine first-person lived experience.
6. The Confidence Trap: research proving that physiological confidence and emotional vividness correlate poorly with objective accuracy.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive memory psychology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic constructive memory diagrams (Event -> Post-event suggestion -> Reconstructed false memory / Video recorder crossed out), lower-third caption-safe area, and no forensic or legal guarantees.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive science to peer-reviewed false memory literature (Loftus & Palmer, Roediger & McDermott, Schacter). Use "false memory," "misinformation effect," "constructive memory," and "source monitoring." Do not give legal testimony advice.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

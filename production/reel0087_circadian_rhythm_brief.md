# REEL-0087 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Circadian Rhythm: Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN), Sunlight Exposure, and Natural Hormonal Cycles"—how the master circadian clock in the hypothalamus (the SCN) uses melanopsin ganglion cells in the retina to sense morning sunlight, triggering a morning cortisol pulse for alertness and setting a 12-to-14-hour timer for night melatonin release. It attributes findings to Charles Czeisler, Satchin Panda, and circadian biologists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is people struggling with morning fatigue, daytime brain fog, insomnia, or shift-work fatigue. The takeaway is that getting 10–15 minutes of direct outdoor morning sunlight suppresses melatonin and anchors your 24-hour hormonal cycle, dramatically improving energy and sleep quality.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why feeling groggy every morning is usually not a lack of sleep, but a misaligned circadian clock.
2. The Master Clock: the Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus regulating body temperature, hormones, and metabolism.
3. Morning Sunlight Mechanism: ipRGCs (melanopsin cells) in the eyes detecting blue-cyan light wavelengths and signaling the SCN.
4. Cortisol Pulse: morning light triggering the natural cortisol peak that activates alertness, immune defense, and metabolism.
5. Night Melatonin Timer: morning light exposure setting the timer for nocturnal pineal gland melatonin secretion 14 hours later.
6. The Evening Light Trap: screen exposure and artificial bright light suppressing melatonin and shifting the SCN clock backwards.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute circadian rhythm research to peer-reviewed literature (Czeisler, Panda, Takahashi, Lockley). Use "circadian rhythm," "Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN)," "melanopsin," and "melatonin timing." Do not diagnose delayed sleep phase syndrome or prescribe sleep aids.

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

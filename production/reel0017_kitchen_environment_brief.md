# REEL-0017 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining how kitchen organization and visual cues influence daily snack choices and subconscious eating patterns. It attributes findings to behavioral research from Cornell University and Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults seeking simple, evidence-backed environmental adjustments to support mindful daily food choices. The takeaway is that making healthy options visible and reducing visual clutter supports conscious snacking decisions.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: a question about how the kitchen layout shapes eating decisions.
2. Evidence: Cornell University behavioral research reference.
3. Observation: visible snacks leading to increased mindless consumption.
4. Positive cue: placing fruit bowls in prominent view encouraging healthier snacking.
5. Influence: subtle environmental cues shaping subconscious daily routines.
6. Individual variation: acknowledging that habits and individual home setups vary.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is not medical advice.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, realistic, well-lit kitchen imagery comparing organized vs cluttered counter spaces, lower-third caption-safe area, and no medical claims or clinical guarantees.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute environmental behavioral research to Cornell and Harvard HSPH. Use "may influence" and "supportive." Do not claim kitchen layout cures metabolic or eating disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

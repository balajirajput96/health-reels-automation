# REEL-0060 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel exploring "Social Buffering: How Human Connection Downregulates the Neurobiology of Stress"—how the physical presence, gentle touch, or supportive voice of a trusted companion blunts HPA axis reactivity, dampens amygdala hyperactivity, releases neuropeptide oxytocin, and stimulates parasympathetic vagal brake on heart rate. It attributes findings to James Coan, Stephen Porges (Polyvagal Theory), and social neurobiology.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experiencing isolation, high stress, or interpersonal tension. The takeaway is that humans are obligate social mammals whose autonomic nervous systems co-regulate; holding the hand of a trusted partner or hearing a warm voice directly reduces biological stress markers.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why simply holding a loved one's hand during a painful or stressful moment instantly lowers physical pain and fear.
2. The Concept: James Coan's fMRI hand-holding experiments demonstrating 'Social Buffering of the Neural Threat Response.'
3. Neurobiology of Connection: touch and supportive vocal tones triggering hypothalamic release of oxytocin neuropeptides.
4. Amygdala Dampening: oxytocin binding to inhibitory GABA interneurons in the amygdala, reducing alarm signals.
5. Vagal Co-Regulation: the ventral vagus nerve acting as a biological brake to reduce heart rate and blood pressure.
6. Evolutionary Baseline: Social Baseline Theory—the human brain calculates that navigating life alone is metabolically more costly than sharing burdens.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is social neuroscience and autonomic biology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic social buffering circuits (Neural Threat Spike -> Hand-hold / Social Cue -> Oxytocin Wave -> Vagal Brake engagement), lower-third caption-safe area, and no relationship therapy promises.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute social neuroscience to peer-reviewed social buffering literature (Coan et al., Eisenberger, Porges, Heinrichs). Use "social buffering," "oxytocin," "vagal co-regulation," and "Social Baseline Theory." Do not offer medical relationship therapy.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

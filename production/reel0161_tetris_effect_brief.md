# REEL-0161 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Tetris Effect (Procedural Memory Transfer): Hypnagogic Imagery, Neural Plasticity, and Cognitive Involuntary Memory"—how engaging intensely in repetitive spatial or cognitive tasks (like Tetris, coding, or spreadsheet work) primes procedural memory networks so deeply that the brain continues generating involuntary mental images, dreams, and geometric patterns during hypnagogia (the transition between wakefulness and sleep). It attributes findings to Dr. Robert Stickgold (Harvard Medical School).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is gamers, coders, students, and professionals experiencing work flashbacks before sleep. The takeaway is that what you immerse your visual brain in for hours before bed programs your involuntary sleep thoughts; disconnect from screens 60 minutes before bedtime to prevent cognitive spillover.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why closing your eyes to sleep after playing a video game or working on Excel makes falling blocks or spreadsheets flash before your eyelids.
2. The Harvard Medical School Discovery: Dr. Robert Stickgold identifying the 'Tetris Effect' in amnesic and healthy subjects.
3. Procedural Memory Consolidation: the brain replaying recent repetitive motor and visual patterns to encode neural pathways into long-term circuits.
4. Hypnagogic Replay Window: during the alpha-to-theta brainwave transition, subconscious sensory buffers empty their active logs into conscious imagery.
5. Real-World Cognitive Spillover: accountants seeing numbers in their sleep, coders debugging in dreams, chess players seeing boards in floor tiles.
6. The Mindful Digital Winddown: stop high-intensity repetitive visual stimuli 60 minutes before bed to allow your subconscious mental slate to clear.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Stickgold, Mallett, Wamsley, Walker). Use "The Tetris Effect," "Procedural Memory," "Hypnagogic Imagery," and "Neural Plasticity."

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

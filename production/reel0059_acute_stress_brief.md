# REEL-0059 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "The Acute Stress Response and Autonomic Orchestration: Sympathetic Surge & HPA Axis"—how the body mobilizes survival resources during immediate acute challenges via rapid sympathetic nervous system activation (adrenaline/noradrenaline, heart rate, bronchial dilation) followed by the slower, stabilizing Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) axis cortisol release. It attributes findings to Walter Cannon, Hans Selye, and Bruce McEwen.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experiencing intense physical arousal during sudden emergency or unexpected shocks. The takeaway is that acute stress is an evolutionary survival cascade designed to rapidly deploy glucose, blood flow, and vigilance; acute stress is adaptive, whereas chronic un-terminated stress causes allostatic wear.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why your pupils dilate, breathing quickens, and palms sweat in less than a second during a sudden near-accident.
2. Two-Stage Cascade: the dual evolutionary alarm system—fast neural sympathetic rush and slower endocrine HPA response.
3. Stage 1 — SAM Axis: Sympathetic-Adrenomedullary release of epinephrine within milliseconds, boosting cardiac output and visual focus.
4. Stage 2 — HPA Axis: Hypothalamus signaling pituitary and adrenals to release cortisol, mobilizing glucose and suppressing digestion.
5. Adaptive Mobilization: prioritizing immediate muscular power and sensory acuity over long-term immune/metabolic maintenance.
6. Physiological De-escalation: parasympathetic rebound (vagus nerve) restoring baseline homeostasis once threat passes.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is human physiology and neuroendocrinology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic autonomic nervous system diagrams (Brainstem alert -> Adrenal flash -> SAM fast path vs HPA endocrine curve), lower-third caption-safe area, and no endocrine diagnosis.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute neuroendocrinology to peer-reviewed acute stress literature (Cannon, Selye, McEwen, Sapolsky). Use "acute stress response," "sympathetic nervous system," "HPA axis," and "cortisol & adrenaline." Do not diagnose adrenal insufficiency or endocrine disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

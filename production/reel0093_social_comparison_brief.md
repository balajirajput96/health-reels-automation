# REEL-0093 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Social Comparison Theory: Upward vs Downward Comparison, Neural Reward/Pain Circuits, and Social Media Anxiety"—how the brain automatically evaluates self-worth relative to peers through upward comparison (activating insular threat/pain networks) and downward comparison (activating ventral striatum reward networks). It attributes findings to Leon Festinger, Social Neuroscientists, and Media Psychology researchers.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is social media users experiencing envy, inadequacy, or anxiety while browsing feeds. The takeaway is that social comparison is an ancient evolutionary drive designed for small group status, but scrolling curated highlights triggers constant, artificial upward comparison—understanding the brain's reaction allows users to set healthy boundaries.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why scrolling social media for 10 minutes can make you feel inadequate even if your life is going great.
2. Festinger's Social Comparison Theory: Leon Festinger's insight that humans determine personal and social worth by comparing themselves to others.
3. Upward Comparison Neural Circuitry: comparing to superior highlights activating the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) and insula (social pain).
4. Downward Comparison Neural Circuitry: comparing to worse-off peers activating the ventral striatum (dopamine/reward).
5. The Curated Highlight Fallacy: social media exposing the brain to unrealistic, top 1% upward comparisons 24/7.
6. Reframing & Digital Hygiene: shifting from external comparison to internal progress tracking and curating social media feeds.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute social comparison research to peer-reviewed literature (Festinger, Fiske, Vogel, Krayer). Use "Social Comparison Theory," "upward comparison," "social pain network," and "digital hygiene." Do not diagnose clinical depression or body dysmorphia.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

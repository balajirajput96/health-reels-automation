# REEL-0025 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "choice overload" and decision fatigue—how having too many choices can paralyze decision-making, increase cognitive load, and reduce post-decision satisfaction. It attributes findings to behavioral economics and cognitive science.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experiencing analysis paralysis when faced with excessive daily options (shopping, tasks, menus). The takeaway is that simplifying choice sets and pre-committing to default options reduces cognitive strain.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why having 30 options often feels more stressful than having 3.
2. Evidence: behavioral economics research on choice overload and decision fatigue.
3. Mechanism: high cognitive load and regret anticipation during complex evaluations.
4. Consequence: decision paralysis or decreased post-choice satisfaction.
5. Strategy: limiting daily options (satisficing) and setting default choices.
6. Context: recognizing situations where variety is genuinely desirable.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is behavioral science education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, minimalist comparison cards (overcrowded menu vs 3 clear curated options), lower-third caption-safe area, and no exaggerated claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral research to classic choice overload and cognitive fatigue studies. Use "may reduce" and "decision strategy." Do not claim choice architecture cures clinical OCD or anxiety disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

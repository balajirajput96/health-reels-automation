# REEL-0011 — journal questions for habit reflection

## Unique angle

Reels 0002–0010 cover habit timelines, if–then planning, automaticity, context limits, fresh starts, cues, and how to read habit studies. Reel 0011 turns the evidence into a research-literacy reflection tool: a short journal can record context, repetition, and perceived automaticity, but the record is not itself a assuranced behaviour-change intervention.

## Format

Target: 9:16 vertical, approximately 60–80 seconds, Hindi (India), warm informative narration, burned-in captions, original scientific visuals, and a general-education disclaimer. No clinical assessment, management, clinical claims, or fixed habit timeline promise.

## Six-scene plan

| Time | Visual action | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | A calm learner opens a blank journal beside a recurring routine | Hook / observation |
| 10–22 s | Four abstract journal prompts connect to cue, context, action, and feeling | Practical synthesis |
| 22–35 s | A daily tick-sheet and a smartphone study panel show recording, not magic | Source-method example |
| 35–48 s | Stable and changing contexts are compared visually | Conditional evidence |
| 48–62 s | Automaticity gauge rises gradually with visible variability | Measurement / limit |
| 62–80 s | The four questions become a gentle self-reflection loop | Takeaway |

## Visual direction

Use the established deep navy, electric teal, and warm saffron scientific motion-graphics style with the recurring young Indian adult learner/researcher. Any journal or app panels must be graphical only; no readable words, numbers, logos, watermarks, or fake study labels. Keep the lower quarter dark and caption-safe.

## Evidence boundaries

The sources support recording behaviour, context, and perceived automaticity as an observation or self-monitoring aid. The 2020 field study used daily app measurements and discussed monitoring as a possible facilitator, but the reel must not claim journaling alone causes habit formation. Habit strength and time-to-automaticity vary across people and behaviours, and evidence quality is mixed.

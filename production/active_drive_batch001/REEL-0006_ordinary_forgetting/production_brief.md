# REEL-0006 — सामान्य भूल और prospective memory

## Unique angle

Reel 0002 covered habit-formation time, Reel 0003 covered if–then planning, Reel 0004 covered automaticity measurement, and Reel 0005 covered a small self-monitoring loop. Reel 0006 explains why intending to do something later is not the same as remembering to execute it while another task is competing for attention. This is a prospective-memory concept, not a diagnosis or a moral judgment.

## Format

Target: 9:16 vertical, approximately 60–75 seconds, Hindi (India), warm documentary voiceover, burned-in captions, original scientific visuals, and a general-education disclaimer. Do not suggest that everyday forgetfulness proves ADHD, dementia, depression, or any other condition.

## Six-scene plan

| Time | Visual action | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | Person intends to do one task later while several competing tasks appear | Hook / definition |
| 10–21 s | A future intention waits in a glowing memory tray while attention shifts | Prospective-memory concept |
| 21–34 s | A demanding task creates interference around the delayed intention | Systematic-review framing |
| 34–47 s | Important intention remains present but its retrieval cue is faint | Review finding |
| 47–59 s | A visible, ethical reminder appears as an optional practical aid | Practical application |
| 59–75 s | Takeaway: lapse is information, not a character verdict; seek help for persistent impairment | Safety / summary |

## Evidence rules

Use prospective memory as the definition of remembering to perform an intended action later. State that ongoing task demands can influence prospective remembering, but do not claim every lapse is caused by workload. State that importance alone does not guarantee successful remembering. Keep reminder advice general and optional; do not repeat Reel 0003’s if–then intervention claim.

## Visual direction

Use the established deep navy, electric teal, and saffron scientific motion-graphics style. Show a calm young Indian adult, delayed intention as a small glowing card or orb, competing task layers, and one gentle reminder cue. No generated readable text, numbers, labels, medical symbols, or diagnostic imagery. Keep the lower quarter caption-safe.

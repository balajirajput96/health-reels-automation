# Reel 0006 source validation — ordinary forgetting and future intentions

## Unique angle

Reel 0002 covered habit-formation time, Reel 0003 covered if–then planning, Reel 0004 covered automaticity measurement, and Reel 0005 covered a small self-monitoring loop. Reel 0006 will explain ordinary forgetting as a prospective-memory problem: intending to do something later is not the same as remembering to execute it while other tasks compete for attention.

## Verified sources

1. **Matos, P., et al. (2020). _How Does Performing Demanding Activities Influence Prospective Remembering? A Systematic Review._** Accessible record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7594016/
   - Search result and accessible article identify this as a systematic review of how ongoing task load affects prospective remembering.
   - Safe claim: demanding ongoing activity can be relevant to prospective remembering; avoid assigning one universal effect size without the paper-specific table.

2. **Koo, Y. W., et al. (2022). _The effects of implementation intentions on prospective memory._ Frontiers in Psychology.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.905860
   - The accessible article description defines a common failure: people can fail to remember to perform an intended action at the right time or appropriate occasion despite intending to do it.
   - This source is used only to define the intention–execution problem; Reel 0003 already covers implementation intentions, so Reel 0006 will not repeat the intervention claim.

3. **Einstein, G. O., & McDaniel, M. A. (2000). _Prospective memory: Forgetting intentions over short delays._** Washington University record: https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/prospective-memory-and-aging-forgetting-intentions-over-short-del/
   - Prospective memory concerns intentions that must be maintained until they can be carried out later.
   - Safe claim: an intention can require delayed retrieval, so a lapse is not automatically evidence of laziness or lack of caring.

4. **McDaniel, M. A., & Einstein, G. O. (2014). _How important is importance for prospective memory? A review._ Frontiers in Psychology.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00657
   - The review discusses time-based and event-based prospective memory and notes that people can forget intentions even when they consider them important.
   - Safe claim: importance alone does not guarantee successful remembering.

## Evidence classification

| Reel statement | Classification | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Remembering to do something later is prospective memory | Psychology definition | Not a diagnosis. |
| Other demanding tasks can compete with prospective remembering | Systematic-review framing | Do not claim every lapse has this cause. |
| An intention can be important and still be forgotten | Review finding | Avoid moralizing or clinical inference. |
| A visible cue or calendar reminder can be a practical aid | General practical suggestion | Do not present it as a guaranteed treatment or repeat the if–then mechanism in Reel 0003. |

## Safety boundary

This is general educational content. Do not diagnose ADHD, dementia, depression, or any other condition from ordinary forgetting. Persistent, worsening, or functionally impairing memory problems should be discussed with a qualified clinician.

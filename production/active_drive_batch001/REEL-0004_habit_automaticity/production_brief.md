# REEL-0004 — Frequent काम और automatic habit एक ही चीज़ नहीं

## Unique angle

Reel 0002 covered how long habits may take; Reel 0003 covered if–then plans. Reel 0004 explains how researchers think about habit automaticity and why simply doing something often—or identifying with it—does not prove that the behaviour is automatic in every context.

## Format

Target: 9:16 vertical, approximately 60 seconds, Hindi (India), clear voiceover, burned-in captions, original scientific visual metaphors, and a general-education disclaimer. No clinical assessment or moral judgment.

## Clip plan

| Time | Visual action | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | A person repeats a behaviour in one setting while the same object appears in a new setting | Hook / distinction |
| 8–18 s | Context cue connects to a response through a neural-style pathway | Review-supported construct |
| 18–30 s | Frequency counter and automaticity meter separate into two streams | Measurement distinction |
| 30–42 s | Research questionnaire turns into a four-item automaticity lens | Peer-reviewed measurement study |
| 42–53 s | Context changes; the old cue weakens while deliberate choice remains visible | Context dependence / boundary |
| 53–63 s | Takeaway: “Frequent ≠ automatically triggered” with source cue and disclaimer | Summary |

## Evidence rules

Use Wood and Rünger (2016) to explain recurring contexts, repeated responses, and the relationship between habit and deliberate goals. Use Gardner et al. (2012) to explain that the 12-item Self-Report Habit Index mixes automatic activation, frequency, and identity, and that their four-item SRBAI was designed to focus on automaticity-related items. Do not present SRBAI as a diagnostic test or claim that a self-report score proves an objective neural habit. Avoid claiming that habits eliminate conscious choice.

## Visual direction

Use the established deep navy, electric teal, and warm saffron scientific motion-graphics language. Contrast a repeated routine in one context with the same person/object in a changed context. Keep the lower quarter caption-safe. Do not generate readable labels, numbers, or paper text; captions and metadata carry the exact claims.

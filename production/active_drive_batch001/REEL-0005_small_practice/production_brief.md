# REEL-0005 — एक छोटा tracking अभ्यास

## Unique angle

Reel 0002 covered time-to-form, Reel 0003 covered if–then planning, and Reel 0004 covered automaticity measurement. Reel 0005 introduces a small, low-pressure observation loop: choose one behaviour, note it briefly, review the pattern, and adjust. The evidence boundary is explicit: self-monitoring and feedback can help in some contexts, but no single technique works equally for every behaviour or person.

## Format

Target: 9:16 vertical, approximately 60 seconds, Hindi (India), warm voiceover, burned-in captions, original scientific visuals, and a general-education disclaimer. Avoid obsessive tracking or diagnosis.

## Six-scene plan

| Time | Visual action | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | Overloaded goal list becomes one small observation card | Hook / practical framing |
| 8–18 s | A simple daily behaviour is observed without judgment | Self-regulation concept |
| 18–29 s | Three-day dots become a small pattern, not a verdict | Feedback and monitoring |
| 29–42 s | Evidence-synthesis bookshelf shows feedback, goals, monitoring as separate tools | Meta-review finding |
| 42–53 s | A person adjusts the environment after reviewing the pattern | Context-sensitive application |
| 53–67 s | Takeaway loop: choose, note, review, adjust | Summary / safety boundary |

## Evidence rules

Use Hennessy et al. (2020) to state that meta-analyses identify personalized feedback, goal setting, and self-monitoring as potentially successful components, but none is consistently successful across all health behaviours and populations. Use Michie et al. (2018) to explain that behaviour-change techniques are difficult to isolate in multi-component interventions and that conclusions need qualifications. Use Singh et al. (2024) only for the broader context that habit formation varies substantially and evidence quality is mixed; do not add a new timeline claim.

## Visual direction

Use the established deep navy, electric teal, and saffron scientific motion-graphics language. Show a small notebook/card, neutral observation dots, and gentle feedback arrows. No generated readable text, numbers, labels, or medical imagery. Keep the lower quarter caption-safe.

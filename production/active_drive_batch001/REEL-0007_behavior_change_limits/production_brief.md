# REEL-0007 — behaviour-change techniques की सीमाएँ

## Unique angle

Reels 0002–0006 already cover habit-formation time, if–then planning, automaticity measurement, self-monitoring, and ordinary forgetting. Reel 0007 focuses on a separate evidence lesson: a technique that appears useful in one behaviour or context may not work the same way for every person. The practical takeaway is to treat a technique as a small experiment, observe fit, and adapt without self-blame.

## Format

Target: 9:16 vertical, approximately 60–75 seconds, Hindi (India), warm documentary narration, burned-in captions, original scientific visuals, and a general-education disclaimer. No promise of treatment, weight loss, or guaranteed behaviour change.

## Six-scene plan

| Time | Visual action | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | One person tries the same tool in two different settings with different outcomes | Hook / context |
| 10–22 s | A behaviour-change toolkit opens into several distinct behaviours | Definition / scope |
| 22–35 s | Systematic-review cards show different contexts, populations, and delivery modes | Review-methods evidence |
| 35–47 s | Short-term positive signal fades into a longer timeline with uncertainty | Durability limitation |
| 47–59 s | Person tests one small adjustment and observes the result neutrally | Practical experiment |
| 59–75 s | Takeaway: fit matters; adapt or seek support rather than moralizing | Safety / summary |

## Visual direction

Use the established deep navy, electric teal, and saffron scientific motion-graphics style. Show one recurring young Indian adult, a modular toolkit, context panels, gentle comparison imagery, and a small experiment loop. No generated readable text, numbers, labels, logos, or watermarks. Keep the lower quarter caption-safe.

## Evidence boundaries

Use the reviews to support context-sensitive interpretation and heterogeneity, not a precise universal effect size. State that short-term improvement is not proof of durable change. Avoid saying techniques never work or that behaviour is fully determined by context. Present adaptation as a practical suggestion, not a validated treatment protocol.

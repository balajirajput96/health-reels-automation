# Reel 0007 source validation — behaviour-change limits and context

## Unique angle

Reels 0002–0006 already cover habit-formation time, if–then planning, automaticity measurement, self-monitoring, and ordinary forgetting. Reel 0007 focuses on a different caution: a behaviour-change technique that helps in one behaviour or context may not work the same way for another person or situation. The reel will encourage small experiments and adaptation, not a universal formula.

## Verified sources

1. **Michie et al. (2018), _Evaluating the effectiveness of behavior change techniques in health behavior-change interventions: a review of methods._** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6062857/
   - The review evaluates methods used to identify effective behaviour-change techniques for given behaviours in given contexts.
   - Safe claim: effectiveness should be interpreted with the behaviour and context in view; do not claim a universal winning technique.

2. **Gardner et al. (2023), _Developing habit-based health behaviour change interventions: twenty years of progress?_** https://doi.org/10.1080/08870446.2021.2003362
   - Search evidence describes a systematic review of interventions promoting habit formation, with encouraging short-term effects but limited assessment of longer-term behaviour change.
   - Safe claim: short-term improvement is not the same as proof of durable change.

3. **Singh et al. (2024), _Time to Form a Habit: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Health Behaviour Habit Formation and Its Determinants._** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/
   - The systematic review/meta-analysis reports habit-score improvements across different habits while also examining determinants.
   - Safe claim: habit formation varies across behaviours and people; avoid converting pooled evidence into a fixed day-count rule.

4. **Feil et al. (2021), _A Systematic Review Examining the Relationship Between Habit and Physical Activity._** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.626750
   - The review discusses how the predictive influence of intention can weaken as habit strength increases.
   - Safe claim: intention and habit are related but not interchangeable; do not imply intention becomes irrelevant for everyone.

## Evidence classification

| Reel statement | Classification | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A technique can behave differently across behaviours and contexts | Review-methods framing | Do not claim a precise moderator without reporting the study. |
| Short-term improvement does not prove durable change | Systematic-review limitation | Avoid saying interventions never last. |
| Habit formation varies by behaviour and person | Synthesis of systematic reviews | Avoid universal timelines. |
| Treat a technique as a small experiment and adapt | Practical suggestion | Not a guaranteed intervention or treatment. |

## Safety boundary

This is general education. It is not a promise of weight loss, treatment, diagnosis, or a substitute for professional care. For distress, compulsion, disordered eating, addiction, or persistent functional impairment, recommend qualified support.

# REEL-0045 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel exploring "The Framing Effect" and Prospect Theory in behavioral economics—how presenting logically equivalent information in terms of positive gains ("90% survival rate") versus negative losses ("10% mortality rate") fundamentally alters human risk preferences and decision-making. It attributes findings to Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults making daily financial, health, or lifestyle choices. The takeaway is that conscious awareness of gain-versus-loss framing prevents cognitive manipulation and enables rational evaluation of identical statistical choices.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: 90% fat-free vs 10% fat—why identical products trigger opposite buying decisions.
2. The Evidence: Kahneman and Tversky's Asian Disease Problem and Prospect Theory.
3. Loss Aversion: losses psychologically weighing nearly twice as heavily as equivalent gains.
4. Gain Frame vs Loss Frame: gain framing inducing risk aversion; loss framing inducing risk seeking.
5. Everyday Contexts: health choices, medical consultations, financial investments, and advertising.
6. Cognitive Reframing: re-evaluating any important proposition in both positive and negative formats before deciding.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is behavioral economics and cognitive science.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic decision trees and scale balance cards (Gain +90% vs Loss -10%), lower-third caption-safe area, and no financial advice.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral economics to peer-reviewed Prospect Theory and framing literature (Tversky & Kahneman). Use "framing effect," "loss aversion," and "cognitive bias." Do not give financial advice.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

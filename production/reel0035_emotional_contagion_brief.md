# REEL-0035 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "Emotional Contagion"—how humans automatically mimic and synchronize facial expressions, vocalizations, postures, and emotional valences with those around them, converging emotionally through mirror neuron systems and motor mimicry.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults interested in social neuroscience and interpersonal dynamics. The takeaway is that emotions spread socially via subtle nonverbal mimicry, highlighting the value of choosing positive social environments and setting emotional boundaries.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: walking into a tense room and feeling immediate physical tension.
2. Evidence: social neuroscience research on emotional contagion and motor mimicry (Hatfield et al.).
3. Mechanism: automatic micro-mimicry of facial muscles, postures, and vocal inflections.
4. Neural mirror systems: how motor imitation feeds back into personal emotional experiencing.
5. Social impact: how collective moods spread across teams, families, and digital spaces.
6. Boundary management: practicing mindful awareness to maintain personal emotional equilibrium.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is social neuroscience education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, minimalist social resonance wave graphics and interpersonal mirror network diagrams, lower-third caption-safe area, and no paranormal claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute social neuroscience to peer-reviewed emotional contagion and mimicry studies. Use "may synchronize" and "motor mimicry." Do not frame contagion as supernatural energy.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

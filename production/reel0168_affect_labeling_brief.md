# REEL-0168 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Affect Labeling and Emotional Granularity: Why Naming Your Feelings Calms the Brain's Alarm System"—explaining functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) research demonstrating that putting feelings into precise words dampens amygdala reactivity while activating the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (rvlPFC). It attributes findings to Dr. Matthew Lieberman (UCLA) and Dr. Lisa Feldman Barrett (Northeastern University).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults dealing with workplace stress, interpersonal tension, and emotional overwhelm. The takeaway is that moving from a vague "I feel bad" to a granular "I feel unappreciated or apprehensive" physically quiets the brain's threat circuitry, transforming reactivity into cognitive clarity.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why simply saying "I feel anxious" or "I feel disappointed" physically reduces your heart rate and emotional distress.
2. UCLA fMRI Brain Scans: Matthew Lieberman's discovery of neural braking mechanisms during emotional labeling.
3. The Amygdala Fire Alarm: how threat and panic circuits flare up when emotions remain unarticulated and vague.
4. Prefrontal Cortex Braking: verbal labeling activating the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex to downregulate the amygdala.
5. Emotional Granularity: Lisa Feldman Barrett's insight that having a rich emotional vocabulary prevents emotional overwhelm.
6. The Two-Second Pause Rule: when stressed, take a breath, name the exact emotion specifically, and notice the mental shift.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Lieberman et al., Barrett, Hariri et al., Torre & Lieberman). Use "Affect Labeling," "Emotional Granularity," "Amygdala Downregulation," "Prefrontal Cortex," and "Matthew Lieberman."

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

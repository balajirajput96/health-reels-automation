# REEL-0062 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel exploring "Stress and Attentional Tunneling: The Weapon Focus Effect & Easterbrook Hypothesis"—how intense acute physiological arousal narrows the attentional field, sharply focusing visual perception onto central high-threat cues while completely blinding the brain to peripheral contextual information. It attributes findings to J.A. Easterbrook, Elizabeth Loftus, and cognitive neuroscience.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is drivers, pilots, emergency responders, students, and professionals making high-stakes decisions under acute pressure. The takeaway is that acute stress causes involuntary "tunnel vision" (Easterbrook cue-utilization narrowing); intentionally expanding visual periphery and pausing wide resets attentional breadth.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why in an emergency or sudden robbery, eyewitnesses can vividly describe the gun/weapon but cannot remember the criminal's face.
2. The Concept: Easterbrook's Cue-Utilization Hypothesis—as arousal rises, the total range of perceived cues narrows.
3. Central vs Peripheral Tradeoff: optimal arousal filters noise, but severe stress blinds the brain to crucial peripheral facts.
4. The Weapon Focus Effect: visual fixation locked onto immediate lethal threat due to noradrenaline surge in the amygdala.
5. High-Pressure Blindspots: how students in panic miss obvious exam instructions or drivers in sudden skid stare at the obstacle (target fixation).
6. Expanding the Periphery: utilizing panoramic vision (soft gaze looking at room edges) to disengage acute adrenergic tunnel vision.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive psychology and attentional science education.

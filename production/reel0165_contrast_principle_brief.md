# REEL-0165 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Contrast Principle (Perceptual Contrast Bias): Cognitive Anchoring, Sensory Baseline Adaptation, and Cialdini's Persuasion Architecture"—how human perception does not measure absolute stimuli, but measures differences between two successive items; putting your hand in warm water feels scalding if it was previously in freezing water, and a $100 sweater feels ridiculously cheap immediately after looking at a $1,000 tailored suit. It attributes findings to Dr. Robert Cialdini (Arizona State University).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is consumers, negotiators, shoppers, and decision makers. The takeaway is that salespeople use the contrast principle to make expensive items look like pocket change; always evaluate purchases on their isolated objective value rather than the preceding contrast.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a ₹2,000 shirt feels like a massive bargain right after looking at a ₹20,000 suit, but exorbitant if looked at by itself.
2. The Classical Three-Bowl Water Physics Demonstration: freezing water, room-temperature water, and hot water resetting sensory baselines.
3. Robert Cialdini's Persuasion Law: perceptual contrast altering brain evaluations without the conscious mind realizing it.
4. Real Estate 'Set-Up' Houses: showing clients terrible, overpriced dump houses first so the subsequent standard house looks like a palace.
5. Retail Add-On Traps: selling you the ₹50,000 phone first, then casually adding the ₹3,000 case and ₹2,000 screen protector that feel trivial in contrast.
6. The Isolated Evaluation Rule: when evaluating accessories, prices, or offers, separate them completely from the anchor and judge their value in isolation.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral psychology to peer-reviewed literature (Cialdini, Helson, Tversky, Ariely). Use "The Contrast Principle," "Perceptual Contrast Bias," "Robert Cialdini," and "Cognitive Anchoring."

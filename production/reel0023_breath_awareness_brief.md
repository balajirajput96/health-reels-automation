# REEL-0023 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining how slow, rhythmic breath awareness engages the parasympathetic nervous system via vagus nerve stimulation, modulating heart rate variability (HRV) and acute physiological arousal. It attributes findings to autonomic physiology and neuroscience research.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults seeking a physiological understanding of why intentional breathing calms the body during moments of acute stress. The takeaway is that slow exhalations activate parasympathetic pathways, supporting physiological regulation.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why taking a slow, deep breath physically changes heart rhythm.
2. Evidence: autonomic physiology research on respiration and vagal tone.
3. Mechanism: prolonged exhalation activating the vagus nerve and parasympathetic brake.
4. Metric: heart rate variability (HRV) reflecting autonomic nervous system flexibility.
5. Practice: using 4-6 second slow breath cycles as a conscious regulatory cue.
6. Individual response: noting individual baseline differences in respiratory rates.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating breathing exercises do not replace clinical anxiety therapy.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, calm, physiological diagrams (respiratory pacing visuals, gentle wave animations, HRV indicators), lower-third caption-safe area, and no medical cure claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute autonomic science to peer-reviewed physiology research. Use "may activate" and "regulatory mechanism." Do not claim breathwork cures clinical panic disorder or cardiovascular diseases.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

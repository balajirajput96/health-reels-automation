# REEL-0107 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Science of Breathwork: Physiological Sigh, Alveoli Re-inflation, CO2 Clearance, and Instant Parasympathetic Reset"—how two rapid nasal inhales followed by an extended oral exhale directly offloads carbon dioxide, reinflates collapsed lung alveoli, and slows heart rate via the respiratory sinus arrhythmia mechanism within 3 breaths. It attributes findings to Jack Feldman, Mark Krasnow, and Andrew Huberman.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone experiencing acute stress, panic, palpitations, or mental fatigue. The takeaway is that the physiological sigh is hardwired in our brainstem pre-Bötzinger complex—it is the fastest real-time physiological brake available to calm the autonomic nervous system.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: how two quick sniffs through the nose and a long slow mouth exhale can drop your heart rate in less than 30 seconds.
2. Alveoli Collapse Under Stress: shallow stress-breathing causing millions of tiny lung sacs (alveoli) to collapse and trap toxic carbon dioxide.
3. The Double Inhale Mechanic: the second quick inhale popping open collapsed alveoli to maximize surface area.
4. Carbon Dioxide Clearance: the long, slow exhale rapidly dumping carbon dioxide, lowering blood acidity.
5. Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia: extended exhales allowing the diaphragm to move up, making the heart larger and signaling the vagus nerve to slow heart rate.
6. The 3-Breath Emergency Rule: performing 2-3 physiological sighs anywhere, anytime during intense stress or panic.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute breathwork neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Feldman, Krasnow, Balban, Huberman). Use "Physiological Sigh," "Pre-Bötzinger Complex," "Alveoli re-inflation," and "Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia." Do not replace emergency medical care for acute respiratory or cardiac failure.

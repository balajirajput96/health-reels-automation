# REEL-0097 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Neurobiology of Loneliness: John Cacioppo's Evolutionary Threat Model, Social Hyper-Vigilance, and Chronic Inflammation"—how subjective loneliness signals biological threat to the brain, shifting the autonomic nervous system into sympathetic overdrive, altering sleep architecture, and elevating systemic inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha). It attributes findings to John Cacioppo, Steve Cole, and social neuroscientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experiencing social isolation or feeling lonely despite being around people. The takeaway is that feeling lonely is an evolutionary biological alarm (like hunger or thirst) signalling a need for connection—it is a physiological state that elevates inflammation and vigilance, but can be reversed through meaningful human contact.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why feeling lonely isn't just an emotional state, but triggers the exact same stress and pain circuits in the brain as physical hunger or danger.
2. John Cacioppo's Threat Model: loneliness as an evolutionary alarm warning the organism that it is outside the protective social group.
3. Hyper-Vigilance State: the brain entering a state of social hyper-vigilance, interpreting ambiguous social cues as hostile or threatening.
4. Autonomic Overdrive & Sleep: sympathetic nervous system activation causing fragmented sleep and elevated baseline cortisol.
5. Inflammatory Cytokine Surge: Steve Cole's research showing loneliness up-regulating pro-inflammatory genes (CTRA) and suppressing antiviral immunity.
6. Re-connecting Biological Signals: realizing loneliness is a biological call to reach out, build trust, and share social support.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute loneliness neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Cacioppo, Cole, Eisenberger, Hawkley). Use "Neurobiology of Loneliness," "social hyper-vigilance," "pro-inflammatory cytokines," and "CTRA gene profile." Do not diagnose clinical depression or anxiety disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

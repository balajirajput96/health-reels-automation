# REEL-0126 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Thermal Contrast Therapy: Morning Cold for Norepinephrine Alertness vs Evening Heat for Parasympathetic Sleep Induction"—how contrasting temperature exposures manipulate the autonomic nervous system: morning cold (10-15°C) triggers a 250% surge in norepinephrine and dopamine for lasting focus, while evening warm baths or saunas (40°C) dilate peripheral blood vessels, dropping core body temperature to trigger rapid deep sleep. It attributes findings to Dr. Peter Srámek and sleep physiologists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone wanting to control their alertness and sleepiness naturally. The takeaway is to use cold to ramp up daytime sympathetic drive and use heat to accelerate nighttime core temperature cooling for restorative sleep.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why taking a cold shower in the morning wakes you up instantly, but a warm bath at night puts you to sleep faster.
2. The Morning Cold Shock: cold water contacting skin thermoreceptors, sending immediate electrical signals to the locus coeruleus.
3. 250% Norepinephrine Surge: Peter Srámek's research showing sustained elevations in alertness hormones without caffeine crashes.
4. The Evening Heat Vasodilation: warm water dilating capillaries in hands and feet, dumping internal heat to the environment.
5. The Core Temperature Drop: falling internal temperature signaling the hypothalamus to release melatonin and initiate Slow-Wave Sleep.
6. Practical Protocol: 2-3 minutes of cool shower after waking; 10-15 minutes of warm bath 90 minutes before bedtime.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute thermal neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Srámek, Haghayegh, Kräuchi, Buijze). Use "Thermal Contrast Therapy," "Locus Coeruleus," "Norepinephrine 250% Surge," and "Core Body Temperature Drop."

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

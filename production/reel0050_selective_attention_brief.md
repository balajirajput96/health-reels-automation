# REEL-0050 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "Selective Attention and the Cocktail Party Effect"—how the auditory and visual attention systems filter out massive sensory noise to focus on a single prioritized stream, while maintaining unconscious pre-attentive monitoring for salient triggers (such as your own name). It attributes findings to Colin Cherry, Donald Broadbent, and Anne Treisman.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults interested in focus, attention science, and noisy environments. The takeaway is that attention is a dynamic biological filter that selectively tunes into target signals while subconsciously scanning for high-salience cues.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: hearing your own name whispered across a loud, crowded party room instantly.
2. The Cocktail Party Phenomenon: Colin Cherry's classic dichotic listening experiments.
3. Attentional Filtering: Broadbent's filter model and Treisman's attenuation model of sensory processing.
4. Top-Down Focus vs Bottom-Up Salience: deliberate goal-directed attention versus automatic stimulus interrupts.
5. Neural Substrates: frontoparietal attention network modulating auditory cortex sensory gain.
6. Practical Focus Habits: minimizing auditory background chatter when engaged in high-precision cognitive work.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive psychology and attention neuroscience.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic acoustic filter diagrams (Multiple soundwaves entering -> Filter attenuates noise -> Single focused wave passes -> Salient name spike triggers alert), lower-third caption-safe area, and no medical claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive science to peer-reviewed selective attention literature (Cherry, Broadbent, Treisman, Posner). Use "selective attention," "cocktail party effect," and "sensory filtering." Do not diagnose ADHD or sensory processing disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

# REEL-0071 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "Theory of Mind and Mental State Attribution: How the Brain Understands What Others Think"—how humans develop the cognitive capacity to impute mental states (beliefs, intents, desires, knowledge, pretending) to themselves and others, mediated by the temporoparietal junction (TPJ), medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), and precuneus. It explains classic developmental paradigms (Sally-Anne False Belief task) and why understanding that others hold different beliefs from reality is a critical milestone of human social cognition. It attributes findings to Simon Baron-Cohen, Uta Frith, and developmental cognitive neuroscience.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is parents, educators, psychologists, managers, and adults. The takeaway is that human empathy and communication depend on "mentalizing"—recognizing that another person acts based on their own internal knowledge and beliefs, which may differ from objective reality.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a 3-year-old thinks everyone knows what is inside their own head, while a 5-year-old understands people can hold false beliefs.
2. The Concept: Theory of Mind (Mentalizing)—the cognitive ability to attribute mental states to others.
3. The Classic False Belief Paradigm: The Sally-Anne experiment (tracking someone else's belief versus objective physical location).
4. The Mentalizing Neural Network: Temporoparietal Junction (TPJ) and Medial Prefrontal Cortex (mPFC) activating during perspective-taking.
5. Real-World Significance: resolving misunderstandings, diplomacy, negotiation, and detecting sarcasm/irony.
6. Egocentric Bias Hazard: our own knowledge often interferes with accurately gauging what another person currently knows.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive developmental psychology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic mentalizing diagrams (Observer -> False Belief representation -> TPJ neural node), lower-third caption-safe area, and no autism diagnostic claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive science to peer-reviewed literature (Baron-Cohen, Frith, Saxe, Premack & Woodruff). Use "Theory of Mind," "mental state attribution," "false belief," and "mentalizing network." Do not diagnose neurodevelopmental disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

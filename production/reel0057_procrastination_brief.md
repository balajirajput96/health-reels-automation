# REEL-0057 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel exploring "Procrastination and Temporal Discounting: The Science of Delaying Difficult Tasks"—how procrastination is fundamentally an emotional regulation failure rather than laziness, driven by hyperbolic discounting (immediate emotional relief versus delayed rational reward) and the conflict between the limbic system (amygdala) and the prefrontal cortex. It attributes findings to Piers Steel, Timothy Pychyl, and behavioral neuroscience.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, remote workers, and creators struggling with procrastination and guilt. The takeaway is that procrastination is an automatic fight-or-flight avoidance of task-induced negative emotions (boredom, fear of failure); lowering task friction to a 2-minute micro-start breaks the avoidance loop.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why you clean your desk or scroll social media the exact moment you need to submit a critical report.
2. The Truth: procrastination is not poor time management or laziness; it is emotional regulation failure.
3. Neural Battle: the emotional limbic system choosing immediate comfort over the prefrontal cortex's delayed goals.
4. Hyperbolic Discounting: why human brains discount the value of future rewards compared to instant dopamine hits.
5. Fear and Perfectionism: how fear of failure and unrealistic standards trigger subconscious task avoidance.
6. The 2-Minute Solution: reducing initial task threshold so low (e.g., write 1 sentence) that the amygdala does not trigger alarm.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is behavioral psychology and neuroscience education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic emotional scale diagrams (Limbic system vs Prefrontal Cortex balance / 2-minute low-threshold gate), lower-third caption-safe area, and no psychiatric ADHD claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral science to peer-reviewed procrastination literature (Steel, Pychyl, Sirois, Ainslie). Use "procrastination," "emotional regulation," "temporal discounting," and "hyperbolic discounting." Do not diagnose chronic mood or executive function disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

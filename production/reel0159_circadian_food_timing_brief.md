# REEL-0159 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Circadian Food Timing & Early Time-Restricted Feeding (eTRF): Liver Peripheral Clock Genes, Nighttime Melatonin Insulin Blockade, and Why Eating at 10 PM Spikes Blood Glucose"—how peripheral clock genes in the liver and pancreas synchronize metabolic efficiency with daylight, and how eating dinner late when melatonin rises inhibits beta-cell insulin secretion, creating severe glycemic spikes, fatty liver, and mitochondrial disruption compared to eating the identical meal at 6 PM. It attributes findings to Dr. Satchin Panda (Salk Institute) and circadian metabolic scientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is late-night snackers, shift workers, and individuals managing insulin resistance or visceral fat. The takeaway is that WHEN you eat is as critical as WHAT you eat; shift your dinner earlier (before 7:30 PM) to align with your body's circadian insulin sensitivity window.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why eating the exact same bowl of rice at 10 PM causes 3 times higher blood sugar spikes than eating it at 6 PM.
2. The Salk Institute Circadian Metabolism Discovery: Dr. Satchin Panda showing peripheral metabolic clocks in the liver and gut tied to solar cycles.
3. The Melatonin-Insulin Receptor Clash: nighttime melatonin secretion binding to MT1/MT2 receptors on pancreatic beta cells, shutting down insulin release.
4. Nocturnal Glycemic Spillover: late carbohydrates lingering in the bloodstream for hours without insulin clearance, accelerating fat storage.
5. Autophagy and Nightly Liver Repair: stopping food intake 3 to 4 hours before sleep allowing the liver to initiate AMPK repair and glycogen clearance.
6. The Early Dinner Habit: close your eating window by 7:30 PM and allow a 12 to 14-hour overnight metabolic rest for your digestive organs.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute circadian metabolic physiology to peer-reviewed literature (Panda, Sutton, Peterson, Scheer). Use "Early Time-Restricted Feeding (eTRF)," "Circadian Metabolism," "Melatonin Insulin Blockade," and "Liver Peripheral Clocks."

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

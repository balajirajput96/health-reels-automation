# REEL-0150 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Zeigarnik Effect 2.0 & Bedtime Cognitive Offloading: Prefrontal Working Memory Loops, Polysomnography, and Why Writing a 5-Minute To-Do List Puts You to Sleep 37% Faster"—how uncompleted daily tasks create persistent open cognitive loops in working memory that trigger nighttime rumination, and how physically writing down a specific to-do list for tomorrow offloads cognitive tension, accelerating sleep onset by 9 to 10 minutes. It attributes findings to Dr. Michael Scullin (Baylor University, Journal of Experimental Psychology 2018).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone suffering from bedtime racing thoughts, work-related insomnia, and bedtime anxiety. The takeaway is to take 5 minutes before bed to write down tomorrow's to-do list on paper; offloading the cognitive loop allows the prefrontal cortex to power down into deep restorative sleep.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why writing down tomorrow's to-do list for 5 minutes makes you fall asleep 37% faster than taking sleeping pills.
2. The Zeigarnik Working Memory Loop: uncompleted tasks remaining stuck in active prefrontal RAM, demanding cognitive resolution.
3. The Baylor University Sleep Study: Dr. Michael Scullin comparing people writing about completed tasks vs writing specific future to-do lists in a sleep lab.
4. The 9-Minute Accelerated Sleep Onset: polysomnography proving subjects writing future to-do lists fell asleep substantially faster.
5. Cognitive Offloading Mechanism: the physical paper convincing the subconscious that the task is secured, terminating the mental alert loop.
6. The 5-Minute Bedtime Offload Ritual: keep a notepad by your bed, write tomorrow's top 3 tasks in specific detail, close the notebook, and turn off the lights.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute sleep psychology and cognitive science to peer-reviewed literature (Scullin, Krueger, Ballard, Zeigarnik). Use "The Zeigarnik Effect," "Cognitive Offloading," "Bedtime To-Do List," and "Baylor Sleep Study."

# REEL-0118 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Yerkes-Dodson Law: The Inverted-U of Stress, Optimal Autonomic Arousal, Choking Under Pressure, and Peak Performance Zones"—how performance on cognitive and motor tasks increases with physiological or mental arousal, but only up to an optimal point; beyond that point, excess sympathetic activation causes tunnel vision, working memory collapse, and choking. It attributes findings to Robert Yerkes, John Dodson, and performance neuroscientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, public speakers, athletes, and test takers who either feel too sluggish or so overly nervous that their brain goes blank. The takeaway is that stress is not the enemy—insufficient stress causes boredom/sluggishness, while excess stress causes panic; learning to modulate your arousal level dials in the sweet spot.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why feeling zero stress makes you careless and slow, but feeling too much stress makes your mind go completely blank during an exam or interview.
2. The Yerkes-Dodson Law (1908): the classic inverted-U curve plotting performance against physiological arousal.
3. The Under-Aroused State: low dopamine and norepinephrine leading to daydreaming, lack of urgency, and sloppy errors.
4. The Optimal Flow Zone: moderate arousal sharpening prefrontal cortex working memory, attention, and reaction speed.
5. The Over-Aroused Crash (Choking): excessive adrenaline flooding the amygdala, shutting down the prefrontal cortex, and creating freeze/panic responses.
6. Arousal Modulation Protocol: if too tired, use fast inhales and brisk movement; if panicking, use extended exhales and physiological sighs to drop back into the sweet spot.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute performance arousal neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Yerkes, Dodson, Diamond, Arnsten). Use "Yerkes-Dodson Law," "Inverted-U Curve," "Prefrontal Collapse," and "Arousal Modulation."

# REEL-0170 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Music Psychology and Mood Awareness: Auditory-Motor Coupling, Striatal Dopamine Release, and Autonomic Modulation"—how intentional music listening is not just passive leisure, but a functional cognitive tool that releases dopamine in the nucleus accumbens during musical anticipation and climax, syncs heart rate variability through auditory-motor entrainment, and provides a mirror for emotional self-awareness. It attributes findings to the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and McGill University neuroscience research (Dr. Valorie Salimpoor & Dr. Robert Zatorre).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is music lovers, students, workers seeking focus, and individuals managing daily stress. The takeaway is that music can be selected intentionally as an emotional regulation tool; listening with conscious awareness helps modulate physiological arousal and process complex feelings.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why hearing your favourite song instantly gives you goosebumps and shifts your emotional state in seconds.
2. McGill University Brain Scans: PET and fMRI imaging showing dopamine surges in the dorsal and ventral striatum.
3. The Anticipation-Chills Climax: the brain predicting harmonic progression and rewarding itself with dopamine at peak moments.
4. Autonomic Entrainment: tempo and rhythm synchronizing breathing rate, galvanic skin response, and heart rate variability.
5. Emotional Processing Mirror: research showing that listening to sad or melancholy music often triggers prolactin release and feelings of comfort.
6. The Intentional Playlist Habit: curate specific soundscapes for deep focus, stress decompression, or energizing movement.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive musicology and neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Salimpoor et al., Zatorre, Juslin & Sloboda, NIH). Use "Music Psychology," "Auditory-Motor Entrainment," "Nucleus Accumbens Dopamine," "Autonomic Modulation," and "Valorie Salimpoor."

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

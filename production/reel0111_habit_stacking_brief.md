# REEL-0111 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Habit Stacking & Synaptic Plasticity: Anchoring New Behaviors to Strong Neural Pathways, Hebb's Law, and Micro-Habits"—how leveraging existing high-frequency neural circuits makes building new habits effortless by piggybacking on automatic basal ganglia routines rather than relying on depletable prefrontal willpower. It attributes findings to Donald Hebb, BJ Fogg, and James Clear.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone struggling to build consistent daily habits (reading, exercising, meditating, hydration). The takeaway is that willpower fails, but synaptic piggybacking succeeds—always stack a new 2-minute habit onto an already automated anchor habit.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why relying on pure willpower to build a new habit almost always fails by day 10, and what neuroscience reveals instead.
2. Basal Ganglia Automation: established daily habits having thick, insulated myelinated neural pathways in the basal ganglia.
3. Donald Hebb's Law: "Neurons that fire together, wire together"—linking two actions physically bonds their synaptic firing patterns.
4. The Habit Stacking Formula: "After [Current Habit], I will immediately do [New Micro-Habit]."
5. Lowering Activation Energy: keeping the new stacked action under 2 minutes to bypass prefrontal resistance.
6. Real-World Stacking Examples: doing 10 squats after brushing teeth; drinking a glass of water after waking up; doing 1 minute of breathwork after opening laptop.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute habit neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Hebb, Fogg, Clear, Wood). Use "Habit Stacking," "Hebb's Law," "Synaptic Plasticity," and "Basal Ganglia."

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

# REEL-0108 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "The Neurobiology of Music: Nucleus Accumbens Dopamine Chills, Mirror Neurons, and Whole-Brain Synchronization"—how musical intervals, anticipation, and rhythm synchronize neural oscillations across the auditory cortex, motor cortex, and reward pathways, triggering goosebumps (frisson) and reducing cortisol. It attributes findings to Robert Zatorre, Daniel Levitin, and music neuroscientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone who listens to music for focus, workout energy, relaxation, or emotional release. The takeaway is that music is an ancient neurobiological technology that activates nearly every known brain region simultaneously, triggering dopamine surges during melodic anticipation and resolution.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why your favorite song can give you literal goosebumps (frisson) and instantly lift a bad mood.
2. Auditory Prediction & Dopamine: the brain constantly predicting the next musical note; the moment of expected or unexpected resolution releasing dopamine in the nucleus accumbens.
3. Whole-Brain Activation: fMRI showing music lighting up the auditory cortex, motor cortex (tapping feet), and limbic system simultaneously.
4. Rhythm Entrainment: brainwaves naturally synchronizing (entraining) to acoustic tempos (e.g. 60 BPM inducing alpha wave relaxation).
5. Mirror Neurons & Empathy: sad or triumphant music activating mirror neuron networks, allowing deep emotional catharsis without real-life threat.
6. Functional Music Rules: using binaural/ambient 60-80 BPM for deep study, and high-tempo 120-140 BPM for dopamine-driven workouts.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute music neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Zatorre, Levitin, Blood, Salimpoor). Use "Music Neurobiology," "Nucleus Accumbens Dopamine," "Musical Frisson," and "Rhythm Entrainment."

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

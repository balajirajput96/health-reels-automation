# REEL-0024 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining the "spacing effect" in cognitive psychology—how distributing study and practice sessions over time significantly enhances long-term retention and memory consolidation compared to massed cramming. It attributes findings to classic and modern cognitive learning research.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, professionals, and lifelong learners seeking effective, science-backed learning strategies. The takeaway is that spaced repetition creates desirable difficulty that strengthens memory consolidation.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: cramming the night before vs spaced intervals.
2. Evidence: cognitive psychology research on distributed practice vs massed repetition.
3. Mechanism: spacing allowing memory consolidation and reactivation intervals.
4. Desirable difficulty: effortful retrieval after a delay strengthening neural pathways.
5. Practice: dividing study sessions into short, spaced chunks across days.
6. Nuance: acknowledging that different subjects and tasks require tailored intervals.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is learning science, not clinical cognitive therapy.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic calendar and timeline charts comparing massed cramming blocks vs spaced intervals, lower-third caption-safe area, and no academic guarantees.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute learning science to cognitive psychology and Ebbinghaus memory consolidation research. Use "may enhance" and "evidence-based strategy." Do not claim spaced practice cures learning disabilities.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

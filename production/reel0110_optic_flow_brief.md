# REEL-0110 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Visual Optic Flow & Anxiety Reduction: Lateral Eye Movements, Forward Ambulation, and Silencing Amygdala Threat Circuits"—how walking outside generates self-generated visual motion (optic flow) across the retinas, which triggers direct inhibitory signaling to the amygdala and fear centers, mechanically lowering anxiety and mental agitation. It attributes findings to Andrew Huberman, Francine Shapiro (EMDR), and visual neuroscientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is anyone dealing with racing thoughts, high stress, screen fatigue, or anxiety. The takeaway is that walking forward outside is not just light physical exercise—the lateral movement of visual scenes past your eyes (optic flow) is an evolutionary neural brake that calms amygdala reactivity.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why going for a 15-minute outdoor walk clears your head faster than sitting in a room trying to talk yourself out of anxiety.
2. The Mechanism of Optic Flow: moving forward causing visual objects to sweep across your retinas laterally from center to periphery.
3. Silencing Amygdala Threat Detection: visual neuroscience showing self-generated lateral eye movement directly dampening threat processing in the amygdala.
4. Panoramic Vision vs Tunnel Vision: stress contracting vision into focal tunnel mode; optic flow shifting retinas into wide panoramic mode that triggers parasympathetic calm.
5. EMDR Clinical Connection: Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) using lateral eye sweeps to process traumatic emotional memories.
6. The Outdoor Walk Protocol: walking outside without looking down at a smartphone for 15-20 minutes when feeling overwhelmed.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute optic flow neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Huberman, Shapiro, de Voogd, Hermans). Use "Visual Optic Flow," "Panoramic Vision," "Amygdala Suppression," and "Forward Ambulation."

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

# REEL-0095 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Cognitive Flexibility: Lateral Prefrontal Cortex, Task Set-Shifting, and Adapting to Unexpected Change"—how the brain dynamically switches cognitive sets, updates strategies when rules change, and reframes unexpected setbacks as learning opportunities. It attributes findings to David Badre, Adele Diamond, and cognitive flexibility neuroscientists.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is people facing unexpected life changes, job pivots, or strategic obstacles. The takeaway is that mental rigidity causes suffering and errors when conditions change; cognitive flexibility can be trained by deliberately exposing oneself to new perspectives and testing alternative solutions.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why some people crumble when plans change, while others adapt and thrive instantly.
2. What is Cognitive Flexibility: one of the three core executive functions (alongside inhibition and working memory) located in the lateral prefrontal cortex.
3. The Wisconsin Card Sorting Principle: neuroscientists testing mental flexibility by changing rules unannounced and measuring how fast subjects adapt.
4. Rigid Persistence vs Adaptive Shifting: rigid brains repeating failing strategies (perseveration error) while flexible brains rapidly update hypotheses.
5. Error Processing as Learning Signal: viewing a surprise outcome not as a threat but as a prediction error signal to update mental models.
6. Training Flexibility: switching routines, taking diverse perspectives, and asking "What else could this mean?" when blocked.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute cognitive flexibility neuroscience to peer-reviewed literature (Badre, Diamond, Grant, Miyake). Use "Cognitive Flexibility," "Lateral Prefrontal Cortex," "task set-shifting," and "error prediction signal." Do not diagnose executive dysfunction disorders.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

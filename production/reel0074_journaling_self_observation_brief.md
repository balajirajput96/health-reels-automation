# REEL-0074 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel presenting journaling as a possible self-observation space rather than a diagnostic test or substitute for therapy. It distinguishes ordinary journaling from structured expressive-writing research and avoids trauma-writing instructions.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults curious about journaling for reflection. The takeaway is a low-pressure prompt—what happened, what I noticed, and what support or next step I need—while remembering that effects vary and difficult writing can sometimes increase distress.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals animated with deterministic motion if official visual quotas permit. Native video generation is optional and may be used only if its official quota is available. If image generation remains exhausted, do not use unrelated visuals; record a blocked preproduction state.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: an adult opens a plain notebook in soft daylight, creating a quiet reflection space; no readable generated text.
2. Research context: a neutral desk with journal pages and a research article silhouette, without fake citations or text-bearing AI graphics.
3. Possible value: the person organizes three simple visual cards or page sections, without claiming a clinical outcome.
4. No guarantee: varied everyday scenes showing that people respond differently; no before/after transformation.
5. Gentle prompt: blank notebook page with three visual areas for event, observation, and next step; captions provide the exact Hindi words.
6. Not self-diagnosis: the person closes a checklist-like page and returns to ordinary life, avoiding diagnostic labels or test scores.
7. Safety: the person pauses writing and reaches toward a qualified-support context when distress is suggested, without crisis imagery.

## Visual constraints

Use a consistent realistic editorial documentary style with warm natural light, inclusive adult representation, neutral clothing, uncluttered backgrounds, lower-third caption-safe space, and no logos, fake research screenshots, diagnostic forms, symptom checklists, readable AI-generated notebook text, or crisis imagery. Avoid romanticizing distress or portraying writing as a cure.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, use readable multiline blocks, remain bottom-centered, and avoid adding writing durations, trauma instructions, diagnostic labels, or therapeutic claims absent from the approved script.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute the possible benefits and mixed-results caveat to Harvard Health and APA summaries. Distinguish ordinary journaling from structured expressive writing. Do not claim that journaling heals trauma, guarantees stress reduction, diagnoses mental illness, or replaces therapy. If writing increases distress or stress is severe/persistent, direct viewers toward qualified professional support.

## Disclosure

If produced, state that visuals are AI-generated reference images animated with deterministic motion. Record any official quota limitation. The package remains unpublished until separate editorial review.

# REEL-0031 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining how behavioral science studies measure behavior change—separating subjective intentions and self-reports from objective behavioral tracking, and distinguishing initial efficacy from long-term real-world maintenance.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults interested in habit formation and evidence-based self-improvement. The takeaway is that stating an intention is distinct from sustained action, and scientific studies track objective adherence across longitudinal follow-ups.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why stating "I will change" is very different from measured long-term action.
2. Intention vs Action: distinguishing self-reported plans from objective behavioral data.
3. Measurement methods: using sensor/device records, attendance logs, and task performance.
4. Efficacy vs Effectiveness: controlled lab trials vs messy real-world contexts.
5. Longitudinal maintenance: tracking adherence decline and barrier shifts over months.
6. Guardrails: why group study averages cannot serve as personal guarantees.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is behavioral science education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, minimalist comparison cards (intention checklist vs objective activity log), lower-third caption-safe area, and no self-help hyperbole.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral science to peer-reviewed intervention and adherence studies. Use "measured outcome" and "longitudinal maintenance." Do not promise effortless habit transformation.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

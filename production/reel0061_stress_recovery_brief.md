# REEL-0061 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "Autonomic Stress Recovery and Parasympathetic Restoration"—how the autonomic nervous system downregulates after acute stress challenges via vagal reactivation, heart rate variability (HRV) recovery, and cortisol clearance, and why recovery is a dynamic temporal trajectory rather than a static single score. It attributes findings to psychophysiology and autonomic regulation literature.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experiencing lingering stress and seeking physiological recovery mechanisms. The takeaway is that post-stress recovery is measured by the slope of parasympathetic reactivation (HRV rebound and vagal tone); slow diaphragmatic breathing and physiological sighs actively engage this brake.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why two people facing the same stressful event recover at vastly different physiological speeds.
2. The Concept: Autonomic Recovery—the return of heart rate, cortisol, and autonomic balance to baseline.
3. The Vagal Brake: how the parasympathetic nervous system (Vagus Nerve) acts like a brake to slow cardiac pacing.
4. Heart Rate Variability (HRV): high HRV indicating flexible autonomic adaptability and robust recovery capacity.
5. Temporal Windows: distinguishing acute laboratory recovery (minutes) from chronic daily allostatic recovery (days/sleep).
6. Activating Recovery: using physiological sighs (double inhale, long exhale) to trigger immediate vagal acetylcholine release.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is psychophysiology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic autonomic recovery curves (Baseline -> Stress Peak -> Recovery Slope / HRV waveform), lower-third caption-safe area, and no medical burnout diagnoses.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute psychophysiology to peer-reviewed stress recovery literature (Porges, Thayer & Lane, McEwen). Use "stress recovery," "parasympathetic reactivation," "heart rate variability," and "vagal brake." Do not diagnose dysautonomia or chronic fatigue.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

# REEL-0044 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "The Science of Feedback in Learning"—contrasting task-focused formative correction (specific, actionable feedback on the process) with person-focused praise or criticism (ego-threatening feedback), and analyzing how feedback timing modulates memory updating. It attributes findings to Kluger & DeNisi (Feedback Intervention Theory) and John Hattie.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, teachers, managers, and parents. The takeaway is that effective feedback focuses precisely on task details and next action steps rather than personal judgment or vague praise.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why saying "You are smart!" often harms future performance compared to "Here is how to fix this step."
2. Feedback Intervention Theory: research showing over 38% of feedback interventions actually decrease performance.
3. Task-Focused vs Ego-Focused: differentiating feedback on the task/strategy from feedback targeting self-worth.
4. Corrective Action: providing immediate information on what was wrong and the exact step to correct it.
5. Timing & Consolidation: allowing an initial attempt before offering corrective guidance to solidify memory updating.
6. Practical Application: giving specific, descriptive feedback ("Your structure is clear, work on paragraph 2 transition").
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is learning science and organizational psychology.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic comparisons (Task-level feedback arrow vs Ego-level evaluation block), lower-third caption-safe area, and no management hype.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute learning science to meta-analyses on feedback interventions (Kluger & DeNisi, Hattie & Timperley). Use "task-level feedback" and "formative correction." Do not promise flawless organizational outcomes.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

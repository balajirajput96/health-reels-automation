# REEL-0040 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "The Interleaving Effect" in learning science—how mixing different topics, problem types, or skills during study sessions (interleaving) dramatically improves long-term discrimination and problem-solving compared to blocked practice (practicing one type over and over). It attributes findings to cognitive psychology and education research (Rohrer & Taylor, Bjork et al.).

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, educators, and lifelong learners seeking evidence-informed study techniques. The takeaway is that interleaved practice creates "desirable difficulty" that forces the brain to actively discriminate and select appropriate problem-solving strategies.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: practicing 20 math problems of the same formula vs mixing three different problem types.
2. Blocked vs Interleaved: explaining AAABBBCCC vs ABCABCABC study schedules.
3. Evidence: cognitive learning research on categorization, retrieval, and transfer test performance.
4. Mechanism: forcing the brain to choose the right formula/strategy rather than passively applying the same one.
5. Perceptual illusion: why blocked practice feels easier in the moment but produces worse long-term retention.
6. Practical implementation: shuffling flashcards or mixing topic problem sets across study sessions.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is cognitive learning science.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic comparison timelines (blocked color blocks vs interleaved mixed color sequences), lower-third caption-safe area, and no academic guarantees.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute learning science to peer-reviewed cognitive psychology and interleaved practice trials. Use "may enhance discrimination" and "desirable difficulty." Do not guarantee exam scores.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

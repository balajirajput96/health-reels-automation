# REEL-0079 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education vertical reel that reframes habit tracking as optional reflection rather than a perfection test. The visual story moves from an anxious, overly rigid streak view to a calm, flexible pattern-observation view without showing medical dashboards or implying guaranteed outcomes.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults experimenting with everyday routines who may feel discouraged by missed checkmarks. The takeaway is: “Tracker को अपने pattern को समझने के लिए इस्तेमाल करें, खुद को judge करने के लिए नहीं।”

## Visual direction

Use topic-specific 9:16 visuals only: a phone or paper tracker with neutral, non-branded marks; a calendar with varied days rather than an uninterrupted streak; a person calmly reviewing a simple routine record; contextual inserts showing schedule changes, energy variation, and an optional reset. Keep the visual language warm, grounded, and documentary-like, with soft daylight, muted teal and amber accents, clear empty space for captions, and no logos or recognizable apps.

The opening image should contrast a rigid wall of perfect checkmarks with a more realistic record containing gaps and notes. The middle sequence should show a tracker being used to notice patterns rather than grade identity. The final sequence should show the tracker being put aside when it increases stress, reinforcing autonomy and accessibility.

## Suggested seven-shot structure

| Span | Visual purpose | Caption emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Hook: tracker feels like an exam | “मदद या परीक्षा?” |
| 02 | Repetition in a familiar context | “Repetition + context” |
| 03 | Record versus report card | “Tracker आपकी value नहीं” |
| 04 | A missed day with contextual notes | “Missed day = information” |
| 05 | Different responses to setbacks | “Association, guarantee नहीं” |
| 06 | Flexible tracking choice | “Pattern समझें, judge नहीं” |
| 07 | Optionality and safety | “Stress बढ़े तो तरीका बदलें” |

## Caption and accessibility rules

Captions must be real multiline SRT blocks rendered with Noto Sans Devanagari, bottom-centered, high contrast, and readable on a 9:16 frame. Keep each caption concise and synchronized to the narration span. Avoid tiny text, dense paragraphs, medical symbols, branded interfaces, and visual streak-count numbers that could be misread as evidence.

## Claim and safety boundaries

The reel may state that repetition in context is relevant to habit formation and that self-tracking experiences differ. It must not state that perfect streaks are necessary, that one missed day has no effect for everyone, that tracking automatically creates habits, or that self-compassion guarantees health outcomes. State that tracking is optional and that distressing, compulsive, unsafe, or inaccessible tracking should be changed or stopped. Include “general education, not medical advice.”

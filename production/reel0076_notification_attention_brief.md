# REEL-0076 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining that smartphone-notification cues can compete for attention in a controlled task, while evidence from a real-world notification-disabling intervention is mixed. The practical takeaway is a reversible, user-controlled settings experiment rather than a blanket digital detox.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults trying to protect attention during selected tasks. The takeaway is to review non-essential alerts, batch them, or test a focus mode in one context while preserving safety-critical, caregiving, work-critical, and accessibility-related alerts.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals animated with deterministic motion if official visual quotas permit. Native video generation is optional and may be used only if its official quota is available. If image generation remains exhausted, do not use unrelated visuals; record a blocked preproduction state.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: an adult focused on a notebook or laptop notices a phone banner or vibration cue at the edge of the scene; no exaggerated startle.
2. Lab finding: neutral cognitive-task setup with a phone notification sound represented visually, without fake EEG or study graphics.
3. Study boundary: transition from a lab-like task to ordinary life, emphasizing that the lab is not a perfect copy of daily life.
4. Intervention evidence: phone settings screen shown from behind or with unreadable UI, paired with neutral everyday scenes; no fabricated numbers or app logos.
5. Mixed effects: one person feels more intentional while another checks for missed updates; avoid portraying fear of missing out as a diagnosis.
6. Reversible experiment: close, topic-specific visuals of turning off selected non-essential alerts, batching them, and enabling a focus mode while keeping a highlighted safety-critical channel active; do not render readable fake text.
7. Safety: inclusive user reviewing accessibility and caregiving needs before choosing settings; no universal “silence everything” message.

## Visual constraints

Use a consistent realistic editorial documentary style with soft natural light, inclusive adult representation, visible phone-and-task context, lower-third caption-safe space, and no logos, fake app interfaces, readable AI-generated notification text, clinical brain imagery, productivity guarantees, or digital-detox symbolism. The phone should be a tool, not a villain.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, use readable multiline blocks, remain bottom-centered, and avoid adding screen-time numbers, deep-work promises, clinical labels, or a blanket instruction to disable all alerts.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute controlled-task findings to Upshaw et al. and the mixed randomized-intervention findings to Dekker et al. Use “may,” “controlled study,” and “mixed results.” Preserve alerts needed for safety, care, work, communication, and accessibility. The reel is general digital-habit education, not medical or psychological treatment.

## Disclosure

If produced, state that visuals are AI-generated reference images animated with deterministic motion. Record any official quota limitation. The package remains unpublished until separate editorial review.

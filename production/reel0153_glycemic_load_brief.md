# REEL-0153 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel on "Glycemic Index (GI) vs Glycemic Load (GL): Carbohydrate Density, Water Content, and Why Watermelon and Carrots Do Not Spike Blood Sugar"—how Glycemic Index only measures the speed of glucose entry based on 50g of pure carbs, whereas Glycemic Load factors in actual portion size and water density, explaining why high-GI foods like watermelon (GI 72, but GL only 5) are completely harmless to insulin levels in normal servings. It attributes findings to Dr. David Jenkins (University of Toronto) and Harvard School of Public Health.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is diabetics, fitness enthusiasts, and anyone confused about carbohydrate nutrition. The takeaway is to focus on Glycemic Load rather than demonizing fruits based on raw Glycemic Index.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why avoiding watermelon or carrots because of 'high sugar' is a massive nutritional myth.
2. The Glycemic Index (GI) Limitation: Dr. David Jenkins creating GI to measure how fast 50g of pure extracted carbohydrates turn into blood sugar.
3. The Watermelon Paradox: watermelon having a high GI of 72, but because it is 92% water, you would need to eat a massive 1 kilogram to get 50g of carbs.
4. The Glycemic Load (GL) Equation: GL = (GI × Carbs per serving) ÷ 100.
5. The Real-World Number: a standard slice of watermelon has a Glycemic Load of only 5 (below 10 is low and safe), causing almost zero insulin spike.
6. The True Culprits: processed white flour, soda, and refined snacks with high GL (>20) packed with dense dry starch.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute nutritional biochemistry to peer-reviewed literature (Jenkins, Willett, Brand-Miller, Ludwig). Use "Glycemic Load vs Glycemic Index," "Carbohydrate Density," "Insulin Impact," and "Harvard Health."

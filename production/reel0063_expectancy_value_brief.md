# REEL-0063 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "The Expectancy-Value Theory of Motivation"—how human persistence, choice, and achievement drive depend multiplicatively on two psychological pillars: Expectancy of Success (can I do it?) and Subjective Task Value (why does it matter to me?). It attributes findings to Jacquelynne Eccles, Allan Wigfield, and John William Atkinson.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is students, educators, career changers, and parents wanting to understand why individuals give up on goals. The takeaway is that motivation is a mathematical product: if either confidence/expectancy is zero OR perceived personal value is zero, total motivation collapses to zero. Building both self-efficacy and personal relevance restores unstoppable drive.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why you can find an activity extremely important (like exercising) but still not do it if you doubt your ability to succeed.
2. The Theory: Eccles & Wigfield's Expectancy-Value Model—Motivation = Expectancy × Value.
3. Component 1: Expectancy of Success—beliefs about personal competence and probability of reaching the outcome.
4. Component 2: Subjective Task Value—Interest value (enjoyment), Attainment value (identity), Utility value (usefulness), and Cost.
5. The Multiplicative Trap: why $10 \times 0 = 0$—high value with zero confidence produces anxiety, while high confidence with zero value produces apathy.
6. Practical Calibration: boosting expectancy through scaffolded wins and boosting value by linking tasks to core identity.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is educational psychology and motivational science education.

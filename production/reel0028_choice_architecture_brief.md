# REEL-0028 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel explaining "Choice Architecture" and default options from behavioral economics—how the presentation and sequencing of options in an environment nudges choices without forbidding any alternatives.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is adults interested in decision-making, workplace design, and habit formation. The takeaway is that default settings strongly determine choices because opting out requires active cognitive effort.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why most people stick with the default option without changing settings.
2. Evidence: behavioral economics research on choice architecture and defaults (Thaler & Sunstein).
3. Mechanism: status quo bias and cognitive friction associated with opting out.
4. Real-world example: organ donation opt-in vs opt-out rates or retirement savings defaults.
5. Personal design: setting personal healthy defaults in home and digital environments.
6. Ethical boundary: ensuring choice architecture preserves freedom of choice.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is behavioral science education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic decision toggle diagrams and opt-in/opt-out visual cards, lower-third caption-safe area, and no manipulative claims.

## Caption policy

Captions must reproduce the approved Hindi narration, remain bottom-centered, use readable multiline blocks, and avoid rigid rules.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute behavioral economics to Nobel-prize winning nudge research. Use "default architecture" and "behavioral friction." Do not present nudge theory as mandatory coercion.

## Disclosure

State that content is for general educational awareness. The package remains subject to standard repository verification.

# REEL-0073 Production Brief

## Concept

A Hindi general-education reel examining "Intrinsic Motivation vs The Overjustification Effect: Why Paying People for Hobbies Can Kill Their Passion"—how extrinsic rewards (money, gold stars, trophies) offered for activities already intrinsically enjoyable can shift the perceived locus of causality from internal joy to external compliance, fundamentally undermining long-term autonomous engagement. It attributes findings to Edward Deci, Mark Lepper, David Greene, and motivation science.

## Audience and takeaway

The audience is parents, educators, creators, startup founders, and workers. The takeaway is that extrinsic rewards should be informational (recognizing competence) rather than controlling (bribes to perform); protecting intrinsic curiosity and autonomy preserves lifelong mastery.

## Format

Portrait 9:16 video, approximately one minute, with Hindi off-screen narration, synchronized Hindi captions, and topic-specific reference visuals.

## Seven-span visual plan

1. Hook: why a child who loves drawing for hours suddenly stops drawing once you start paying them 50 rupees per picture.
2. The Overjustification Effect: Mark Lepper's classic experiment with preschoolers and drawing rewards.
3. The Internal Shift: shifting from "I do this because I love it" to "I only do this for the reward."
4. Edward Deci's Soma Puzzle Study: participants paid per puzzle solution stopped playing during free-choice time, unlike unpaid peers.
5. Extrinsic vs Intrinsic Dopamine Dynamics: external bribes hijack the brain's reward prediction errors, creating transactional dependency.
6. How to Reward Correctly: using unexpected praise for effort and competence rather than conditional transactional bribes.
7. Safety boundary: standard educational disclosure stating content is motivation and cognitive psychology education.

## Visual constraints

Use clean, graphic Overjustification effect curves (Intrinsic baseline -> Extrinsic bribe spike -> Post-reward collapse below baseline), lower-third caption-safe area, and no business consulting guarantees.

## Evidence and safety gates

Attribute motivation psychology to peer-reviewed literature (Lepper & Greene, Deci & Ryan, Henderlong & Lepper). Use "intrinsic motivation," "overjustification effect," "free-choice paradigm," and "informational feedback." Do not promise financial or career outcomes.

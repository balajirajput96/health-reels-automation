# Batch 07 — Regular Physical Activity and Everyday Stress: Source Validation

**Topic:** Regular physical activity and everyday stress-management context.

**Audience and boundary:** General-public Hinglish education. This Reel describes established public-health and research context around physical activity, emotional balance, and psychological distress. It does not diagnose stress, depression, or anxiety; prescribe an exercise plan; set a universal activity amount; promise relief; or replace professional care.

## Evidence Reviewed

| Source | Date | Supported educational point | Allowed use in Reel |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [CDC — *Physical Activity Boosts Brain Health*](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/features/boost-brain-health.html) | 2025-08-13 | CDC states that regular physical activity can support thinking, learning, problem-solving, and emotional balance. It also states that short bursts can support memory and thinking skills. | Explain that physical activity belongs in a broader brain-health and wellbeing context. Avoid causal claims about personal stress resilience. |
| [Singh et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine* — umbrella review of physical activity interventions](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/18/1203) | 2023-09-19 | The review synthesized 97 reviews and 1,039 trials in adults. It found physical-activity interventions were associated with improvements in depression, anxiety, and psychological-distress outcomes; review quality and context varied. | Mention that a large evidence synthesis studied psychological-distress outcomes, but do not turn results into diagnosis/treatment advice or compare physical activity with clinical care. |
| [Mayo Clinic — *Exercise and stress: Get moving to manage stress*](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/stress-management/in-depth/exercise-and-stress/art-20044469) | 2025-09-20 | Mayo Clinic describes physical activity as one stress-management approach and notes people with health concerns should consult a healthcare professional before a new plan. | State that activity can be one supportive routine among many; include a professional-consultation boundary for personal health concerns. |

## Approved Claim Boundary

> Regular physical activity is one part of a broader health routine. Public-health guidance and research discuss links with brain health, emotional balance, and psychological-distress outcomes. Individual experiences, health needs, and what feels manageable differ, so this Reel does not promise a stress or mood result.

## Do Not Claim

- Do not say physical activity cures, treats, prevents, or replaces care for anxiety, depression, burnout, trauma, or another condition.
- Do not promise stress relief, emotional resilience, improved mood, or a productivity outcome.
- Do not prescribe a universal workout type, duration, intensity, or target.
- Do not claim that exercise chemicals, hormones, or mechanisms explain every response.
- Do not depict a person in distress becoming “fixed” through activity.

## Required Disclaimer

> General education only; it is not medical advice. For personal health concerns, speak with a qualified professional.

No expert-video claim is used. The institutional and peer-reviewed sources above meet the source-quality gate.

# Batch 08 — Social Connection and Everyday Wellbeing: Source Validation

**Topic:** Social connection and everyday wellbeing.

**Audience and boundary:** General-public Hinglish education. This Reel defines social connection in a public-health sense and explains that supportive relationships are associated with health and wellbeing. It does not diagnose loneliness, social anxiety, depression, or relationship problems; guarantee that contact will improve wellbeing; or prescribe a social intervention.

## Evidence Reviewed

| Source | Date | Supported educational point | Allowed use in Reel |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [CDC — *Social Connection*](https://www.cdc.gov/social-connectedness/about/index.html) | 2024-05-15 | CDC defines social connection through the quantity, quality, and variety of relationships someone wants; it describes belonging, support, and care as relevant dimensions. | Explain that social connection is not merely the number of contacts; quality, support, and belonging also matter. |
| [WHO — *Social connection linked to improved health and reduced risk of early death*](https://www.who.int/news/item/30-06-2025-social-connection-linked-to-improved-heath-and-reduced-risk-of-early-death) | 2025-06-30 | WHO reports that social connection is a public-health issue and that loneliness and social isolation have multiple structural and personal causes. | State that connection has broader community and health relevance, while avoiding mortality statistics and individualized risk claims. |
| [Wickramaratne et al., *PLOS One* — *Social connectedness as a determinant of mental health: A scoping review*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9560615/) | 2022-10-13 | The scoping review examined 66 longitudinal studies and reported that most relevant studies found social support and connectedness were associated with later depression/anxiety outcomes, with variability and possible reciprocal effects. | Explain that longitudinal research often finds associations, not a simple guaranteed causal effect for every person. |

## Approved Claim Boundary

> Social connection includes the quality, variety, and support within relationships—not only how many people someone knows. Public-health sources and longitudinal research link supportive connection with health and wellbeing, but every person’s circumstances and relationships differ.

## Do Not Claim

- Do not say a message, call, group, friendship, or relationship cures loneliness, depression, anxiety, or another condition.
- Do not promise that social contact will improve mood, health, or life expectancy.
- Do not imply that people who are isolated are personally at fault.
- Do not prescribe a universal amount, type, or frequency of contact.
- Do not use loneliness prevalence or mortality figures as an alarmist hook.

## Required Disclaimer

> General education only; it is not medical advice. For personal health concerns, speak with a qualified professional.

## Expert-Video Status

No expert-video claim is used. The institutional and peer-reviewed sources above meet the source-quality gate.

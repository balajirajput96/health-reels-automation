## Verified findings and safe framing

1. The default mode network (DMN) is a set of interacting brain regions associated with internally oriented cognition. University of Stanford's neuroscience explainer describes it as active when attention turns inward and connected to memory, self-related thought, plans, and daydreaming. This is an explanatory synthesis, not proof that one network alone causes every instance of mind-wandering.

2. Fox, Spreng, Ellamil, Andrews-Hanna, and Christoff (2015) performed a quantitative meta-analysis of neuroimaging studies of spontaneous thought and mind-wandering. The reel may safely say that converging neuroimaging research links mind-wandering-related thought to distributed brain networks, including DMN-related regions, but should not depict a single brain area as an on/off mind-wandering switch.

3. A mechanistic review of lesion, tDCS, and intracranial EEG evidence argues that these methods can inform causal relevance and neurophysiology, but evidence is still about interacting systems, task context, and subjective reports. The reel should label the proposed mechanism as a hypothesis/model, not settled fact.

4. The practical visual model for this reel is: external task demand and internally generated thought compete and coordinate through multiple interacting networks. This is a simplified teaching model. Avoid claims that DMN activity is always bad, that it literally creates thoughts by itself, or that a single scan can read a person's thoughts.

## Claim discipline

- Say "एक प्रस्तावित मॉडल" or "शोध में जुड़े हुए नेटवर्क" rather than "DMN mind-wandering कराता है".
- Do not diagnose or imply psychiatric conditions from network activity.
- Do not show fabricated neural recordings or numerical brain maps.
- Distinguish a university expert explanation from a systematic review or meta-analysis.

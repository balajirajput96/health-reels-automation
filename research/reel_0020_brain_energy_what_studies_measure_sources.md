# Reel 0020 — मस्तिष्क ऊर्जा: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Verified source record

### [1] Magistretti & Pellerin (1999), *Cellular mechanisms of brain energy metabolism and their relevance to functional brain imaging*

- DOI: `10.1098/rstb.1999.0471`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1999.0471
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1692634/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed mechanistic review.
- Relevant boundary: PET can measure signals related to cerebral blood flow, glucose use, and oxygen consumption depending on tracer; fMRI BOLD is related to blood oxygenation and vascular/metabolic processes; MRS can track selected metabolic intermediates. These are not interchangeable measurements of “energy” or direct recordings of thought.

### [2] Magistretti & Allaman (2015), *A Cellular Perspective on Brain Energy Metabolism and Functional Imaging*

- DOI: `10.1016/j.neuron.2015.03.035`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.03.035
- Full text: https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(15)00259-7
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed cellular and functional-imaging review.
- Relevant boundary: PET tracers can reveal glucose utilization, blood flow, or oxygen consumption; BOLD-fMRI is a hemodynamic surrogate related to oxy/deoxyhemoglobin changes; MRS can study metabolic fluxes; cellular-resolution studies and purified-cell experiments help interpret how neurons and astrocytes contribute to whole-brain signals. A cellular metabolic model is not a direct measurement of a person’s cognitive energy budget.

### [3] Jamadar et al. (2020), *Simultaneous BOLD-fMRI and constant infusion FDG-PET data of the resting human brain*

- DOI: `10.1038/s41597-020-00699-5`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-00699-5
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7578808/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed human multimodal dataset and methods paper.
- Relevant boundary: simultaneous FDG-PET/fMRI captures glucose uptake and hemodynamic BOLD signals from the same resting scan. The paper describes BOLD as a hemodynamic-based surrogate with non-neuronal vascular components and notes that functional, metabolic, and static covariance/connectivity calculations are distinct. New hybrid methods have processing, reliability, and interpretation limitations.

### [4] Shan et al. (2022), *Integrated Positron Emission Tomography/Magnetic Resonance Imaging for Resting-State Functional and Metabolic Imaging in Human Brain: What Is Correlated and What Is Impacted*

- DOI: `10.3389/fnins.2022.824152`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.824152
- Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.824152/full
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed human PET/MRI study.
- Relevant boundary: FDG-PET produces measures such as standardized uptake values and regional cerebral metabolic rate of glucose; fMRI produces metrics such as low-frequency amplitude, regional homogeneity, and degree centrality. Correlation between PET and fMRI metrics does not mean the two modalities measure the same thing or prove causation. Scan timing and MRI pulse sequences can affect quantitative interpretation.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement layer | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glucose use | FDG-PET uptake, SUV, or regional metabolic rate of glucose | Regional tracer distribution or glucose-utilization estimate under a protocol | A direct readout of “how hard you are thinking” or a universal energy score |
| Blood flow | Oxygen-15 water PET, arterial-spin labeling, or related methods | Regional perfusion changes | A cell-specific or thought-specific measurement |
| Oxygen metabolism | Oxygen-15 PET or models combining flow and oxygen-related signals | Oxygen consumption estimates under defined assumptions | A direct measure of ATP production in every cell |
| Hemodynamics | BOLD-fMRI changes in oxy/deoxyhemoglobin-related signal | A blood-oxygenation and vascular surrogate associated with neural activity | Direct neuronal firing, exact energy use, or a diagnosis |
| Metabolic intermediates / flux | MRS, stable-isotope tracing, microdialysis, or nanosensors | Selected metabolites or fluxes such as glucose, lactate, or related pathways | A complete whole-brain energy budget from one signal |
| Cellular metabolism | Purified cells, cultures, slices, animal models, or cell-specific assays | Cell-specific pathways and responses under controlled conditions | Automatic equivalence to the intact living human brain |
| Multimodal association | PET–fMRI correlation or metabolic connectivity | Statistical relationship between distinct modalities | That the signals are identical or causally linked |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel uses “brain energy” as a methodological topic, not as a self-help score. Brain-energy studies can estimate or observe different things: tracer uptake, blood flow, oxygen consumption, hemodynamic contrast, metabolites, or cell-specific fluxes. The script does not claim that one scan reveals a person’s intelligence, mental effort, fatigue, diagnosis, or future performance. Group-level and model-derived estimates are kept separate from individual clinical inference.

# R0072 Evidence Record: Intrinsic Motivation — What Studies Measure

## Research question and unique angle

This reel asks what researchers actually record when they study intrinsic motivation. The angle is to distinguish enjoyment and interest reported for a particular activity from free-choice behavior, persistence, task performance, reward manipulations, and neural correlates.

## Evidence table

| Measurement class | Example operationalization | Direct outcome | Boundary on interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subjective experience | Interest/enjoyment, curiosity, fun, reasons for engagement | Activity-specific self-report | A report about a defined activity and time, not a universal trait |
| Related psychological dimensions | Perceived choice, competence, effort, value, pressure/tension, relatedness | Contextual subscale scores | Related predictors or dimensions are not the same as intrinsic motivation itself |
| Behavioral indicator | Free-choice activity, persistence, exploration, manipulation, facial engagement | Observable choice or time-on-task | Sensitive to opportunity, task design, instructions, and context |
| Experimental context | Rewards, feedback, autonomy support, communication style | Change in reported or behavioral engagement | An effect belongs to the studied manipulation and protocol; it does not prove a universal law |
| Performance outcome | Learning, performance, creativity, or task completion | Task outcome | Performance can be influenced by skill, incentives, and many non-motivational factors |
| Neural correlate | Reward-related or large-scale network activity | Physiological or neural signal | A correlate under a protocol, not a direct readout of private motive |

## Source 1 — neuroscience and measurement review

Di Domenico, S. I., & Ryan, R. M. (2017). “The Emerging Neuroscience of Intrinsic Motivation: A New Frontier in Self-Determination Research.” *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 11, 145. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00145

The review describes intrinsic motivation as interest in and inherent satisfaction from an activity, distinct from engagement for separable rewards. It states that researchers assess it behaviorally through freely pursued activities, experientially through self-report about reasons for engagement and states such as interest, curiosity, and fun, and in some laboratory work through exploratory or manipulatory behaviors and facial displays of interested engagement. It also reviews emerging neuroscience work. Safe interpretation: free-choice behavior is a protocol-bound indicator, self-report describes experience or reasons, and neural activity is a correlate rather than direct mind-reading.

## Source 2 — meta-analysis of motivation measurement streams

Luther, L., Firmin, R. L., Lysaker, P. H., Minor, K. S., & Salyers, M. P. (2018). “A Meta-analytic Review of Self-reported, Clinician-rated, and Performance-based Motivation Measures in Schizophrenia: Are We Measuring the Same ‘Stuff’?” *Clinical Psychology Review*, 61, 24–37. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2018.04.001

This meta-analysis compared self-reported, clinician-rated, and performance-based motivation measures in a clinical literature. Its abstract reports modest convergence between clinician-rated measures and self-report or performance-based measures; the available self-report versus performance-based comparison was negligible and nonsignificant but underpowered. The authors conclude that methods should not be used interchangeably and should be described as the specific motivational construct or domain they capture. The population and clinical context also limit direct generalization to every motivation study.

## Source 3 — Intrinsic Motivation Inventory documentation

Self-Determination Theory. “Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI).” https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/intrinsic-motivation-inventory/

The documentation identifies the Interest/Enjoyment subscale as the primary self-report measure of intrinsic motivation itself. Other subscales address perceived choice, perceived competence, effort, value/usefulness, pressure/tension, and relatedness. Items may be adapted to specific activities. The documentation cautions that the inventory should be interpreted for a particular activity rather than as a universal trait, and that self-report and behavioral indices can correlate only modestly. Safe interpretation: a person’s enjoyment report in one task is not a complete measurement of motivation everywhere.

## Evidence-bounded conclusion

Intrinsic-motivation research uses multiple streams: activity-specific enjoyment reports, free-choice engagement or persistence, exploratory behavior, performance, reward or autonomy manipulations, and neural correlates. These measurements answer different questions. A participant choosing to continue may be showing behavior under a specific opportunity; a questionnaire records reported interest or reasons; a performance score is an outcome; and a neural signal is a correlate. None alone is a universal motivation meter or a direct window into private intent.

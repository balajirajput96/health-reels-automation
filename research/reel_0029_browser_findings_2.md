# Reel 0029 browser findings — default-effect source

On 2026-08-25, two public pages were opened.

1. PubMed record PMID 14631022 identifies Johnson and Goldstein, “Do Defaults Save Lives?”, *Science*, 2003, DOI 10.1126/science.1091721.

2. Columbia Business School’s faculty research page confirms the article title, authors, journal, year, and that the work investigated default effects on donation agreement rates in three studies. The reel will use this as an example of comparing choices under different default settings, not as a universal guarantee that defaults cause a particular real-world outcome.

The source is retained as a foundational example of default manipulation and recorded agreement decisions. The reel will explicitly distinguish a measured choice outcome from a person’s stable preference or moral character.

# REEL-0074 Source Validation: Journaling for Self-Observation, Not Diagnosis

## Editorial question

How can a reel present journaling as a possible self-observation tool without claiming that writing cures stress, works for everyone, diagnoses a condition, or replaces therapy?

## Claim register

| Claim | Evidence class | Support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expressive writing means writing about thoughts and feelings related to a stressful or meaningful experience. | Institutional explanation | Harvard Health describes expressive writing as writing about thoughts and feelings arising from a traumatic or stressful experience [1]. APA’s interview with James Pennebaker describes structured writing about challenging experiences [2]. | Ordinary journaling and research protocols are not identical. The reel should not turn a simple diary into a trauma-writing prescription. |
| Some studies suggest expressive writing may help some people cope with emotional fallout or stress. | Public-facing research summary and expert interview | Harvard says expressive writing may help some people cope but is not a cure-all and will not work for everyone [1]. APA presents Pennebaker’s account of a broad research literature with mixed results [2]. | Use “may help some people,” not “reduces stress for everyone” or “heals trauma.” |
| Writing can help organize thoughts and construct a coherent account of an experience. | Mechanistic hypothesis / expert interpretation | Harvard describes organizing thoughts and giving meaning as possible mechanisms; APA discusses translating emotional experience into words and creating structure [1] [2]. | These are proposed explanations, not proof of a single brain mechanism or guaranteed outcome. |
| Journaling can be used for self-observation, but it is not a diagnostic tool. | Editorial/safety boundary | The sources discuss reflection and writing, not self-diagnosis. | Do not infer a disorder from mood words, use a diary to diagnose, or present a template as clinical assessment. |
| Writing about difficult experiences can initially feel upsetting, and timing and context matter. | Safety-relevant institutional guidance | Harvard notes that expressive writing may initially upset people and that writing immediately after a traumatic event may make some people feel worse; it also notes limits for people with ongoing or severe mental-health challenges [1]. | The reel should not instruct viewers to write about trauma. If writing worsens distress or symptoms are significant, stop and seek appropriate support. |

## Editorial boundaries

The reel will focus on a low-intensity self-observation cue: write what happened, what you noticed, and what you need next, without treating the page as a diagnostic test. It will distinguish ordinary journaling from structured expressive-writing research and avoid trauma-writing instructions.

The reel will say that writing may help some people organize thoughts, but results vary. It will not promise stress relief, healing, improved physical health, or a specific duration/frequency. It will explicitly state that journaling is not therapy, diagnosis, or crisis care.

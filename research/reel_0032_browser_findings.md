# Reel 0032 source-retrieval findings

1. Lieberman et al. (2007), *Psychological Science*, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x, is indexed by PubMed. Its fMRI study compared affect labeling with other encoding conditions while participants viewed affective images. The abstract reports diminished amygdala and other limbic responses, increased right ventrolateral prefrontal activity, and an inverse relationship between those signals. The source supports discussing neural correlates, not reading a person’s exact feeling from a scan.

2. Lieberman et al. (2011), *Emotion*, DOI 10.1037/a0023503, is available through PMC. Across four studies, affect labeling was compared with passive watching, reappraisal, and distraction while participants reported emotional experience. The paper describes self-reported distress as an outcome and also separates participants’ predictions from experienced responses. It notes that labeling was associated with diminished self-reported distress for negative images and diminished self-reported pleasure for positive images in the reported paradigm; the reel uses only the measurement distinction and avoids universal advice.

3. Burklund et al. (2014), *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00221, reports a study comparing affect labeling, reappraisal, observation, and a shape-matching control in healthy older adults. It combines self-report and fMRI task contrasts. This supports the distinction between task instructions, subjective ratings, and neural activity.

4. Levy-Gigi and Shamay-Tsoory (2022), *PLOS ONE*, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0279303, reports performance-based affect-labeling paradigms in which participants choose labels and rate distress and label accuracy. The experiments manipulate labeling timing and emotional intensity; the authors report context-dependent effects, so the reel avoids telling viewers to use labeling as a guaranteed coping technique.

PMC pages were accessible for the latter two records through public extraction. No diagnosis, treatment claim, or individualized recommendation is made.

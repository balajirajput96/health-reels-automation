**Unique angle:** “सामाजिक समर्थन तनाव घटाता है” कहने से पहले पूछना कि किस social condition, किस stressor, किस measure और किस time window की तुलना हुई।

**Safety status:** SAFE_WITH_EDITS

## Evidence summary

The opened literature uses “social buffering” for a reduction or modulation of a specified stress response under a specified social condition. Researchers may compare the presence or assistance of another person, perceived access to support, received support, or a more specific interaction such as best-friend support during a laboratory stress task. These are different operationalizations and should not be treated as interchangeable [1] [2].

The measured response can be subjective, physiological, behavioral, neural, or an outcome observed later. Subjective stress, anxiety, calmness, affect, and appraisal are reports of experience. Salivary cortisol, cardiovascular reactivity, HPA-axis activity, or brain activity are signals collected under study conditions. A task response or intermediate endpoint is not automatically a diagnosis, a private-state readout, or an individual prediction [1] [3].

Daily-life studies use repeated experience-sampling or ecological-momentary-assessment prompts to capture current context, mood, stress, and behavior. Random smartphone prompts can improve temporal resolution and reduce dependence on retrospective recall, but repeated observations still require careful interpretation and do not turn association into causality [4].

## Source 1: Social-buffering review across development

Hostinar, C. E., Sullivan, R. M., & Gunnar, M. R. (2014). *Psychobiological Mechanisms Underlying the Social Buffering of the HPA Axis: A Review of Animal Models and Human Studies across Development*. Psychological Bulletin. DOI: [10.1037/a0032671](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032671). PMCID: [PMC3844011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3844011/).

This review defines social buffering as a dampened HPA-axis stress response to threat or challenge that occurs with the presence or assistance of a conspecific. It integrates neuroendocrine, behavioral, and social levels of analysis across animal and human research and emphasizes that the mechanisms and relevant components of social interaction remain incomplete. The source supports describing social buffering as a research construct, not as a universal property of all relationships or a clinical test.

## Source 2: Received versus perceived support and acute stress reactivity

Uchino, B. N., Carlisle, M., Birmingham, W., & Vaughn, A. A. (2010). *Social Support and the Reactivity Hypothesis: Conceptual Issues in Examining the Efficacy of Received Support During Acute Psychological Stress*. Biological Psychology, 86(2), 137–142. DOI: [10.1016/j.biopsycho.2010.04.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2010.04.003). PMID: [20398724](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20398724/). PMCID: [PMC2928410](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928410/).

This review separates perceived support, meaning potential access to support, from received support, meaning support resources reported as received during a specified time frame. It explains that laboratory reactivity paradigms often model received support and that task-related, recipient-related, and provider-related factors can moderate effectiveness. Cardiovascular reactivity is discussed as an intermediate endpoint whose health implications require additional work. The source supports insisting on the support type, task, provider, recipient, measure, and time window.

## Source 3: Specific human laboratory experiment

Heinrichs, M., Baumgartner, T., Kirschbaum, C., & Ehlert, U. (2003). *Social support and oxytocin interact to suppress cortisol and subjective responses to psychosocial stress*. Biological Psychiatry, 54(12), 1389–1398. DOI: [10.1016/S0006-3223(03)00465-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0006-3223(03)00465-7). PMID: [14675803](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14675803/).

The PubMed abstract describes a placebo-controlled, double-blind study of 37 healthy men exposed to the Trier Social Stress Test. Participants were assigned to intranasal oxytocin or placebo and to best-friend social support during the preparation period or no support. The study measured salivary free cortisol, calmness, and anxiety. The report is useful for showing how a social-buffering claim is tied to a specific sample, stressor, support timing, manipulation, and outcomes. It does not establish a universal effect or public treatment recommendation.

## Source 4: Experience-sampling mapping study

Dogan, G., Patlar Akbulut, F., Catal, C., & Mishra, A. (2022). *Stress Detection Using Experience Sampling: A Systematic Mapping Study*. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(9), 5693. DOI: [10.3390/ijerph19095693](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19095693). PMID: [35565088](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35565088/). PMCID: [PMC9105860](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9105860/).

This mapping study describes experience sampling or ecological momentary assessment as repeated smartphone prompts that capture current context and psychological events such as mood in daily life. Random sampling is common; physiological signals are often used for physical-stress research, while surveys are common for mental-stress research. The source supports explaining why daily-life studies can sample context and timing repeatedly, while still remaining measurements rather than complete mental-state readings.

## Measurement map

| Layer | Possible operationalization | Evidence-bounded interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Social condition | Presence, assistance, perceived support, received support, or best-friend support during a preparation window | These conditions are distinct; “support” is not one universal variable. |
| Stressor/task | Trier Social Stress Test, speech preparation, social evaluation, or another defined challenge | The task and its timing constrain the result. |
| Subjective appraisal | Stress, anxiety, calmness, affect, fear, perceived effort, or support rating | A report describes the participant’s stated experience, not every body process. |
| Physiology | Salivary cortisol, cardiovascular reactivity, HPA-axis activity, or related signals | A correlate or endpoint is not direct mind reading or diagnosis. |
| Behavior/social process | Support-seeking, interaction quality, provider behavior, task performance, or current context | Recipient, provider, relationship, and context can moderate the finding. |
| Daily-life timing | Repeated random smartphone prompts, current mood, stress, context, and behavior | More time points improve temporal resolution but do not guarantee causality. |
| Outcome | Immediate reactivity, recovery, intermediate health endpoint, or later association | Association and intermediate endpoints are not automatic individual predictions. |

## Allowed claims for R0080

The reel may say that social-buffering studies compare a specified stressor with a specified social condition and then measure selected subjective, physiological, behavioral, neural, or outcome variables. It may say that perceived and received support differ, that task and relationship context matter, and that daily-life sampling adds repeated time points. It may use the human laboratory experiment as an example of a defined design without generalizing beyond its sample and measures.

## Excluded claims and safety boundaries

Do not say that friends always lower cortisol, that social contact cures anxiety, that oxytocin should be taken or used by viewers, that one interaction proves resilience, or that one biomarker diagnoses mental health. Do not give an individual recommendation, imply a guarantee, or present a laboratory association as a universal causal law. Keep the reel educational and not published.

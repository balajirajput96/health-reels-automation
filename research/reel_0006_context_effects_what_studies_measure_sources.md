# Reel 0006 — Context Effects: What Studies Measure

## Unique angle
This reel explains that a framing/context experiment usually measures how responses change when the same or closely matched choice is presented in different descriptions or option sets. It does not establish that a person has a permanent, global “bias score.”

## Evidence class
Peer-reviewed review plus peer-reviewed experimental study. The reel distinguishes an experimental response pattern from a fixed personality label, diagnosis, or prediction about an individual.

## Sources and verified findings

1. **Kühberger, A. (2023). A systematic review of risky-choice framing effects. EXCLI Journal, 22, 1012–1031.** DOI: [10.17179/excli2023-6169](https://doi.org/10.17179/excli2023-6169); PMID: [37927347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37927347/); full text: [PMC10620856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620856/).
   - The review describes risky-choice framing as a comparison in which linguistically different descriptions of ostensibly equivalent options can produce different judgments or choices.
   - It surveys variations in framing-task design, response mode, between- versus within-participants comparisons, and domain. The review states that these variations can change effect size and interpretation.
   - The review argues that framing effects should not automatically be treated as simple evidence of human irrationality; context-sensitive processes can be adaptive in some settings.
   - Reel-safe interpretation: a framing task measures a response under a specified description and design. It does not by itself prove a stable trait in every situation.

2. **Aczel, B., Bago, B., Szollosi, A., Foldes, A., & Lukacs, B. (2015). Measuring Individual Differences in Decision Biases: Methodological Considerations. Frontiers in Psychology, 6, 1770.** DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01770](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01770); PMID: [26635677](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26635677/); full text: [PMC4652008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4652008/).
   - In two studies, the authors examined whether different examples of bias tasks could be treated as interchangeable and whether they measured a common underlying construct.
   - The abstract reports weak internal consistency and substantial discrepancies in extracted factor patterns; the authors emphasize domain specificity, wording, and response mode as methodological issues.
   - Reel-safe interpretation: a score from one task, or a short collection of tasks, should not automatically be treated as a complete measure of a person’s reasoning ability or character.

3. **Di Crosta, A., Marin, A., Palumbo, R., et al. (2023). Changing Decisions: The Interaction between Framing and Decoy Effects. Behavioral Sciences, 13(9), 755.** DOI: [10.3390/bs13090755](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs13090755); PMID: [37754033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37754033/); full text: [PMC10525293](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10525293/).
   - In an undergraduate sample of 471, the study used a 2 × 3 within-subject design to examine positive versus negative framing and the presence/type of a decoy option.
   - The reported pattern was that an incongruent decoy reduced the framing effect in both valence conditions relative to controls, while a congruent decoy increased the framing effect in the positive-framing condition; confidence effects also differed by condition.
   - Reel-safe interpretation: adding another contextual feature can change the measured effect, showing why task design and option context matter.

## Claim boundaries

- **Established evidence:** In controlled experiments, wording, framing, and option context can alter measured choices; task design and response mode influence interpretation.
- **Not established by this reel:** That every person is equally affected; that one task diagnoses a bias in an individual; that a task score is permanent; that a “bias test” predicts life outcomes; or that one framing result proves irrationality.
- **Public-education safety:** No diagnosis, treatment, personalized assessment, guarantee, or outcome promise. Viewers should treat the reel as a research-methods explainer, not a self-test.

# Reel 0018 — मस्तिष्क-गोलार्ध मिथक: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Verified source record

### [1] Nielsen, Zielinski, Ferguson, Lainhart & Anderson (2013), *An Evaluation of the Left-Brain vs. Right-Brain Hypothesis with Resting State Functional Connectivity Magnetic Resonance Imaging*

- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0071275`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0071275
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3743825/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed resting-state functional-connectivity study.
- Relevant boundary: the study analyzed hemispheric lateralization of functional connections in 1,011 publicly available scans. It found lateralized hubs and networks, but reported that lateralization appeared local rather than a whole-brain phenotype of globally greater “left-brained” or “right-brained” network strength across individuals.

### [2] Cai, Van der Haegen & Brysbaert (2013), *Complementary hemispheric specialization for language production and visuospatial attention*

- DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1212956110`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1212956110
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed task-based neuroimaging study.
- Relevant boundary: the study measured lateralization for language production and visuospatial attention with separate tasks in people with typical and atypical language dominance. The result concerns these defined functions and task contrasts, not a general personality category or a universal division of all cognition into two sides.

### [3] Corballis (2014), *Left Brain, Right Brain: Facts and Fantasies*

- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pbio.1001767`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001767
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed PLOS Biology essay synthesizing lateralization evidence.
- Relevant boundary: the essay distinguishes established asymmetries from popular extrapolations such as a creative right brain and logical left brain. It notes that lateralization is a property of particular functions and networks, and that creative thought involves a widespread network rather than a simple one-hemisphere label.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement window | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Language task | Activation or laterality index during word generation, picture naming, or speech production | Task-specific hemispheric preference for that function in that sample | That all language or all reasoning is confined to one side |
| Visuospatial task | Activation or laterality index during a defined attention or line-bisection task | Task-specific spatial-attention asymmetry | That the right hemisphere is a universal “creative” side |
| Resting-state fMRI | Statistical dependence among regional BOLD time series and hemispheric asymmetry of connections | Lateralized network organization under the scan and analysis choices | A global left-brain/right-brain personality type |
| Structural asymmetry | Gray-matter or anatomical differences between homologous regions | A measured anatomical asymmetry | A direct readout of cognitive style or talent |
| Network analysis | Hubs, modules, degree, or lateralized connection patterns | Mathematical summaries of selected regions and edges | A single region or hemisphere as the sole cause of behavior |
| Group comparison | Average or distributional differences across a sample | A population-level result under the design | A label for every individual in the population |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel says that hemispheric specialization is real for some defined functions, while the popular “left-brained versus right-brained person” category is not supported by the cited resting-state evidence. It avoids claiming that one hemisphere is always dominant, that creativity belongs to only one side, or that handedness alone reveals a person’s cognitive style. Task measures, resting-state measures, and individual inference remain distinct.

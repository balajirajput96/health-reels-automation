# Reel 0041 research retrieval audit

## Research question

Reel 0041 asks: **When studies connect identity with habits, what is actually being measured?** The unique angle is that identity is usually a self-description or role-related construct, while habit strength, repeated behavior, context stability, and observed outcomes are separate variables. The reel will not treat “I am this kind of person” as proof of a fixed trait or a causal guarantee.

## Evidence notes

Verplanken and Sui’s paper is about the intertwined relationship between habit and identity, including behavioral, cognitive, affective, and motivational facets. The safe methodological takeaway is that identity and habit are related constructs that can be measured separately; neither label is a direct readout of a person’s full self or future behavior. The reel will avoid quoting a result that was not independently read from the full article because the PMC access page was CAPTCHA-gated in this retrieval.

Van der Weiden et al. tracked people who wanted to form a habit over approximately 90 days. The sample included health behaviors such as eating fruit or vegetables, exercising, or drinking water; interpersonal, financial, and environmental behaviors were also included. Daily measures covered goal-congruent behavior performance, self-control capacity, and habit strength. The abstract reports that habit formation increased substantially over three months, especially among people who consistently performed the desired behavior, while self-control capacity did not appear to affect the habit-formation process as expected. The study is longitudinal and informative about trajectories, but it does not establish a fixed duration for every person or show that identity statements cause habit change.

The Frontiers article explains that habit strength is often tracked through repeated behavior in a context and a measure of automaticity or habit strength. It also describes a distinction between a person’s intentions, their repeated goal-congruent behavior, and the later development of habit strength. This supports a script that asks viewers to separate what participants said about themselves, what they repeatedly did, what context was present, and what outcome scale was used.

## Evidence boundary for the script

The reel may say: “Identity is usually operationalized through a self-report about a role, value, or self-description; habit may be assessed through automaticity items, context-behavior consistency, repeated behavior, or longitudinal ratings.” It may say: “A relationship between identity and behavior is not the same as a causal identity intervention.” It must avoid personality diagnosis, moral judgment, fixed labels, self-help prescriptions, guarantees, and claims that declaring an identity rewires the brain.

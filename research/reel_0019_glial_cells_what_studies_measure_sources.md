## Verified source record

### [1] Guttenplan & Liddelow (2019), *Astrocytes and microglia: Models and tools*

- DOI: `10.1084/jem.20180200`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20180200
- Full text: https://rupress.org/jem/article/216/1/71/42439/Astrocytes-and-microglia-Models-and-toolsThe
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed methods review.
- Relevant boundary: glial studies use cell-type markers, genetic lines, in-vivo and in-vitro models, purification, culture, and transcriptome resources. Markers are not automatically perfect labels; for example, GFAP does not identify every astrocyte and may also be expressed outside the intended population. Culture and genetic targeting can differ from intact brain biology and require validation.

### [2] Garland, Hartnell & Boche (2022), *Microglia and Astrocyte Function and Communication: What Do We Know in Humans?*

- DOI: `10.3389/fnins.2022.824888`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.824888
- Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.824888/full
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed review of human glial methods.
- Relevant boundary: human glial research combines genetics, transcriptomics, primary culture, iPSC-derived models, post-mortem immunohistochemistry, PET, MRI, and fluid biomarkers. Each has trade-offs: post-mortem tissue is static, iPSC or culture models may differ from cells in vivo, MRI lacks cell specificity, and biomarker links to glial activity can be indirect. Simple M1/M2 or A1/A2 labels may oversimplify heterogeneous states.

### [3] İş, Min, Wang, Oatman, Daniel & Ertekin-Taner (2024), *Multi Layered Omics Approaches Reveal Glia Specific Alterations in Alzheimer’s Disease: A Systematic Review and Future Prospects*

- DOI: `10.1002/glia.24652`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1002/glia.24652
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11784841/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed systematic review.
- Relevant boundary: genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and lipidomics measure different molecular layers. Single-cell and single-nucleus RNA sequencing can reveal cell-type expression profiles, but the review emphasizes that different sampling and technical choices can produce differences and that molecular association is not by itself proof of a cell’s complete function or disease causation.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement layer | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cell identity / markers | Protein or gene markers, immunostaining, genetic reporters | A labeled or enriched cell population under the chosen method | That one marker captures every cell or every state of that glial type |
| Morphology | Cell shape, process length, branching, density, spatial relation | Structural state or location in a sample | A complete functional or emotional interpretation |
| Physiology | Calcium signals, membrane properties, uptake, metabolism, phagocytosis, or communication in a model | A measured cellular response under defined conditions | That an in-vitro or animal response is identical in humans |
| Transcriptomics / omics | RNA, chromatin, protein, metabolite, or lipid profiles | Molecular associations and cell-state signatures | Direct proof of behavior, diagnosis, or causality without validation |
| Post-mortem tissue | Cells and markers in preserved human brain sections | In-situ snapshots of human tissue | The full time course of a living process |
| PET / fluid biomarkers | Tracer binding or molecules in blood/CSF | Indirect or molecular-level signals linked to selected targets | Perfect cell specificity or a complete readout of glial function |
| MRI | Tissue-level structure, water or hemodynamic contrasts | Non-invasive organ/tissue-level correlates | A glia-specific measurement in an individual scan |
| Model systems | Primary culture, iPSC-derived cells, organoids, rodents | Mechanistic tests under controlled conditions | Automatic equivalence to intact human brain biology |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel explains that “glial activity” is not one single observable. Researchers may measure markers, morphology, physiology, molecular profiles, tissue snapshots, or indirect in-vivo signals. The reel does not treat a marker as a complete cell identity, a PET/MRI signal as a direct thought or diagnosis, or an animal/culture finding as automatically identical to human brain function. The older binary activation labels are presented as simplifications rather than universal categories.

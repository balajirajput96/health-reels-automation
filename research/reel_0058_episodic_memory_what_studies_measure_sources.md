# Reel 0058 — Episodic memory: what studies measure

## Research question

When a study says it measured episodic memory, did it measure free recall, recognition, source/context memory, sequence reproduction, confidence, or a model-derived proxy? This record keeps those categories distinct.

## Operational categories

**Free recall or sequence reproduction:** a participant produces studied material or an ordered sequence. Accuracy, omissions, intrusions, and order errors are observable task outputs.

**Item recognition:** a participant classifies an item as old/new or selects a studied item among alternatives. It can be influenced by familiarity and lure similarity.

**Source/context memory:** a participant identifies where, when, or under which context an item was encountered. Source accuracy may differ from item recognition accuracy.

**Confidence and vividness:** participant-level or trial-level judgments about certainty or the experience of remembering. These are subjective reports and can dissociate from factual accuracy.

**Neural/model-derived interpretation:** brain activity or a mathematical model may be used to study mechanisms or estimate latent processes. Such signals and parameters are proxies/estimates tied to the task and assumptions, not direct access to a memory trace or consciousness.

## Safe conclusion

Episodic-memory research often measures one or more task-defined outputs: recall, recognition, source/context accuracy, sequence order, response time, confidence, or vividness. These measures can relate but are not interchangeable. A single score does not establish a person’s complete autobiographical memory, mental time travel, or cognitive health.

## Safety and editorial boundaries

This is public education, not diagnosis, treatment, prescription, or individualized cognitive assessment. Do not infer a memory disorder, deception, intelligence, or mental state from one test result, confidence rating, or brain/model signal. No personal prediction or guarantee is made.

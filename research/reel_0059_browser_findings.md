# Reel 0059 browser/research findings

## Source 1 — De Marco, Blackburn, & Venneri (2021)
DOI: 10.3389/fnagi.2021.678588
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8370562/
Title: Serial Recall Order and Semantic Features of Category Fluency Words to Study Semantic Memory in Normal Ageing.

The open study describes the Category Fluency Test as a common semantic-memory measure but explicitly states that performance is also influenced by other cognitive functions. In a one-minute category-fluency task, participants generate valid category members; the study examines serial recall order and linguistic features of generated words. The introduction notes contributions from executive functioning/semantic control, processing speed, episodic memory, and expressive language. Safe interpretation: number and order of generated category words are task outputs with multiple contributors, not a pure readout of semantic knowledge.

## Source 2 — Duff, Covington, Hilverman, & Cohen (2019)
DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2019.00471
URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2019.00471/full
Title: Semantic Memory and the Hippocampus: Revisiting, Reaffirming, and Extending the Reach of Their Critical Relationship.

The review describes semantic memory as flexible, reconstructive, relational, and multimodal, and examines methods and theories concerning its relationship with episodic memory and the hippocampus. It contrasts remote word knowledge tests such as naming or matching labels with phrases/definitions/sentences against new semantic learning. Safe interpretation: semantic-memory performance and neural/theoretical accounts depend on the materials, task, learning history, and analysis; a brain region or one score is not a direct readout of all knowledge.

## Source 3 — Irak, Soylu, & Yavuz (2023)
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-28595-z
URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-28595-z
Title: Comparing event-related potentials of retrospective and prospective metacognitive judgments during episodic and semantic memory.

The open-access study compared behavioral and ERP correlates of retrospective confidence judgments and prospective feeling-of-knowing judgments during episodic and semantic-memory tasks. It reported that the two judgment types showed different behavioral/domain patterns and different ERP temporal dynamics, with metacognitive judgments linked to distributed neural substrates. Safe interpretation: confidence/FOK are metacognitive judgments, while ERP patterns are neural correlates associated with a task; neither is a direct measurement of all semantic knowledge.

## Source 4 — Kumar (2021)
DOI: 10.3758/s13423-020-01792-x
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01792-x
Title: Semantic memory: A review of methods, models, and current challenges.

The review describes semantic memory as knowledge about the world, concepts, and symbols, while emphasizing that modern accounts are sensitive to context, task demands, and perceptual/sensorimotor information. It reviews network, feature, and distributional models and argues for comparisons of computational models with human baselines on semantic tasks. Safe interpretation: model outputs are theoretical/computational accounts evaluated against task behavior; they are not direct measurements of an individual’s stored knowledge.

# Reel 0046 — चिंतन परंपराएँ: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence-bounded claim

A contemplative tradition is broader than a laboratory intervention. A study may assign a practice with a particular duration, frequency, instruction, and framing, then measure selected outcomes such as self-reported well-being, affect, sleep, social engagement, or spiritual experience. These measurements do not capture every philosophical, ethical, cultural, or metaphysical dimension of a tradition.

## Source 1 — Religious framing in a randomized trial

**Citation:** Rohde, J. S., Goldy, S., Graziosi, M., Ferguson, M. A., Thrul, J., & Yaden, D. B. (2024). *Centering and flourishing: an online intervention study assessing the effects of a Christian contemplative practice on stress-reduction and human flourishing*. BMC Psychology, 12, 373. DOI: [10.1186/s40359-024-01836-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-024-01836-0).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed randomized controlled trial.

**Design:** The online trial enrolled 702 self-identifying Christians. Participants were randomized to Centering Prayer with an explicitly religious framing, the same practice without an explicitly religious framing as an active control, or simple reflection on the day as a passive control. Participants completed the assigned practice daily for 28 days. Follow-ups occurred one day, one week, and one month after the practice period.

**Outcomes:** The primary one-week well-being measure showed no group differences between conditions, although each group increased from baseline to follow-up. Group differences were found on acute spiritual-experience measures, including the Mystical Experience Questionnaire and Daily Spiritual Experience Questionnaire. The authors concluded that religious framing may not enhance well-being effects but may alter spiritual outcomes related to contemplative practices.

**Measurement boundary:** This tests one Christian contemplative practice, one sample, one set of framings, and specified questionnaires. It does not establish that all traditions work alike, that spiritual experience proves a supernatural claim, or that the practice guarantees flourishing.

## Source 2 — Broader mindfulness outcome review

**Citation:** Keng, S.-L., Smoski, M. J., & Robins, C. J. (2011). *Effects of mindfulness on psychological health: a review of empirical studies*. Clinical Psychology Review, 31(6), 1041–1056. DOI: [10.1016/j.cpr.2011.04.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2011.04.006).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed empirical review.

**Use in this reel:** This review is used as broader context that modern studies operationalize mindfulness with selected psychological outcomes and study designs. The reel does not import unsupported numerical claims from the review.

**Measurement boundary:** A review of empirical outcomes cannot substitute for a tradition’s complete worldview or settle metaphysical questions. Effects depend on the specific practice, comparator, population, and outcome instrument.

## Source 3 — Observational practice behavior and well-being

**Citation:** Rich, T., et al. (2022). *Contemplative Practices Behavior Is Positively Associated with Well-Being*. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(20), 13485. DOI: [10.3390/ijerph192013485](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192013485).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed observational research.

**Use in this reel:** This source illustrates that practice behavior and well-being can be studied as an association. Observational association is not randomized causal evidence.

**Measurement boundary:** Reported practice frequency and well-being scores may be affected by self-selection, recall, confounding, and shared method. The finding does not prove that practice caused an individual’s well-being.

## Language and safety controls

Use “tradition,” “assigned practice,” “religious framing,” “self-report,” “spiritual-experience questionnaire,” “well-being outcome,” and “in this sample.” Do not say “science proves or disproves spirituality,” “a questionnaire measures enlightenment,” “religious practice guarantees flourishing,” or “one score reveals faith.” Respect spiritual belief while distinguishing belief, philosophy, hypothesis, and empirical measurement.

No diagnosis, treatment, individualized spiritual instruction, moral judgment, guaranteed benefit, religious persuasion, or universal claim will be given.

# Reel 0048 — स्पेसिंग इफ़ेक्ट: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence-bounded claim

Spacing-effect research compares repetitions separated by different time gaps and then measures performance either soon after training or after a delay. Immediate learning, delayed retention, actual adherence to the schedule, and transfer to a new task are separate outcomes. A reported “optimal gap” is conditional on the material, task, learner group, and final-test delay; it is not a universal personal timetable.

## Source 1 — Long-timescale review

**Citation:** Smith, C. D., & Scarf, D. (2017). *Spacing Repetitions Over Long Timescales: A Review and a Reconsolidation Explanation*. Frontiers in Psychology, 8, 962. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00962](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00962).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed review and hypothesis/theory article.

**Operational distinction:** The review defines the spacing effect as repetitions spaced in time producing stronger memories than repetitions massed closer together. It defines learning or acquisition as performance at the end of training or on an immediate test, and retention as performance after a delay. Those measures are not interchangeable: learning reflects peak performance, whereas retention reflects forgetting across time.

**Scope:** The review covers long-timescale spacing in skill-related tasks, language-related tasks, and generalization across adults and children. It reports that spaced practice often outperforms massed practice, but also emphasizes task- and age-related differences. Its reconsolidation account is a proposed explanation, not a directly established mechanism in every learning context.

## Source 2 — Primary retention study

**Citation:** Cepeda, N. J., Vul, E., Rohrer, D., Wixted, J. T., & Pashler, H. (2008). *Spacing effects in learning: a temporal ridgeline of optimal retention*. Psychological Science, 19(11), 1095–1102. DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed original multi-interval retention research.

**Design and measures:** More than 1,350 participants learned a set of facts. A review gap of up to 3.5 months followed learning, and a final test occurred after an additional delay of up to one year. The study manipulated or compared interstudy gaps and measured final-test performance after specified delays.

**Reported result:** At a given test delay, increasing the interstudy gap first improved and then gradually reduced final-test performance. The optimal gap increased as test delay increased. Expressed as a proportion of test delay, the PubMed abstract reports an optimal gap of about 20–40% of a one-week test delay and about 5–10% of a one-year test delay.

**Limits:** These ranges describe the study’s fact-learning conditions and delayed tests. They do not create a universal schedule for every learner, subject, or skill. The outcome was delayed fact retention, not intelligence, motivation, identity, or all forms of real-world transfer.

## Source 3 — Spacing retrieval-practice synthesis

**Citation:** Latimier, A., Peyre, H., & Ramus, F. (2021). *A meta-analytic review of the benefit of spacing out retrieval practice episodes on retention*. Educational Psychology Review. DOI: [10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed meta-analysis identified during evidence review.

**Use in this reel:** This source supports the distinction between spacing repetitions and spacing retrieval-practice episodes. The reel does not add numerical claims from the meta-analysis without a separately verified full-text extraction.

## Language and safety controls

Use “interstudy gap,” “massed,” “spaced,” “immediate performance,” “delayed retention,” and “conditional optimal gap.” Say “in this study” and “on average” where appropriate. Do not say “review every exact number of days and everyone will remember,” “spacing guarantees transfer,” or “a recall score measures intelligence.” No individualized study schedule, diagnosis, grade guarantee, or personal performance prediction will be given.

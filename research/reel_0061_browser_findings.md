# Reel 0061 browser/research findings

## Source 1 — Lee, Nader, & Schiller (2017)
DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2017.04.006
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5605913/
Title: An update on memory reconsolidation updating.

The review describes the reconsolidation framework as follows: reactivation can, but does not always, destabilize an existing memory; a subsequent process may restabilize or update it. Behavioral interference protocols reactivate a long-term memory and then present new or conflicting information. Outcomes can include reduced accuracy for an initial procedural sequence, altered retrieval of original declarative information, or intrusions of new material. The review emphasizes that the original memory is not simply erased, and that effects depend on memory system and experimental conditions. Safe interpretation: later behavior is evidence about memory expression/update under a protocol, not a direct observation of a hidden memory trace.

## Source 2 — Forcato et al. (2016)
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0151381
URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0151381
Title: Differential Left Hippocampal Activation during Retrieval with Different Types of Reminders: An fMRI Study of the Reconsolidation Process.

This human study compared reminder structures during retrieval of picture-word associations and later interference/retention tests. It reported that a specific context-plus-one-cue reminder, rather than context alone or a full cue pair, was associated with susceptibility to interference and later memory effects. The same condition showed differential left hippocampal activity during reminder presentation. Safe interpretation: the study supports condition-specific behavioral and fMRI associations; it does not mean every retrieval makes a memory labile, nor that hippocampal activity directly reads memory contents.

## Source 3 — Elsey, Van Ast, & Kindt (2018)
DOI: 10.1037/bul0000152
URL: https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000152
Title: Human memory reconsolidation: A guiding framework and critical review of the evidence.

The review formalizes a framework for evaluating human reconsolidation studies and notes inconsistent findings and alternative explanations. Its PubMed abstract cautions that current evidence cannot conclusively infer reconsolidation at the neurobiological level from observed human behavioral effects; reconsolidation remains a viable but contested explanation for some changes in memory expression. Safe interpretation: later recall, fear response, or recognition changes are outcomes that may be consistent with updating, but they do not uniquely prove the mechanism.

## Source 4 — Stemerding, Stibbe, Van Ast, & Kindt (2022)
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-06119-5
URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-06119-5
Title: Demarcating the boundary conditions of memory reconsolidation: An unsuccessful replication.

This open-access replication tested prediction-error conditions in a human fear-memory procedure and assessed expectancy ratings, later fear retention, and related outcomes. The article reports that the reconsolidation intervention was not guaranteed and strongly depended on successful destabilization; in its manipulation, expectancy ratings did not decline as expected, which may indicate that memories were not successfully destabilized. Safe interpretation: retrieval, prediction error, timing, and memory strength are experimental conditions; absence or presence of a behavioral effect cannot be simplified into routine memory erasure or guaranteed therapeutic change.

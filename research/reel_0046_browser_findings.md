# Reel 0046 research retrieval audit

## Research question

Reel 0046 asks: **What changes when a contemplative tradition becomes a study intervention?** The safe distinction is between a tradition’s spiritual or philosophical aims, the practice assigned in an experiment, and the outcomes measured by questionnaires, behavior, or physiological proxies. A measured change in one outcome does not validate every metaphysical or spiritual claim.

## Rohde et al. (2024) checked details

This online randomized controlled trial enrolled 702 self-identifying Christians. Participants were randomized to one of three conditions: Centering Prayer presented with an explicitly religious framing, the same practice presented without an explicitly religious framing as an active control, or simple reflection on the day as a passive control. Assigned practice was completed daily for 28 days. The study assessed well-being at one day, one week, and one month after the practice period.

The primary one-week well-being outcome showed no group differences between the conditions, while each group increased in well-being from baseline to follow-up. The study did find group differences on acute spiritual-experience measures, including the Mystical Experience Questionnaire and Daily Spiritual Experience Questionnaire. The authors’ interpretation was that religious framing may not enhance well-being effects but may alter spiritual outcomes related to contemplative practices.

This is useful for the reel because it keeps framing and outcome separate. The trial tested one Christian contemplative practice, one sample, and specified questionnaires and follow-up windows. It does not prove that all contemplative traditions work the same way, that a spiritual experience is supernatural evidence, or that the practice produces universal flourishing.

## Evidence boundaries

The reel may say that contemplative traditions contain meanings and goals that are not identical to experimental variables. A study may operationalize a practice by instructions, frequency, duration, and framing, then measure self-reported well-being, daily affect, sleep, social engagement, or spiritual-experience questionnaires. Randomization can compare assigned conditions, but it does not measure every dimension of a tradition.

The reel may also say that well-being and spiritual-experience outcomes can diverge: in the checked trial, religious framing did not produce a one-week primary well-being difference, while some acute spiritual-experience measures differed. This is a study-specific result, not a verdict on religion, spirituality, or all meditation.

The reel must not dismiss spiritual belief, claim that science proves or disproves metaphysical ideas, equate a questionnaire with enlightenment, or infer a person’s faith or inner state from a score. No diagnosis, treatment, individualized spiritual instruction, moral judgment, guarantee, or universal claim will be given.

# Reel 0054 evidence record: बेस-रेट तर्क — studies क्या मापते हैं

## Claim boundary

This reel explains base-rate reasoning as the use of prior probability together with case-specific evidence. It distinguishes laboratory base-rate neglect patterns from real-world expert decisions and does not diagnose any person or give medical advice.

## Source 1 — base rates and intuitive processing

Pennycook, G., Trippas, D., Handley, S. J., and Thompson, V. A. (2014). *Base rates: both neglected and intuitive*. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 40(2), 544–554. DOI: [10.1037/a0034887](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034887). Participants solved base-rate problems with belief/statistics instructions, both with free time and a time limit. In conflict problems, base rates interfered with belief judgments as stereotypes interfered with statistics judgments, reflected in response time and confidence. The authors concluded that base rates may be accessible to intuitive processing even though they are often underweighted.

## Source 2 — real-world sequential expertise

Link, D. and Raab, M. (2021). *Experts use base rates in real-world sequential decisions*. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 29(2), 660–667. DOI: [10.3758/s13423-021-02024-6](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-021-02024-6). The study analyzed 1,347 beach-volleyball matches and operationalized a player's base rate as prior success over a defined window and selection rate as how often that player received a serve. Expert players adapted selection decisions to opponent performance and showed threshold-like sensitivity. This is a sport-specific observational analysis of sequential decisions, not proof that all experts always use base rates.

## Source 3 — broader problem space and modeling

Stengård, E., Juslin, P., Hahn, U., and van den Berg, R. (2022). *On the generality and cognitive basis of base-rate neglect*. Cognition, 226, 105160. DOI: [10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105160](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105160). The study varied base rate, hit rate, and false-alarm rate and compared normalized versus natural-frequency and symbolic versus pictorial formats. Patterns associated with base-rate neglect generalized across a broader problem space, but there were large individual differences and different model fits. The result supports conditional, task-specific interpretation rather than an all-or-none label.

## Source 4 — measuring full beliefs

Howe, P. D. L., Perfors, A., Walker, B., Kashima, Y., and Fay, N. (2022/2023). *Base rate neglect and conservatism in probabilistic reasoning: Insights from eliciting full distributions*. Judgment and Decision Making, 17(5), 962–987. DOI: [10.1017/S1930297500009281](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1930297500009281). Across three experiments, participants reported full prior and posterior distributions. The method separated base-rate neglect from conservatism more directly than a single point estimate and showed substantial individual variation; explicit priors reduced neglect for many participants.

## Operational measure map

| Study element | What it measures | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base rate or prior | Frequency or prior belief before new evidence | The final probability without likelihood information |
| Hit rate | Probability of a positive cue when the condition is present | The probability the condition is present after a positive cue |
| False-alarm rate | Probability of a positive cue when the condition is absent | A person's actual risk in a new population |
| Posterior estimate | Updated probability after combining prior and evidence | Whether the participant used a particular cognitive mechanism |
| Response time/confidence | Effort or certainty associated with an answer | Correctness or intelligence by itself |
| Choice in a sequential task | How decisions track prior performance over time | General reasoning ability across domains |
| Natural-frequency format | Performance with counts rather than percentages | A universal cure for reasoning errors |
| Cognitive model fit | A candidate description of response patterns | Direct observation of mental processes |

## Safety and scientific limits

A study answer can differ from a normative Bayesian answer for many reasons, including representation, format, uncertainty, task demands, cue diagnosticity, and individual differences. Base-rate neglect is an empirical pattern relative to a model, not a clinical label. A medical-diagnosis-style example illustrates probability structure only; it is not personal medical risk assessment. The reel is public education and provides no medical, financial, or individualized decision advice.

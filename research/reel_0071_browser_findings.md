# R0071 Browser Research Findings: Belonging — What Studies Measure

## Source 1: Allen et al. (2021), Belonging: A Review of Conceptual Issues, an Integrative Framework, and Directions for Future Research
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8095671/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00049530.2021.1883409

The review defines a sense of belonging as a subjective feeling of deep connection with social groups, places, and individual or collective experiences. It presents an integrative framework with four interrelated components: competencies, opportunities, motivations, and perceptions. The review emphasizes that belonging is dynamic and exists in connection with social systems and contexts. Safe interpretation: a belonging score is a measure of a defined perceived experience or construct; it is not a direct count of relationships and does not automatically represent belonging in every setting.

## Source 2: Gordon & Stiefel (2024), A brief but comprehensive three-item social connectedness screener
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11259274/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0307107

The paper distinguishes three social-connectedness subdomains: frequency of social contacts, perceived social/emotional support, and loneliness/social isolation. Its brief screener asks about contact with people one cares about, feeling lonely or isolated, and having enough social/emotional support. The article also distinguishes objective structural measures such as network size, contact frequency, living situation, or relationship status from subjective perceptions of support and loneliness. In its cross-sectional survey, loneliness/social-isolation and support-risk variables showed stronger associations with emotional-health self-report measures than contact frequency alone, while contact frequency and structural variables were related but not interchangeable. Safe interpretation: number of interactions, perceived support, loneliness, and belonging are related dimensions, not identical outcomes; the screener is not a universal social-connection meter.

## Source 3: Brady et al. (2020), A brief social-belonging intervention in college improves adult outcomes for Black Americans
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7190359/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aay3689

This randomized follow-up studied a one-hour social-belonging intervention delivered during the first year of college. The intervention represented common early-college social and academic adversity as normal and temporary. The reported adult follow-up assessed specified outcomes including career satisfaction and success, psychological well-being, community involvement and leadership, with college mentorship examined as a statistical mediator. The study population and intervention were specific: Black American college students in a defined educational context.

Safe interpretation: an intervention result can test a causal contribution of a specified belonging-related concern under the studied conditions, but it does not show that one short exercise changes every person’s belonging, prove a universal mechanism, or substitute for measuring the relevant outcome directly. The reel should distinguish belonging-related self-report or uncertainty from academic, health, mentorship, or community outcomes.

## Working evidence boundary

Belonging research may measure a subjective sense of fit, acceptance, connection, or uncertainty; structural contact and network variables; perceived support and loneliness; or outcomes such as engagement, persistence, well-being, and performance. These variables can be associated or linked in a specified design, but they are not interchangeable. A frequency of contact is not identical to felt belonging, and an intervention’s outcome is not itself a direct belonging score.

# R0077 Evidence Record: तनाव-मूल्यांकन — अध्ययन वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Research question
When a study reports “stress,” does it measure exposure to events, a person’s appraisal of recent or anticipated circumstances, physiological signals, or downstream outcomes? This reel uses the angle that **stress is not one directly observable number**.

## Evidence category
This is a measurement-literacy reel based on peer-reviewed psychometric studies and a systematic review. It distinguishes stressors/events, perceived or cognitive appraisal, physiology, outcomes, association, and validation evidence. It does not diagnose a condition, assess the viewer, or imply that a single score is the person’s true stress.

## Measurement map

1. **Exposure/events:** what happened or what situation occurred.
2. **Appraisal/perception:** how a person evaluates recent or anticipated circumstances, often through a questionnaire such as PSS or SAM.
3. **Physiology:** cardiovascular, heart-rate-variability, or other bodily signals collected under a protocol.
4. **Outcomes:** mood, behavior, health, performance, or other defined endpoints.

These layers may relate, but they are not identical. Measurement validity is about whether an instrument behaves as expected for a defined construct and population; it is not permission to make a universal diagnosis or causal claim.

## Claims allowed in the reel

1. The PSS is designed to measure perceptions of recent stress, not simply the number of events.
2. Appraisal instruments can target evaluations of anticipated situations, with multiple subscales or factors.
3. Self-report and physiological measures can show associations in daily-life studies, but not in every analysis and not as proof of identity or causality.
4. A score’s interpretation depends on the instrument, time window, language, sample, task/context, and outcome.
5. Between-person stability and within-person change can coexist in repeated PSS scores.

## Claims excluded

The reel must not say stress is “just in the mind,” that a PSS/SAM score diagnoses anxiety, depression, ADHD, burnout, or a medical disease, that heart-rate variability alone reveals stress, that threat/challenge appraisal is universally accurate, or that any intervention guarantees a result. Do not give personal health advice or ask viewers to self-diagnose.

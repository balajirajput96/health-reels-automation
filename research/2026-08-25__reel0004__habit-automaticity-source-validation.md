# Reel 0004 source validation — habit automaticity and measurement

## Unique angle

Reel 0002 covered time-to-form and stable repetition; Reel 0003 covered if–then planning. Reel 0004 will explain how researchers distinguish frequent behaviour from automaticity, and why a habit measure is not the same thing as a diagnostic truth about a person.

## Verified sources

1. **Wood, W., & Rünger, D. (2016). _Psychology of Habit._ Annual Review of Psychology, 67, 289–314.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417
   - The abstract states that people tend to repeat behaviours in recurring contexts.
   - The review characterizes habits as involving cognitive, motivational, and neurobiological properties.
   - It says habits form as people pursue goals by repeating the same responses in a given context, and that habits and deliberate goal pursuit can guide actions together, with habits often functioning as an efficient default response.
   - Editorial boundary: describe recurring cues and automaticity as mechanisms discussed in a review, not as a claim that all behaviour is unconscious or that goals never matter.

2. **Gardner, B., Abraham, C., Lally, P., & de Bruijn, G.-J. (2012). _Towards parsimony in habit measurement: Testing the convergent and predictive validity of an automaticity subscale of the Self-Report Habit Index._ International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity, 9, 102.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/1479-5868-9-102
   - The open full text defines habits as learned context–behaviour associations elicited automatically when associated contexts are encountered.
   - It reports that the popular 12-item Self-Report Habit Index combines automatic activation, behavioural frequency, and self-identity.
   - The study identified a four-item automaticity-specific subscale, the Self-Report Behavioural Automaticity Index (SRBAI), and reports it was reliable and sensitive to hypothesised habit–behaviour and habit-by-intention relationships in the datasets studied.
   - Editorial boundary: the SRBAI is a research measure, not a clinical diagnosis and not proof that a behaviour is objectively automatic in every context.

3. **PubMed record for Wood & Rünger (2016).** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/
   - Confirms review type, authors, affiliation at the University of Southern California, publication details, DOI, and abstract wording.

## Allowed claims

| Claim | Evidence class | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| Habits are linked to recurring contexts and repeated responses | Review-supported construct | “बार-बार मिलने वाले context में response repeat होने पर habit-like automaticity विकसित हो सकती है।” |
| Frequency alone is not identical to automaticity | Methodological distinction | “किसी काम का frequent होना, अपने-आप में automatic habit का प्रमाण नहीं।” |
| SRBAI isolates automaticity-related self-report items | Peer-reviewed measurement study | “Researchers ने automaticity को अलग से measure करने की कोशिश की है।” |
| A quick self-test proves a true habit | Unsupported | Do not claim a questionnaire settles the matter. |
| Habits eliminate conscious choice | Overclaim | Explicitly state goals and context can still matter. |

## Safety boundary

This is general behavioural-science education. Avoid diagnosing compulsions, addiction, ADHD, depression, or personality from habit language. Do not imply that difficulty changing a behaviour is a moral failure.

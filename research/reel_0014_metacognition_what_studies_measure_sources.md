## Verified source record

### [1] Fleming & Lau (2014), *How to measure metacognition*

- DOI: `10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443
- Open full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443/full
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed methodological review.
- Relevant boundary: the review distinguishes metacognitive sensitivity, metacognitive bias, and metacognitive efficiency. It explains that many experiments compare trial-by-trial confidence with objective accuracy, and that simple correlations can be influenced by response bias and task performance. Type-2 ROC and model-based approaches address some, but not every, confound.

### [2] Katyal & Fleming (2024), *The future of metacognition research: Balancing construct breadth with measurement rigor*

- DOI: `10.1016/j.cortex.2023.11.002`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2023.11.002
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11139654/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed critical review.
- Relevant boundary: metacognition is broader than one confidence score. The review describes older questionnaire approaches to global beliefs and newer task-based measures of momentary confidence, while warning that rigorous confidence metrics do not capture every form of metacognitive knowledge, experience, or control. It also notes limitations and ongoing debate around metrics such as AUROC2 and meta-d′/d′.

### [3] Fleming (2024), *Metacognition and Confidence: A Review and Synthesis*

- DOI: `10.1146/annurev-psych-022423-032425`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-022423-032425
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed Annual Review synthesis.
- Relevant boundary: confidence about one’s own decisions is an inferential computation informed by models of the world and of one’s cognitive system. Confidence can therefore diverge from objective task performance; a confidence report is not a direct readout of a private mental state.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Research window | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Global questionnaire | General beliefs about memory, knowledge, or ability | Self-reported metacognitive knowledge | That the person’s global estimate is objectively accurate |
| Trial-by-trial task | Accuracy plus confidence after each decision | Sensitivity, bias, calibration, or efficiency under that task design | A person’s metacognition in every domain or everyday context |
| Prospective judgment | Prediction of later recall or task success | How predictions relate to later performance | That the prediction is a direct measurement of an inner mechanism |
| Retrospective judgment | Confidence after an answer or memory decision | Whether confidence tracks correct versus incorrect outcomes | That confidence is free from task difficulty, criterion, or reporting effects |
| Computational model | Parameters such as AUROC2 or meta-d′/d′ | A formal summary under stated assumptions | A unique psychological cause or a universal “metacognition meter” |
| Neural or physiological correlate | EEG, fMRI, pupil, or other signal in a task | A signal associated with confidence or monitoring in that context | Literal mind-reading or a diagnosis |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel uses “measurement” in the operational scientific sense: an observable report, task outcome, or model parameter tied to a defined procedure. It must not claim that questionnaires, confidence ratings, brain scans, or computational parameters directly reveal all of someone’s thoughts, intelligence, personality, or clinical state. It must distinguish objective task accuracy from subjective confidence and state that methods sample different windows of metacognition.

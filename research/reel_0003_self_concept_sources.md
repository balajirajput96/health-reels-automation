# Reel 0003 sources — स्व-अवधारणा को study में कैसे मापा जाता है?

## Source 1 — primary peer-reviewed study

Campbell, J. D., Trapnell, P. D., Heine, S. J., Katz, I. M., Lavallee, L. F., & Lehman, D. R. (1996). Self-concept clarity: Measurement, personality correlates, and cultural boundaries. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 70*(1), 141–156. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.1.141. APA record/full-text endpoint: https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/1996-01707-011.html

**Use in reel:** Establishes the self-concept-clarity construct and its measurement context. The construct is treated as clarity/consistency/stability of self-beliefs, not as a complete identity label, self-esteem synonym, or diagnosis.

**Evidence boundary:** The accessible APA endpoint confirmed the citation but did not expose the full article text during this pass. No numerical result from the article is used in the narration.

## Source 2 — peer-reviewed measurement review record

DeMarree, K. G., & Bobrowski, M. E. (2017/2018 record). Structure and validity of self-concept clarity measures. University at Buffalo research record: https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/structure-and-validity-of-self-concept-clarity-measures/

**Use in reel:** The institutional record states that the review documents six measurement strategies, compares their relationships with one another and with self-esteem, and concludes that the measures largely reflect different constructs. It also reports that relationships with previously examined criteria become considerably weaker after controlling for self-esteem, and discusses possible cultural boundedness.

**Evidence boundary:** The reel uses only these attributed measurement cautions. It does not claim that any measure is universally valid, culturally neutral, causal, diagnostic, or predictive of an individual’s life.

## Evidence taxonomy

- **Established research context:** Self-concept clarity is a measurable construct studied with operational instruments.
- **Measurement review finding:** Different self-concept-clarity strategies may reflect different constructs; interpretation depends on the instrument and context.
- **Not claimed:** A score defines a person, diagnoses a condition, guarantees an outcome, or proves causation.

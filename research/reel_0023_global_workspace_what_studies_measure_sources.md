# Reel 0023 — ग्लोबल वर्कस्पेस: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Verified source record

### [1] Mashour, Roelfsema, Changeux & Dehaene (2020), *Conscious Processing and the Global Neuronal Workspace Hypothesis*

- DOI: `10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.026`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.026
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed Neuron review.
- Relevant boundary: the global-neuronal-workspace hypothesis proposes nonlinear amplification and recurrent processing that makes information globally available to local processors. Its empirical signatures are investigated using reportability, masking, attention, EEG/ERP, fMRI, intracranial methods, connectivity, and model predictions. The hypothesis is not a single anatomical “consciousness spot,” and a late signal such as P3 is debated as awareness versus post-perceptual processing.

### [2] Cogitate Consortium (2025), *Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness*

- DOI: `10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed Nature adversarial collaboration.
- Relevant boundary: a preregistered, theory-neutral collaboration tested divergent predictions using fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG in human participants. The study reported findings that aligned with some predictions of both theories while challenging key tenets of both. This demonstrates that theory testing is about preregistered predictions and competing measurements, not a final universal proof from one marker.

### [3] Melloni et al. (2023), *An adversarial collaboration protocol for testing contrasting predictions of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theory*

- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0268577`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0268577
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed PLOS ONE study protocol.
- Relevant boundary: competing theories were compared by preregistered predictions about location, timing, conscious content, and interareal connectivity, using fMRI, M-EEG, and intracranial EEG with built-in replication and open-science procedures. A protocol specifies the test; it is not itself a result proving a theory.

### [4] Gaillard et al. (2009), *Converging Intracranial Markers of Conscious Access*

- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pbio.1000061`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1000061
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed intracranial electrophysiology study.
- Relevant boundary: masked and unmasked words were compared using forced-choice performance and multiple neural measures, including sustained voltage, gamma power, phase synchrony, and Granger-causality estimates. Masking also changes stimulus processing and task demands, so the contrast is not a pure, theory-free readout of consciousness.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement layer | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reportability | Verbal report, button press, visibility or confidence rating | Whether information is available for a defined response | A report-free, complete readout of phenomenal experience |
| Behavioral access | Accuracy, d-prime, reaction time, working-memory or task performance | Access to information under task instructions | That performance and subjective experience are identical |
| Ignition-style neural signature | Sudden late amplification, sustained activity, recurrent or widespread signal | A pattern compatible with a workspace hypothesis in a paradigm | That a single ERP or region is the cause or universal marker of consciousness |
| Connectivity / broadcasting | Long-range synchrony, phase coupling, directed influence, network interaction | Statistical or model-based relationships among areas | Direct proof that information is globally broadcast in the exact theoretical sense |
| Imaging / electrophysiology | fMRI, MEG, EEG, iEEG, ERP, spectral power, decoding | Temporal or spatial correlates under specified conditions | Individual mind-reading or clinical diagnosis |
| Adversarial theory testing | Preregistered predictions, cross-method replication, theory-neutral analysis | Whether specific predictions survive direct comparison | A final proof of an entire theory from one study |
| Computational modeling | Spiking-network simulation, decoding, formal workspace model | Mechanistic or quantitative explanation of observed patterns | Direct measurement of subjective experience or literal neural implementation |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel explains global workspace as a hypothesis about how information may become widely available across neural processors. It keeps theory, observable, and interpretation separate. “Ignition,” broadcasting, P3, frontoparietal activation, long-range synchrony, or decoding are not presented as universal consciousness meters. The 2025 adversarial study is used to illustrate that rigorous theory testing can challenge parts of multiple theories rather than producing a simplistic winner.

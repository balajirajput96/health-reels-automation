## Verified source record

### [1] Angelaki, Gu & DeAngelis (2009), *Multisensory integration: psychophysics, neurophysiology and computation*

- DOI: `10.1016/j.conb.2009.06.008`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2009.06.008
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2749464/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed review.
- Relevant boundary: multisensory integration is studied across psychophysics, single-cell electrophysiology, and computational models. Behavioral cue combination can be compared with reliability-weighted or Bayesian predictions; neural studies can measure firing responses and neural thresholds. A behavioral model fit is not a direct observation of a person’s internal probability calculation.

### [2] Noppeney (2021), *Perceptual Inference, Learning, and Attention in a Multisensory World*

- DOI: `10.1146/annurev-neuro-100120-085519`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-100120-085519
- Full text: https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-neuro-100120-085519
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed Annual Review of Neuroscience synthesis.
- Relevant boundary: studies examine perceptual inference, causal binding, attention, and learning using sensory uncertainty, congruency, temporal/spatial proximity, and computational models. “Bayesian” often describes a model of behavior or inference, not proof that the brain literally executes a specific algorithm in every condition.

### [3] Jagini (2021), *Temporal Binding in Multisensory and Motor-Sensory Contexts: Toward a Unified Model*

- DOI: `10.3389/fnhum.2021.629437`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.629437
- Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.629437/full
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed mini-review.
- Relevant boundary: temporal binding studies measure subjective timing judgments, temporal-binding windows, illusion susceptibility, and effects of spatial/temporal proximity, semantic congruency, attention, and learned associations. The review notes variability across stimulus combinations, paradigms, ages, and cognitive factors.

### [4] Klaffehn, Sellmann, Kirsch, Kunde & Pfister (2021), *Temporal binding as multisensory integration: Manipulating perceptual certainty of actions and their effects*

- DOI: `10.3758/s13414-021-02314-0`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-021-02314-0
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8550101/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed experimental study.
- Relevant boundary: the study manipulated certainty of action and auditory-effect timing and measured temporal judgments and estimation variance. A shift in perceived timing is a behavioral effect; it is not direct proof of agency, consciousness, or a particular neural mechanism.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement layer | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psychophysics | Detection accuracy, discrimination threshold, response bias, reaction time | Whether combined cues change perceptual performance | That the participant consciously “calculates Bayes” or that integration occurs in one specific brain area |
| Cue weighting | Bias and precision when unimodal and bimodal cues vary in reliability | Whether behavior is consistent with reliability-weighted combination | That the fitted weights are literal synaptic weights or universal across contexts |
| Temporal binding | Subjective timing estimates, estimation error, temporal-binding window | Perceived attraction or fusion of events in time | An implicit diagnosis, fixed agency trait, or a unique neural cause |
| Illusions | McGurk, ventriloquist, double-flash, rubber-hand or related perceptual reports | How conflicting or congruent cues alter perception | That an illusion reveals a person’s character or disorder |
| Electrophysiology | Single-unit firing, EEG/ERP timing, neural tuning, source estimates | Neural responses correlated with sensory combination | Direct access to a private percept or proof of a single computational algorithm |
| Neuroimaging | fMRI, PET, MEG or other regional/network signals | Correlates of multisensory processing | Cell-level causality, exact subjective experience, or individual diagnosis |
| Computational model | Bayesian causal inference, reliability-weighted, drift-diffusion, or related model fit | Quantitative account that may explain observed data | Confirmation that the brain uses the model literally in every situation |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel explains that sensory integration is operationalized through defined tasks and observables: accuracy, thresholds, response bias, reaction time, temporal judgments, neural signals, and model fits. It does not claim that a single illusion measures intelligence, attention disorder, autism, agency, or personality. It distinguishes a behavioral effect from a proposed neural or computational explanation and avoids individual-level diagnosis.

# Reel 0049 evidence record: Interleaving — अध्ययन वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं

## Claim boundary

This reel explains how interleaving studies compare blocked and mixed sequences, and why practice difficulty, delayed tests, category similarity, and transfer must be read separately. It does not recommend a universal study schedule, promise grades, diagnose ability, or provide treatment.

## Source 1 — authentic course study

Samani, J., and Pan, S. C. (2021). *Interleaved practice enhances memory and problem-solving ability in undergraduate physics*. NPJ Science of Learning, 6, 32. DOI: [10.1038/s41539-021-00110-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00110-x). In an eight-week undergraduate physics course, 350 students completed the same total homework problems under blocked or interleaved arrangements. Interleaved homework was more difficult and had lower practice accuracy, but surprise criterial tests with novel, more challenging problems favored interleaving. The published Stage 1 and Stage 2 standardized effects were d = 0.40 and d = 0.91. These are outcomes in that course and task, not guarantees for every learner.

## Source 2 — immediate versus delayed category transfer

Carvalho, P. F., and Goldstone, R. L. (2014). *Effects of interleaved and blocked study on delayed test of category learning generalization*. Frontiers in Psychology, 5, 936. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00936](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00936). Participants learned high-similarity or low-similarity categories with blocked or interleaved schedules and completed immediate and approximately 24-hour delayed transfer tests. The study reported an interaction between schedule and category type, while the longer retention interval did not modulate that interaction. The authors discuss cross-category comparison as a key explanation rather than treating interleaving as merely a spacing manipulation.

## Source 3 — systematic review

Firth, J., Rivers, I., Boyle, J., and colleagues (2021). *A systematic review of interleaving as a concept learning strategy*. Review of Education, 9(2), 642–684. DOI: [10.1002/rev3.3266](https://doi.org/10.1002/rev3.3266). The review included 26 studies; 17 studies with 32 constituent datasets formed the meta-analysis. It reported benefits up to Hedges’ g = 0.65 for memory of studied items and up to g = 0.66 for transfer to novel items, with greatest usefulness when differences between categories were subtle. It also reported that benefits extended to delayed tests, while noting that the literature was dominated by laboratory studies of university undergraduates.

## Operational measures to explain

- **Blocked practice:** examples or problems from one category appear together.
- **Interleaved practice:** examples or problems from different categories alternate.
- **Practice accuracy and judgment of learning:** performance and self-report during training; these can make interleaving feel worse.
- **Immediate test:** a test soon after training.
- **Delayed retention:** a test after a time interval.
- **Transfer:** performance on novel items or new problems, not merely repeating studied examples.
- **Category similarity and task structure:** conditions that can change which sequence is useful.

## Safety and scientific limits

An association or advantage observed in a particular experiment is not a personal guarantee. Interleaving is not the same as distributing all repetitions over days, and a study outcome does not establish a single best timetable for an individual. The reel is educational content only; it is not medical advice, psychological diagnosis, or treatment.

# Reel 0033 source-retrieval findings

1. Hoemann et al. (2023), *Affective Science*, DOI 10.1007/s42761-023-00185-2, is available through PMC. The paper defines emotional granularity or emotion differentiation as differentiated and nuanced emotional experiences. Across three studies it used experience-sampling or diary data, repeated emotion-intensity ratings, and natural-language descriptions of everyday contexts and activities. It reports that emotional granularity is not the same as merely using specific emotion words; the measure is based on patterns of differentiated ratings across contexts. This supports the reel’s distinction between a label and a broader estimate of context-specific experience.

2. Hoemann, Barrett, and Quigley (2021), *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.704125, describes emotional granularity as typically measured through experience sampling: participants report emotion intensities multiple times per day across multiple days. The paper discusses aggregate and within-person estimates and combines experience sampling with peripheral physiological monitoring and end-of-day diaries. It reports that methodological factors such as the number of prompts can influence estimates, so the reel will not present granularity as a fixed universal trait.

3. Tan, Wachsmuth, and Tugade (2022), *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.715966, reviews positive emotional granularity and describes experience sampling, repeated ratings, and differentiation indices based on the interrelatedness of same-valence emotion terms. It also discusses vignette-based self-report measures such as the Differentiation of Positive Emotion Scale. The reel uses this only to show that different operationalizations exist.

4. Wilson-Mendenhall and Dunne (2021), *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.703658, is a perspective on cultivating emotional granularity. It presents theory and hypotheses about language, context, concepts, and contemplative practices; it is not treated as proof that any practice improves an individual’s granularity. The reel clearly separates established measurement approaches from theoretical or philosophical proposals.

The retrieved sources support wording such as “measured,” “estimated,” “reported,” “associated,” and “context-specific.” They do not justify diagnosing viewers, inferring private feelings from a score, or promising health outcomes.

## Evidence distinctions

- **Trait self-report:** typical tendency या usual response के बारे में retrospective/global questionnaire estimate।
- **Momentary self-report:** prompt के समय current episode, context और reported intensity/process का snapshot।
- **Content:** feelings, problems, upsetting memories या negative future situations जैसे reported topics।
- **Process:** repetitiveness, intrusiveness, difficulty disengaging, burden या duration जैसे reported features।
- **Construct choice:** depressive rumination, broader repetitive negative thinking, और processing-mode measures related लेकिन identical नहीं।
- **Association:** rumination score का mood, stress या अन्य measure से संबंध causation या diagnosis सिद्ध नहीं करता।
- **Interpretation limit:** scale का score व्यक्ति के हर विचार, हर समय, या clinical state का complete record नहीं है।

## Four-scene visual plan

### Scene 1 — Trait questionnaire window

A vertical conceptual card shows a neutral person silhouette, a repeated-thought loop icon, and a rating scale labelled only with abstract markers. The visual communicates a global self-report without showing clinical labels or readable private thoughts.

### Scene 2 — Smartphone experience sampling

A phone sends several spaced prompts along a day timeline. Each prompt connects to small context icons for feelings, problems, past memories, and future concerns. A second layer shows changing intensity dots, emphasizing snapshots and fluctuations rather than continuous recording.

### Scene 3 — Content versus process

Two adjacent panels compare topic icons with process icons: a memory card, a problem symbol, repeating arrows, an interruption symbol, and a pause/stuck marker. The scene shows that researchers can ask about what thoughts concern and how the thinking unfolds.

### Scene 4 — Scale boundary and guardrail

Three differently shaped measurement cards feed into a bounded box labelled “reported features in a defined window”. A stop symbol blocks a larger label reading “diagnosis / complete mind record”.

## Safety and evidence boundary

Do not diagnose depression, anxiety, suicidality, or any other condition from rumination language, a questionnaire score, or a phone-based report. Do not give treatment or coping prescriptions. Do not claim rumination is always harmful, that reflection is always helpful, or that an association proves causation. Do not equate a score with a viewer’s complete inner life.

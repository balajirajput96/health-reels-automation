# R0075 Evidence Record: Self-Determination — What Studies Measure

## Research question and unique angle

This reel asks what Self-Determination Theory studies actually measure when they discuss autonomy and motivation. The angle is to separate experienced psychological needs, motivational regulation, self-report scales, and observed outcomes rather than presenting SDT as a device that reads a person’s private motive.

## Evidence table

| Construct or outcome | Typical operationalization | What it can indicate | Boundary on interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | Reported sense of volition or internal causality, often through a questionnaire | Whether an activity or setting feels self-endorsed | A context-specific experience report, not total freedom or diagnosis |
| Competence | Reported mastery, efficacy, or effectiveness in a domain | Experienced capability or effectance | Not proof of objective skill or guaranteed performance |
| Relatedness | Reported connection, belonging, or social support | Experienced connection in a relationship or setting | Domain-bound and not a complete social profile |
| Intrinsic motivation | Interest and enjoyment in doing an activity for its own sake | Reported inherent interest in a specific activity | Not a permanent trait or the only form of motivation |
| Autonomous/controlled regulation | Reasons or regulatory styles behind an activity | Whether action feels more self-endorsed or pressured | Self-report and theory-based categories, not direct mind-reading |
| Observed outcomes | Engagement, persistence, performance, creativity, well-being, or participation | Recorded behavior or outcome in a defined context | Separate from need ratings and not a guaranteed individual forecast |

## Source 1 — official theory overview

Center for Self-Determination Theory. “Theory.” https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/

The official overview presents SDT as a broad framework for human motivation and personality. It identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness as basic psychological needs and says supports for these experiences are argued to foster more volitional forms of motivation and engagement, including performance, persistence, and creativity. Safe conclusion: these are theory constructs about experienced needs and motivation in context, not a direct meter of an individual’s complete inner state.

## Source 2 — measurement and well-being paper

Martela, F., Ryan, R. M., & Steger, M. F. (2021). “In selecting measures for a comprehensive assessment of well-being, it is essential to include indicators of psychological need satisfaction.” *Preventive Medicine Reports*, 23, 101518. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8264600/

The paper argues that well-being assessment should include indicators of autonomy, competence, and relatedness alongside other well-being dimensions. It describes autonomy as volition/internal causality, competence as mastery/effectance/efficacy, and relatedness as connection with others. Safe conclusion: instruments can capture reported need satisfaction as one dimension of well-being in a specified setting; they do not diagnose or replace broader outcomes.

## Source 3 — SDT motivation review

Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2020). “Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation from a self-determination theory perspective: Definitions, theory, practices, and future directions.” *Contemporary Educational Psychology*, 61, 101860. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2020.101860

The review describes SDT as a framework for intrinsic motivation, autonomous extrinsic motivation, and psychological wellness. It defines intrinsic motivation as doing activities for inherent interest or enjoyment and discusses more autonomous forms of extrinsic motivation. It links these forms to educational outcomes and to supports for autonomy, competence, and relatedness across specified educational levels and cultural contexts. Safe conclusion: motivational regulation types and outcomes are differentiated constructs, not one universal motivation score.

## Source 4 — official Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction Scales documentation

Center for Self-Determination Theory. “Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction Scales.” https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/basic-psychological-need-satisfaction-scales/

The official scale documentation says the general-life Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction Scale has 21 items assessing competence, autonomy, and relatedness. It also lists domain-specific forms, including a 9-item relationship-domain scale and a 21-item work-domain scale. Safe conclusion: SDT measurement is item-based and can be domain-specific; general-life, work, and relationship scores are not interchangeable, and a scale captures reported need satisfaction rather than direct private motives.

## Evidence-bounded conclusion

SDT research may measure experienced autonomy, competence, and relatedness, along with intrinsic or autonomous versus controlled reasons for acting. It commonly uses questionnaires or domain-specific scales and may relate those measures to engagement, persistence, performance, creativity, or well-being. Need satisfaction, motivational regulation, and observed outcomes are separate evidence categories. Results are theory- and context-bound, not direct mind-reading or guaranteed individual predictions.

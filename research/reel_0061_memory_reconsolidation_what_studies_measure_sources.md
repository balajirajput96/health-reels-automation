# Reel 0061 — Memory reconsolidation: what studies measure

## Research question

When a study says a memory was “reconsolidated” or “updated,” what was actually observed? This record separates reactivation, prediction error, behavioral memory change, expectancy or confidence ratings, physiological/neural correlates, and mechanistic interpretation.

## Operational categories

**Reactivation/reminder:** A cue or procedure intended to retrieve an established memory. It is an experimental manipulation, not itself evidence that the memory entered a labile state.

**Prediction error or mismatch:** A difference between expected and observed outcomes, sometimes measured with expectancy ratings or task outcomes. It is a candidate boundary condition, not a universal switch.

**Behavioral updating:** Later recall, recognition, intrusion, sequence accuracy, or conditioned response after reactivation and new information/interference. These are observable task outputs and may have more than one explanation.

**Expectancy, confidence, and self-report:** Ratings can index subjective expectation or memory experience. They are data about reports or beliefs, not direct access to the underlying memory representation.

**Neural or physiological correlates:** fMRI, hippocampal activity, autonomic responses, or other signals can be associated with retrieval or updating conditions. They are correlates/proxies and cannot directly read a memory’s content or factual truth.

**Mechanistic inference:** Reconsolidation is a biological and computational interpretation of a pattern involving reactivation, boundary conditions, interference, and later memory expression. Human evidence is active and contested; a behavioral change alone does not uniquely identify the mechanism.

## Safe conclusion

Reconsolidation studies generally measure what happens to later task performance or reported memory after a defined reminder/reactivation and an intervention or new learning episode. They may also measure expectancy ratings, conditioned responses, neural activity, or model-derived quantities. These outcomes can inform theories of memory updating, but they do not prove that a memory was erased, reveal its complete content, or guarantee a clinical or personal result.

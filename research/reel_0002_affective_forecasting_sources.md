# Reel 0002 sources — affective forecasting measurement

## Evidence classification

This reel uses one verified primary empirical study and one peer-reviewed review for terminology and measurement framing. The primary study is the anchor for the claim about overestimated duration in its tested situations. No universal population claim or exact statistic is used.

## Primary empirical study

Gilbert, D. T., Pinel, E. C., Wilson, T. D., Blumberg, S. J., & Wheatley, T. P. (1998). Immune neglect: A source of durability bias in affective forecasting. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 75*(3), 617–638. PubMed PMID: 9781405. DOI: 10.1037//0022-3514.75.3.617. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9781405/

The PubMed abstract states that six studies examined forecasts of how long negative affect would last across several negative events and compared those forecasts with later experienced affect. In the tested contexts, participants overestimated the duration of their affective reactions and did not distinguish situations in which coping mechanisms were likely to operate. The reel uses cautious wording: “उन tested situations में duration का अनुमान अक्सर ज़्यादा था।” It does not claim that all people always mispredict emotions.

## Peer-reviewed review

Wilson, T. D., & Gilbert, D. T. (2005). Affective forecasting: Knowing what to want. *Review of General Psychology, 9*(3), 131–155. PDF: https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/WILSON%20%26%20GILBERT%20(2005).pdf

The opened review defines affective forecasts as predictions about emotional reactions to future events and discusses predicted intensity and duration, impact bias, focalism, and coping/adaptation. It is used for vocabulary and conceptual framing, not as a substitute for the primary study.

## Limits and non-claims

Affective forecasting studies do not read minds; they compare forecast reports with later self-reports or other operational measures. The exact measure can be intensity, duration, valence, specific emotions, or overall mood. Forecast accuracy depends on the event, timing, population, measure, and later experience. This reel does not provide diagnosis, treatment, therapy, a guaranteed decision aid, or personalized advice.

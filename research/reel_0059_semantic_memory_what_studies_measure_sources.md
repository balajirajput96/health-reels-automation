# Reel 0059 — Semantic memory: what studies measure

## Research question

When a study says it measured semantic memory, did it measure category fluency, naming, factual knowledge, concept judgments, metacognitive confidence, neural correlates, or a computational model? This record keeps those categories distinct.

## Operational categories

**Category fluency:** generating as many valid examples as possible from a category in a fixed time. Count, order, clustering, and linguistic features are observable outputs, but the task also recruits search, speed, language production, and executive control.

**Naming and factual/concept judgments:** identifying an object, producing a label, matching a definition, or answering a knowledge question. Accuracy depends on the item, wording, language, familiarity, education, retrieval conditions, and scoring.

**Self-report/metacognition:** feeling-of-knowing, confidence, or retrospective judgments about one’s answer. These describe subjective monitoring and can be useful without guaranteeing correctness.

**Neural/model-derived signals:** ERP or other brain measures and computational representations can help study mechanisms or hypotheses. They are proxies or model outputs tied to tasks and assumptions, not direct access to a complete semantic store.

## Safe conclusion

Semantic-memory studies usually measure task-defined behavior such as category generation, naming, factual accuracy, concept similarity, confidence, or response time. They may also test neural correlates or computational models. These measures can inform different parts of semantic knowledge, but none alone is a complete readout of a person’s knowledge, intelligence, language ability, or cognitive health.

## Safety and editorial boundaries

This is public education, not diagnosis, treatment, prescription, or individualized cognitive assessment. Do not infer dementia, intelligence, deception, or a person’s mental state from one fluency score, knowledge answer, confidence judgment, ERP signal, or model output. No personal prediction or guarantee is made.

## Evidence boundary

Reel 0003’s planned angle is “what studies measure,” not a claim that high or low self-concept clarity causes life outcomes. The script should distinguish self-report scales, card-sort/structure tasks, and indirect measures; avoid diagnosis, treatment, personality labeling, or claims that a single score defines a person.

The University at Buffalo research record for DeMarree & Bobrowski’s “Structure and validity of self-concept clarity measures” states that the review documents six measurement strategies, compares their relationships with one another and with self-esteem, and concludes that the measures largely reflect different constructs. It also reports weaker relationships with previously studied criteria once self-esteem is statistically controlled, and notes possible cultural boundedness of existing assessment strategies: https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/structure-and-validity-of-self-concept-clarity-measures/

## Safe reel claim

A defensible explainer can say that researchers use different operationalizations—such as self-report scales and other structure/validity strategies—and that a score is an instrument-specific measure, not a complete definition of a person. Avoid saying one measure is universally best, or that self-concept clarity itself causes mental-health or life outcomes.

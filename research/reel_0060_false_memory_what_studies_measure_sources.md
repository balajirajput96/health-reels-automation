# Reel 0060 — False memory: what studies measure

## Research question

When a study reports a false memory, is it measuring false recall, false recognition, source-monitoring error, misinformation acceptance, imagination inflation, confidence, or a neural/model-derived correlate? This record keeps those outcomes distinct.

## Operational categories

**False recall:** reporting an item or detail that was not presented, often scored as an intrusion or critical-lure response. It is an observable error under a defined study and test procedure.

**False recognition:** endorsing a lure or misleading detail as “old” or previously seen. Signal-detection indices can summarize discrimination and response tendencies, but they remain task-specific.

**Source monitoring:** assigning a memory to the wrong origin, such as confusing the original event with post-event misinformation, imagination, or a related word list.

**Suggestion and imagination paradigms:** misinformation introduces misleading post-event information; imagination inflation asks whether imagining an event changes later reports. These are different manipulations and should not be conflated with DRM semantic lures.

**Confidence and subjective reports:** confidence, vividness, or belief ratings describe a participant’s monitoring or experience. High confidence does not by itself prove accuracy, and false remembering is not the same as intentional lying.

**Neural/model-derived measures:** fMRI, ERP, pupil, or other physiological signals can be studied as correlates or mechanism-related proxies. They are not mind-reading and do not directly reveal the truth of an autobiographical report.

## Safe conclusion

False-memory research generally measures errors under controlled recall, recognition, source-monitoring, misinformation, DRM, or imagination procedures. Studies may also collect confidence, belief, physiological, neural, or model-derived variables. These measures illuminate different pathways to memory distortion but do not establish deception, a permanent susceptibility trait, or the factual truth of a person’s life story from one score.

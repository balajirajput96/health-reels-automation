# Reel 0029 browser findings

On 2026-08-25, two public sources were opened.

1. Mertens et al., “The effectiveness of nudging: A meta-analysis of choice architecture interventions across behavioral domains” (*PNAS*, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2107346118), was extracted from a public article page. The meta-analysis pooled more than 200 studies and over 440 effect sizes, measuring behavior change across choice-architecture techniques and domains. It reports an overall small-to-medium average effect, variation by technique and domain, and moderate publication bias toward positive results. The reel will use this to emphasize that effects are averages across designs, not guarantees for every person or setting.

2. Chandrashekar and Fillon, “Framing the Default: Influence of Choosing Versus Rejecting Frame on Default Effects” (*Experimental Psychology*, DOI 10.1027/1618-3169/a000617; PMC11612645), was read from public PMC full text. The study involved 1,072 participants and hypothetical work and health scenarios. It found framing effects in both domains, while default effects were limited in work and absent in the health domain in that study. The reel will describe this as a context-specific comparison of manipulated presentation and recorded choices, not as proof that defaults never matter.

The PNAS page displayed a captcha interstitial after extraction, but its article title, DOI, significance, abstract, and methodological summary were available in the extracted content. No claim beyond that extracted content was used.

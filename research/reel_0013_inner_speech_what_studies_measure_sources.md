# Reel 0013 source record: inner speech — what studies actually measure

## Evidence-bounded angle

Inner speech is a private experience of language or verbal thinking without audible speech. Because it has no reliable outward marker, studies measure it indirectly through generalized questionnaires, momentary experience sampling, introspective interviews, overt private speech, verbal-interference tasks, behavioral language tasks, electromyography, brain imaging, and related methods. These methods do not all measure the same construct, and a task-instructed inner-speech episode may differ from spontaneous everyday inner speech.

## Verified sources

### 1. Alderson-Day & Fernyhough (2015), questionnaire versus experience sampling

- Citation: Ben Alderson-Day and Charles Fernyhough. “Relations among questionnaire and experience sampling measures of inner speech: a smartphone app study.” *Frontiers in Psychology*, 6, 517 (2015).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00517
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4410513/
- Identifiers: PMCID: PMC4410513; PMID: 25964773.
- Evidence class: peer-reviewed empirical measurement study.
- What it measured: 51 university students completed the Varieties of Inner Speech Questionnaire and received random smartphone prompts over two weeks to report current inner-speech characteristics. The study compared generalized endorsement with momentary reports.
- What it supports: generalized questionnaire scores and randomly sampled reports did not align equally across all subscales; momentary endorsements were lower than generalized endorsements in this sample and design. This supports caution about treating a broad self-estimate as a direct frequency meter.
- Boundary: the sample was small and university-based, response rates varied, and the findings do not establish a universal inner-speech frequency for all people.

### 2. Alderson-Day & Fernyhough (2015), methodological and theoretical review

- Citation: Ben Alderson-Day and Charles Fernyhough. “Inner Speech: Development, Cognitive Functions, Phenomenology, and Neurobiology.” *Psychological Bulletin*, 141(5), 931–965 (2015).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000021
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/
- Identifiers: PMCID: PMC4538954; PMID: 26011789.
- Evidence class: peer-reviewed review.
- What it supports: inner speech is a multifaceted phenomenon that is difficult to observe directly. The review discusses questionnaires, experience sampling, descriptive experience sampling, private speech, verbal interference, working-memory paradigms, neuropsychological tasks, electromyography, and neuroimaging as indirect or task-based approaches.
- Boundary: a neural correlate or performance effect is not identical to subjective inner speech itself; methods can capture different forms, functions, or proxies.

### 3. Fernyhough & Borghi (2023), contemporary review of methods and neural interpretation

- Citation: Charles Fernyhough and Anna M. Borghi. “Inner speech as language process and cognitive tool.” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 27(12), 1180–1193 (2023).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2023.08.014
- Open article: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364661323002103
- Evidence class: peer-reviewed contemporary review.
- What it supports: self-report questionnaires, experience sampling, articulatory suppression, verbal language tasks, brain imaging, neurostimulation, EMG, and emerging neural-decoding work make inner speech more tractable to study. The review stresses distinctions between covert inner speech and overt private speech and cautions that instructed experimental inner speech may not match spontaneous inner speech.
- Boundary: brain activity and decoding signals provide correlates or task-specific information; they do not amount to a general-purpose readout of a person’s private thoughts.

## Measurement distinctions for the reel

A questionnaire measures endorsed beliefs about one’s typical inner speech, including qualities such as dialogue, evaluation, condensation, or other voices. Experience sampling asks what is happening at randomly selected moments and can reduce the need for a broad retrospective estimate, though it depends on compliance and momentary reporting. Descriptive interviews can enrich phenomenological detail but are interpretive and resource-intensive.

Behavioral methods can test consequences or interference: articulatory suppression may disrupt verbal rehearsal, and language tasks can probe phonological or lexical processing. EMG, EEG, fMRI, or other neural methods measure bodily or neural signals during specified tasks. These signals can be related to inner speech, but they are not the subjective experience itself.

A safe conclusion is that inner-speech research combines multiple imperfect windows. The method determines which aspect is sampled: remembered self-report, momentary experience, observable private speech, task performance, peripheral muscle activity, or neural correlates. No single method provides a universal inner-speech meter or literal mind-reading.

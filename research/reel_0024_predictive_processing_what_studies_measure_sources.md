# Reel 0024 — प्रेडिक्टिव प्रोसेसिंग: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Verified source record

### [1] Walsh, McGovern, Clark & O’Connell (2020), *Evaluating the neurophysiological evidence for predictive processing as a model of perception*

- DOI: `10.1111/nyas.14321`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.14321
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187369/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed methodological review.
- Relevant boundary: predictive processing models describe hierarchical inference in which predictions and prediction errors are exchanged. The review emphasizes that many observations, such as repetition suppression or omission responses, can have alternative explanations, and that noninvasive population measures do not directly identify distinct prediction and error units.

### [2] Tscshantz, Millidge, Seth & Buckley (2023), *Hybrid predictive coding: Inferring, fast and slow*

- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011280`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011280
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10395865/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed computational-model paper.
- Relevant boundary: a hybrid model combines fast, feedforward amortized inference with slower iterative recurrent inference. The model provides testable computational proposals, not a direct measurement of human subjective experience or proof that the biological brain implements the exact architecture.

### [3] Ficco et al. (2021), *Disentangling predictive processing in the brain: a meta-analytic study in favour of a predictive network*

- DOI: `10.1038/s41598-021-95603-5`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-95603-5
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed coordinate-based meta-analysis and functional-connectivity analysis.
- Relevant boundary: across 70 selected experiments, prediction-violation and general-prediction analyses converged in some frontal, insular, and parietal regions, while prediction-encoding convergence was more heterogeneous. The reported predictive network overlaps with attention and task-execution networks, and the study did not provide a universal separation of error and prediction processing.

### [4] Caucheteux, Gramfort & King (2023), *Evidence of a predictive coding hierarchy in the human brain listening to speech*

- DOI: `10.1038/s41562-022-01516-2`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01516-2
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed human fMRI/model-mapping experiment.
- Relevant boundary: fMRI responses from 304 listeners hearing stories were compared with representations from language models. Adding long-range and hierarchical model representations improved brain mapping, with different cortical regions associated with different forecast ranges and representational levels. A better model-to-brain correlation is evidence about correspondence, not proof that the brain uses the exact model or that a single number is a person’s prediction-error level.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement layer | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Behavioral expectation | Detection accuracy, reaction time, perceptual reports, learning or surprise responses | How prior regularities influence behavior under a task | That all behavior is generated by predictive coding or that a subjective belief was directly observed |
| Prediction violation | Responses to unexpected, omitted, or deviant stimuli | A measurable mismatch or expectation-related effect in a defined paradigm | A pure prediction-error neural signal, because surprise, attention, adaptation, and arousal can contribute |
| Repetition / expectation suppression | Smaller or larger EEG, MEG, fMRI, or neuronal responses to repeated or expected items | Neural modulation associated with repetition or expectation | That the modulation uniquely identifies prediction error rather than adaptation or another mechanism |
| Neural population signal | EEG/MEG power or timing, fMRI BOLD, intracranial activity, laminar or single-unit recordings | Timing, location, and population-level correlates of modeled conditions | Direct access to predictions, precision, individual priors, or private experience |
| Connectivity | Functional correlations, phase coupling, directed or model-derived interactions | Relationships among regions under specified conditions | Anatomical causation, a unique predictive network, or a literal top-down error circuit |
| Computational model fit | Bayesian, predictive-coding, neural-network, or language-model correspondence | A quantitative way to compare hypotheses and forecast data | Proof that the biological brain implements that exact model |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel presents predictive processing as a family of models and hypotheses about perception. It separates the observable manipulation from the interpretation. Prediction error is not treated as a synonym for generic surprise, nor are fMRI/EEG patterns treated as direct readouts of beliefs or individual mental states. Predictive-processing language is not used to diagnose a person, explain every symptom, or promise a guaranteed outcome.

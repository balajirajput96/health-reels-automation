

## Source-level retrieval findings

Smith et al. (2014), *Consciousness and Cognition*, DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2014.05.003, was opened in PMC. The abstract reports three experiments on non-focal prospective-memory tasks. Implementation intentions improved prospective-memory performance, and the third experiment also found improved recall of the appropriate action. The paper reports that improvement could involve greater allocation of conscious resources to the prospective-memory task, with a cost to the ongoing task, and it uses a multinomial model to distinguish cognitive processes such as preparatory attention, target recognition, and action retrieval. This supports treating cue detection, action recall, and ongoing-task performance as separate measurements.

Koo, Neumann, Ownsworth, and Shum (2022), *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.905860, was opened in the publisher full text. The study compared young and older adults in conventional-instruction and implementation-intention conditions across laboratory and ecological prospective-memory tasks. Prospective memory includes remembering that something must be done and remembering what must be done. Time-based and event-based tasks differ, and event-based cues can be focal or non-focal to the ongoing task. The reported overall facilitation was not statistically significant; a single-session strategy showed only a trend in one first-session older-adult laboratory condition, while generalization and longer-term ecological transfer were not significant. This is a direct boundary against presenting cue-linked laboratory performance as a guaranteed everyday habit.

Together with the previously verified implementation-intention reviews and meta-analysis, these sources support a reel about cue competition and measurement layers: external-cue detection, prospective versus retrospective memory, action initiation, ongoing-task interference, follow-up behavior, and transfer. The reel will not claim that an if-then plan automatically wins every competition or permanently changes behavior.

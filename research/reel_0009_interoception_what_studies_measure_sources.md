# Reel 0009 source record: interoception — what studies actually measure

## Evidence-bounded angle

Interoception is not a single personal ability score. Studies can target different bodily channels and different constructs: objectively measured performance, self-reported beliefs or attention, and the correspondence between performance and confidence. Cardiac tasks are common, but task validity, reliability, physiological state, and non-interoceptive strategies matter. The reel must not diagnose, promise health outcomes, or treat a heartbeat-counting score as a complete measure of bodily awareness.

## Verified sources

### 1. Kleckner et al. (2015), methodological recommendations

- Citation: Ian R. Kleckner, Jolie Baumann Wormwood, W. Kyle Simmons, Lisa Feldman Barrett, and Karen S. Quigley. “Methodological Recommendations for a Heartbeat Detection-Based Measure of Interoceptive Sensitivity.” *Psychophysiology* 52(11), 1432–1440 (2015).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12503
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4821012/
- Identifier: PMCID: PMC4821012; PMID: 26265009.
- Evidence class: methodological study with reliability and power simulations.
- What it supports: heartbeat-detection tasks attempt to measure the ability to detect cardiac sensations; at least 40–60 trials were needed in their study to yield sufficient reliability for the task outcome, although the optimal number can vary by design. The paper distinguishes heartbeat detection from the mental tracking method and notes that the latter can be influenced by beliefs about typical heart rate rather than online beat-to-beat detection.
- Boundary: this is a task/reliability recommendation, not a universal cutoff for every interoception study and not proof that a score captures all bodily awareness.

### 2. Murphy, Catmur, and Bird (2019), construct classification

- Citation: Jennifer Murphy, Caroline Catmur, and Geoffrey Bird. “Classifying individual differences in interoception: Implications for the measurement of interoceptive awareness.” *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 26(5), 1467–1471 (2019).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-019-01632-7
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797703/
- Identifier: PMCID: PMC6797703; PMID: 31270764.
- Evidence class: theoretical and measurement framework.
- What it supports: the familiar distinction between interoceptive accuracy, interoceptive sensibility, and interoceptive awareness; the paper proposes also separating accuracy from attention and objective measurement from self-report in a 2×2 framework. Accuracy is objective performance relative to a bodily signal; sensibility concerns self-reported beliefs; awareness is a metacognitive correspondence, dependent on how both components are measured.
- Boundary: this is a conceptual framework and measurement critique; it does not justify reducing interoception to one questionnaire or one heartbeat task.

### 3. Ponzo et al. (2021), ecological cardiac task

- Citation: Sonia Ponzo, Davide Morelli, Chatrin Suksasilp, Massimo Cairo, and David Plans. “Measuring Interoception: The CARdiac Elevation Detection Task.” *Frontiers in Psychology* 12 (2021), article 712896.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.712896
- Open full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.712896/full
- Evidence class: original task-development study.
- What it supports: the CARED task combined wearable heart-rate measurement with repeated real-world prompts asking whether heart rate was higher than usual, plus confidence and activity reports. The published study had a final sample of 30 healthy participants. It illustrates that measurement can be ecological and can separately examine objective performance, confidence, and attention-related context.
- Boundary: small, exploratory task-development study; it does not establish a universal interoceptive classifier or clinical conclusion.

### 4. Desmedt et al. (2023), systematic review of newer tasks

- Citation: Olivier Desmedt, Olivier Luminet, Marta Walentynowicz, and Olivier Corneille. “The new measures of interoceptive accuracy: A systematic review and assessment.” *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 153 (2023), article 105388.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105388
- Abstract and article preview: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763423003573
- Evidence class: systematic review.
- What it supports: newer cardiac, respiratory, and gastrointestinal tasks have been developed to address limitations of classical measures, but many still have important limitations; high validity may be difficult to achieve, and interoceptive accuracy may not be the only or most critical dimension for theory.
- Boundary: systematic review conclusion about measurement validity, not a claim that interoception is unmeasurable.

## Planned claim boundaries

- Directly observed: recorded heartbeats or heart-rate signals, participant choices/counts/detections, confidence ratings, and task performance.
- Inferred or constructed: accuracy scores, modelled correspondence, and awareness/insight indices.
- Self-report: beliefs, attention, or perceived bodily awareness, which are not interchangeable with objective accuracy.
- Safe conclusion: ask what the study measured, how reliable and valid the task was, and whether the result concerns accuracy, attention, self-report, or metacognitive correspondence.
- Exclusions: no diagnosis, treatment, personal clinical advice, guaranteed improvement, or claim that a single heartbeat task reveals a person’s whole mind-body awareness.

# Reel 0051 evidence record: फीडबैक से सीखना — studies क्या मापते हैं

## Claim boundary

This reel explains why feedback timing and feedback content cannot be judged from one short-term score or one learner preference. It distinguishes immediate performance, delayed retention, transfer, motivation, cognitive-load reports, and student perceptions. It does not prescribe one universally best feedback schedule, diagnose learning ability, or provide treatment.

## Source 1 — classroom feedback timing and transfer

Mullet, H. G., Butler, A. C., Verdin, B., von Borries, R., and Marsh, E. J. (2014). *Delaying feedback promotes transfer of knowledge despite student preferences to receive feedback immediately*. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition, 3(3), 222–229. DOI: [10.1016/j.jarmac.2014.05.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2014.05.001). In two experiments in an upper-level college engineering course, students practiced applying complex concepts in weekly homework and received feedback either immediately after the deadline or one week later. Delayed feedback was followed by better performance on later course exams containing new problems about the same concepts, while students reported preferring immediate feedback. This supports separating perceived usefulness from measured transfer in this specific setting.

## Source 2 — feedback timing and content in multimedia learning

Taxipulati, S. and Lu, H.-D. (2021). *The influence of feedback content and feedback time on multimedia learning achievement of college students and its mechanism*. Frontiers in Psychology, 12, 706821. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2021.706821](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.706821). The experiment assigned 157 college students to six combinations of immediate or delayed timing and adaptive, elaborated, or knowledge-of-correct-response content. It measured test-stage problem solving, motivation, subjective cognitive-load reports, and eye-movement indicators. Immediate feedback produced higher test scores than delayed feedback in that multimedia task, while adaptive feedback outperformed the other content conditions. This is evidence that timing and content depend on the task and outcome, not a universal immediate-feedback rule.

## Source 3 — meta-analysis of computer-based feedback

Van der Kleij, F. M., Feskens, R. C. W., and Eggen, T. J. H. M. (2015). *Effects of feedback in a computer-based learning environment on students’ learning outcomes: A meta-analysis*. Review of Educational Research, 85(4), 475–511. DOI: [10.3102/0034654314564881](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314564881). The review synthesized 40 studies and 70 effect sizes. Elaborated feedback had a larger average effect than correctness-only or correct-answer feedback, especially for higher-order outcomes. The abstract reports effect sizes of 0.49 for elaborated feedback, 0.05 for knowledge of results, and 0.32 for knowledge of the correct response. The review also reports moderation by timing and educational level and does not reduce feedback timing to one simple rule.

## Source 4 — developmental feedback processing review

Fyfe, E. R., Borriello, G., and Merrick, M. (2022). *A developmental perspective on feedback: How corrective feedback influences children’s literacy, mathematics, and problem solving*. Educational Psychologist, 58(3), 130–145. DOI: [10.1080/00461520.2022.2108426](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2022.2108426). This systematic review covered 44 peer-reviewed papers and 59 unique experiments involving children from preschool through sixth grade. It treats feedback as a multistep process: noticing, decoding, relating the message to the original response, integrating it with prior knowledge, deciding what to do, and acting. Age, prior knowledge, feedback features, and learner differences can affect whether a message produces learning. Its population is children and should not be generalized automatically to adults.

## Operational measure map

| Research observable | What it can describe | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate test score | Performance close to practice or feedback | Durable retention or transfer |
| Delayed test score | Later retention under the tested conditions | A universal effect across contexts |
| New-problem performance | Transfer to an unpractised problem type | Generalization to every domain |
| Feedback timing | When information was delivered | That timing caused a benefit independent of content and task |
| Feedback content | Correctness, answer, explanation, or adaptive message | That a learner noticed or used it |
| Motivation, cognitive-load, or preference rating | Subjective experience and belief | Actual learning outcome |
| Eye movements or other proxies | Attention or processing correlate | Direct proof of understanding |

## Safety and scientific limits

The sources support conditional interpretation, not a universal prescription. Immediate feedback can outperform delayed feedback in one multimedia problem-solving task, while delayed feedback produced better transfer in one engineering-course context. Feedback quality also depends on message content, learner characteristics, noticing, and the final assessment. The reel is public education only; it is not medical advice, psychological diagnosis, or individualized treatment.

# REEL-0067 source validation — plant diversity and balanced meal variety

## Working topic

**Plant diversity and balanced meal variety** as a practical, non-prescriptive nutrition-education cue.

## Claim register

| Claim used in the reel | Evidence class | Boundary | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant-rich dietary patterns are associated with improved health and reduced non-communicable-disease risk in the reviewed literature. | Mixed: association and review-level synthesis | The source discusses associations and uncertainties; the reel must not present this as proof that plant diversity alone causes a specific outcome. | Woodside et al., *Meeting report: plant-rich dietary patterns and health* [1] |
| Plant-focused eating does not have to mean veganism; an omnivorous pattern can still be rich in fruits and vegetables. | Established conceptual classification in the source | Do not prescribe a vegan or vegetarian diet; individual nutritional requirements and context differ. | Woodside et al. [1] |
| Greater dietary diversity can help support adequate nutrient intake, while restrictive patterns can create micronutrient considerations. | Strong practical interpretation | “Can help support” is not a assurance. The reel does not give a personal nutrient plan or supplement advice. | Woodside et al. [1] |
| Human intervention studies have reported short- to moderate-term effects of plant-based diets on some gut-microbiome and health-related measures. | Emerging / mixed | The systematic review found contradictory results for some taxa and states that the relationship remains largely unexplored. No fixed microbiome outcome is promised. | Sidhu et al., *Effect of Plant-Based Diets on Gut Microbiota: A Systematic Review of Interventional Studies* [2] |
| Research on plant-food diversity is methodologically inconsistent. | Strong review finding | A 2025 scoping review found 43 eligible studies, most observational, with no standard definition or consensus measurement method. This is a research limitation, not a reason to dismiss varied eating. | Creedon et al., *Diversity of Plant-Based Food Consumption* [3] |
| A variety cue may be a reasonable everyday behavior prompt. | Practical interpretation | This is not a validated prescription, a management, or a universal target such as “30 plants per week.” | [1] [2] [3] |

## Evidence hierarchy and interpretation

The evidence is **mixed**. The plant-rich dietary-pattern paper is a meeting report and synthesis of epidemiological and intervention research, not a single randomized trial proving a causal effect of “plant diversity.” The 2023 systematic review included 12 human intervention studies and reported some short- to moderate-term beneficial effects, but also contradictory findings for selected microbiome taxa and stated that more interventional research is needed. The 2025 scoping review included 43 studies, of which 38 were observational, and reported no standardized definition or measurement tool for plant-based food diversity.

The reel therefore uses “may support,” “can be part of,” and “research is still developing.” It does not claim that variety alone prevents disease, assurances gut-microbiome diversity, causes weight loss, or works identically for every person.

## Safety and inclusion boundary

This is general education, not personalized medical or nutritional advice. The reel should not instruct viewers to remove animal foods, follow a vegan diet, hit a fixed weekly number, or use supplements. Viewers with allergies, gastrointestinal disease, kidney disease, diabetes, pregnancy-related nutrition needs, or other medical conditions should seek advice from a qualified clinician or registered dietitian before making major dietary changes.

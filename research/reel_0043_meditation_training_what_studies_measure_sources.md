# Reel 0043 — ध्यान-अभ्यास: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence-bounded claim

Meditation-training studies may measure assigned practice duration, self-reported adherence, questionnaire scores, dropout, behavioral performance, or EEG features. These are different variables. Practice minutes are not automatically meditation skill, neural change, or a guaranteed benefit. An EEG classifier’s accuracy is a model result under a particular validation strategy, not a direct readout of private experience.

## Source 1 — EEG and machine-learning study

**Citation:** Shang, B., Duan, F., Fu, R., Gao, J., Sik, H., Meng, X., & Chang, C. (2023). *EEG-based investigation of effects of mindfulness meditation training on state and trait by deep learning and traditional machine learning*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 17, 1033420. DOI: [10.3389/fnhum.2023.1033420](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2023.1033420).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed original research.

**Design and measures:** EEG was recorded during resting and meditation from 11 novice MBSR practitioners, six men and five women, with mean age 35.7 years. The article used deep-learning and traditional machine-learning classifiers, with inter-subject, mixed-subject, intra-subject, and subject-transfer strategies.

**Reported outcome:** The paper reports classification performance for recognizing meditation state and trait effects under the specified models and validation strategies, and concludes that short-term training had EEG-recognizable state and trait effects in the study data.

**Measurement boundary:** These are model-derived classifications from EEG features under a particular dataset and validation design. They are not a direct meter of meditation quality, attention, private thoughts, or an individual’s future performance.

**Limitations:** The sample included 11 novice practitioners, and the result depends on the feature engineering or learned representation, classifier, and evaluation strategy. The reel does not generalize the reported percentages to every meditator.

## Source 2 — Randomized dose-and-type trial

**Citation:** Fincham, G. W., Mavor, K., & Dritschel, B. (2023). *Effects of Mindfulness Meditation Duration and Type on Well-being: an Online Dose-Ranging Randomized Controlled Trial*. Mindfulness, 14, 1171–1182. DOI: [10.1007/s12671-023-02119-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-023-02119-2).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed multi-arm randomized controlled trial.

**Design and exposure:** Participants were randomly allocated to four online interventions and asked to practice daily for two weeks. Sitting-meditation conditions were approximately 9.8 or 29.2 minutes daily; mindful-movement conditions were approximately 8.9 or 30.1 minutes daily.

**Outcomes:** The primary outcome was the Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Well-Being Scale. Secondary outcomes included GP-CORE and the Mindful Attention Awareness Scale. These questionnaires are outcomes, not direct recordings of meditation minutes or hidden mental states.

**Adherence and dropout:** Adherence was collected by asking how many of the 14 assigned sessions participants practiced. This was retrospective self-report, not device-recorded minute-by-minute exposure. Dropout was evaluated through post-intervention questionnaire completion.

**Measurement boundary:** Assigned dose, reported adherence, questionnaire change, and completion are distinct. A randomized design strengthens causal comparison for the assigned protocol, but a two-week online study does not establish that more minutes always help or that the result applies to every person.

## Source 3 — Randomized EEG example

**Citation:** Moore, A., Gruber, T., Derose, J., & Malinowski, P. (2012). *Regular, brief mindfulness meditation practice improves electrophysiological markers of attentional control*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 6, 18. DOI: [10.3389/fnhum.2012.00018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00018).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed randomized longitudinal EEG study.

**Use in this reel:** This is a related example of separating a training protocol from task performance and EEG/ERP correlates. It is not used to claim a universal meditation effect.

## Language and safety controls

Use “assigned dose,” “reported adherence,” “questionnaire outcome,” “model-derived EEG feature,” and “in this study.” Do not say “more minutes always work,” “EEG reads thoughts,” “meditation cures attention,” or “this proves your brain changed.” No diagnosis, treatment, prescription, individualized meditation plan, guaranteed outcome, or universal timeline.

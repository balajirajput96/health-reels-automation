# R0070 Evidence Record: Dehumanization — What Studies Measure

## Research question and unique angle

This reel asks what a dehumanization study actually records. The angle is to separate explicit humanness ratings, context-specific self-report scales, implicit association or categorization performance, observed interaction outcomes, and physiological or neural correlates.

## Evidence table

| Measurement class | Example operationalization | Direct outcome | Boundary on interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit rating | Rate how human, complex, or mind-bearing a defined target seems | Target-specific rating | Depends on target, wording, comparison, and context |
| Context-specific scale | Report treatment or experience in a defined relationship | Scale score for a construct and population | Not automatically a universal trait or private-belief readout |
| Implicit task | Speed or error when categorizing paired concepts | Association or categorization performance | Not identical to explicit attitudes or behavior |
| Interaction behavior | Cooperation, empathic accuracy, or social responses with a target | Task behavior in one design | Associations do not prove causation or general transfer |
| Neural correlate | EEG mu-suppression or another signal during interaction | Correlated neural response under a protocol | Not direct mind-reading or a complete moral evaluation |

## Source 1 — face-to-face behavioral and neural measurement

Simon, J. C., & Gutsell, J. N. (2021). “Recognizing humanity: dehumanization predicts neural mirroring and empathic accuracy in face-to-face interactions.” *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*, 16, 463–473. https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsab014

In face-to-face dyadic interactions, the study measured how human participants evaluated one another, empathic accuracy about partners’ emotions, cooperation on a task, and EEG mu-suppression as an index of neural mirroring. The reported results linked higher perceived humanity with stronger neural mirroring, marginally greater empathic accuracy, and better cooperation in that study design. These are separate rating, behavior, and neural outcomes—not one universal dehumanization meter.

The paper also describes recognition of another person’s humanity as contextually contingent and studied strangers in ordinary face-to-face interactions. The safe conclusion is association within a defined design; the measures do not directly reveal private mental states or establish a stable universal trait.

## Source 2 — methodological caution for implicit association tasks

Greenwald, A. G., Nosek, B. A., Banaji, M. R., and colleagues. “Best research practices for using the Implicit Association Test.” Open-access methodological review. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9170636/

The review was identified as a methodological source on IAT research. Its relevant measurement distinction is that an IAT produces performance-based association-strength indices from categorization speed and errors. Such an index is not interchangeable with a self-reported attitude, a specific act, or a full description of an individual’s private beliefs. Because the accessible browser page encountered an access check during this run, the reel does not attribute a detailed numerical conclusion to it; it uses only the general operational distinction supported by the source title and research-method context.

## Source 3 — validated scale within romantic relationships

Pizzirani, B., Karantzas, G. C., & Mullins, E. R. (2019). “The Development and Validation of a Dehumanization Measure Within Romantic Relationships.” *Frontiers in Psychology*, 10, 2754. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02754

The authors developed and validated the Dehumanization in Romantic Relationships Scale, a self-report measure of dehumanization perpetration and targeting within close relationships. The work used factor-analytic and criterion-validity analyses across studies. Its construct is tied to relationship behaviors and appraisals in a defined context; it is not automatically a universal measure of attitudes toward every person or group. Reliability and validity evidence support interpreting the instrument within its intended construct and population.

## Evidence-bounded conclusion

Dehumanization studies do not all measure the same thing. A rating can measure perceived humanity of a defined target. A relationship scale can measure reported treatment or experience in that relationship. An implicit task can measure association-strength performance. Cooperation or empathic accuracy can be observed in a particular interaction. EEG or other physiology can be a correlate under a protocol. None of these alone is direct mind-reading, a complete moral judgment, a diagnosis, or proof of causality.

# Reel 0049 retrieval audit: interleaving and what studies measure

## Retrieval scope

The retrieval focused on peer-reviewed work that distinguishes interleaved practice from blocked practice and separates immediate performance, delayed retention, category memory, novel-item transfer, and authentic course outcomes.

## Verified findings

**Samani and Pan (2021), NPJ Science of Learning, DOI 10.1038/s41539-021-00110-x.** In a preregistered undergraduate physics course study, 350 students experienced blocked and interleaved homework across two four-week stages. The same total practice problems were used; only their arrangement differed. Interleaved homework was harder and received lower practice accuracy and lower judgments of learning, yet surprise criterial tests with novel, more challenging problems favored interleaving. The paper reports standardized effects of d = 0.40 in Stage 1 and d = 0.91 in Stage 2, with median improvements of 50% and 125%, respectively. These are study-specific task outcomes, not universal learning guarantees.

**Carvalho and Goldstone (2014), Frontiers in Psychology, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00936.** Participants learned high-similarity or low-similarity categories under blocked or interleaved schedules and completed immediate and approximately 24-hour delayed transfer tests. The study tested whether the schedule-by-category interaction changed with delay. Its abstract reports that the interaction was not modulated by the longer retention interval and that the pattern was consistent with cross-category comparison rather than a simple temporal-spacing explanation. The result is conditional on the category structure and transfer task.

**Firth, Rivers, Boyle, and colleagues (2021), A systematic review of interleaving as a concept learning strategy, Review of Education, DOI 10.1002/rev3.3266.** The review included 26 studies; 17 studies with 32 constituent datasets formed the meta-analysis. It reported interleaving benefits up to Hedges g = 0.65 for memory of studied items and up to g = 0.66 for transfer to novel items, with the greatest usefulness when category differences were subtle. The review states that benefits extended to delayed tests but also emphasizes that the literature is dominated by laboratory studies of university undergraduates and that classroom generalization remains a research need.

## Evidence boundaries

Interleaving means switching among related concepts or categories during learning; it is not identical to spacing all repetitions across days. A lower score during practice can coexist with better delayed or transfer performance, but this pattern is not guaranteed for every material, similarity structure, learning strategy, or outcome. The evidence supports asking what was practiced, how items were sequenced, when the test occurred, whether test items were old or novel, and whether performance was based on similarity, rule abstraction, or problem solving. No individual study schedule, diagnosis, treatment, or grade promise should be inferred.

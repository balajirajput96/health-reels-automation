# Reel 0038 — cue competition: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence question

Prospective memory means remembering to carry out a planned action in the future while continuing another task. In implementation-intention research, an if-then plan may strengthen the link between a critical cue and a response, but the cue still competes with ongoing task demands. Studies therefore separate cue detection, remembering the intended action, response initiation, ongoing-task performance, and later transfer.

## Measurement layers

| Layer | Example operationalization | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective component | Detecting that a target event or time has arrived | “Remember that something must be done,” not necessarily remembering the action |
| Retrospective component | Recall of what action is required | Correct action memory, not proof that it was executed |
| Cue focality | Focal cue is processed by the ongoing task; non-focal cue is not | Focal and non-focal tasks place different demands on monitoring |
| Cue detection/retrieval | Target recognition, response time, action retrieval, or multinomial-model parameters | Cognitive-process evidence, not automatically everyday adherence |
| Ongoing-task cost | Accuracy or slowing on the task being performed alongside PM | Improvement in PM can coexist with a cost to the ongoing task |
| Immediate PM performance | Correct target-triggered action in a laboratory task | Tested cue, task, sample, and session only |
| Transfer and durability | New laboratory task, ecological task, or follow-up session | Must be measured directly; a single session may not generalize |

## Vetted scholarly sources

1. Smith, R. E., Rogers, M. D., McVay, J. C., Lopez, J. A., & Loft, S. (2014). “Investigating How Implementation Intentions Improve Non-Focal Prospective Memory Tasks.” *Consciousness and Cognition*, 27, 213–230. DOI: [10.1016/j.concog.2014.05.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2014.05.003); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4113409/). Across three experiments, the paper examined non-focal prospective-memory performance, conscious-resource allocation, ongoing-task costs, recall of the appropriate action, and multinomial-model processes. The results support separating target recognition, action retrieval, and ongoing-task performance.

2. Koo, Y. W., Neumann, D. L., Ownsworth, T., & Shum, D. H. K. (2022). “The effects of implementation intentions on prospective memory in young and older adults.” *Frontiers in Psychology*, 13, 905860. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2022.905860](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.905860); [full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.905860/full). The study compared young and older adults across laboratory and ecological prospective-memory tasks. It distinguishes prospective from retrospective memory and time-based from event-based tasks, including focal versus non-focal cues. The overall facilitation was not statistically significant; a single-session strategy showed a limited first-session laboratory trend in one older-adult condition, while generalization and longer-term ecological transfer were not significant.

3. Wieber, F., Thürmer, J. L., & Gollwitzer, P. M. (2015). “Promoting the translation of intentions into action by implementation intentions: behavioral effects and physiological correlates.” *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 9, 395. DOI: [10.3389/fnhum.2015.00395](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00395); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500900/). The review describes implementation intentions as if-then plans and summarizes cue accessibility, response initiation, prospective-memory tasks, and physiological or neural correlates. It supports the claim that process measures and behavior outcomes are related but distinct.

## Claim boundaries

The reel will explain cue competition as a measurement problem, not a literal battle inside the brain. A plan may improve target detection or action retrieval under some conditions, but it can also draw attention away from an ongoing task, and a laboratory benefit need not transfer to a new or ecological task. The reel will not promise automatic memory, permanent habits, superior intelligence, or guaranteed everyday performance.

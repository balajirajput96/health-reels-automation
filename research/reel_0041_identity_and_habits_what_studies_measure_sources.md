# Reel 0041 — Identity और habits: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence-bounded claim

Identity और habit एक ही चीज़ नहीं हैं। Identity को अक्सर self-description, role, value, या “मैं किस तरह का व्यक्ति हूँ” जैसे self-report items से operationalize किया जाता है। Habit को repeated behavior, context-behavior consistency, automaticity items, habit-strength scales, या longitudinal behavior records से assess किया जा सकता है. इनके बीच association मिले तो भी वह अपने-आप causal identity change, fixed personality, या future behavior की guarantee नहीं बनती।

## Source 1 — Habit and identity

**Citation:** Verplanken, B., & Sui, J. (2019). *Habit and Identity: Behavioral, Cognitive, Affective, and Motivational Facets of an Intertwined Relationship*. Frontiers in Psychology, 10, 1504. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01504](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01504).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed research article.

**What it contributes:** The article examines how habit and identity can be intertwined across behavioral, cognitive, affective, and motivational facets, including what people regard as their “true self.” For this reel, it supports treating identity and habit as related but distinguishable constructs.

**Measurement boundary:** A self-description is a report about how a person sees a role, value, or self. It is not a direct recording of all behavior, an objective personality meter, or a readout of a hidden brain state. The reel therefore uses “identity self-report” rather than “true identity detected.”

**What it does not establish:** It does not justify diagnosing someone from a habit label, declaring a permanent identity, or claiming that saying an identity statement causes a particular behavior.

## Source 2 — Longitudinal habit formation in daily life

**Citation:** van der Weiden, A., Benjamins, J., Gillebaart, M., Ybema, J. F., & de Ridder, D. T. D. (2020). *How to Form Good Habits? A Longitudinal Field Study on the Role of Self-Control in Habit Formation*. Frontiers in Psychology, 11, 560. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00560](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00560).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed longitudinal field study.

**Design and measures:** Adults who wanted to form a habit were followed for approximately 90 days. The target behaviors included health, interpersonal, financial, and environmental domains. Daily measures included goal-congruent behavior performance, self-control capacity, and habit strength.

**Reported finding:** Habit formation increased substantially over the three-month period, particularly among people who consistently performed the desired behavior. Contrary to the authors’ expectation, self-control capacity did not appear to affect the habit-formation process in the predicted way.

**What it supports:** Repeated behavior and longitudinal measurement can show a trajectory in habit strength; consistency and self-report variables can be studied separately.

**What it does not support:** It does not establish a universal number of days for habit formation, prove that identity statements cause habits, or guarantee that one person will follow the group pattern.

## Source 3 — Parsimonious habit measurement

**Citation:** Gardner, B., Abraham, C., Lally, P., & de Bruijn, G.-J. (2012). *Towards parsimony in habit measurement: Testing the convergent and predictive validity of an automaticity subscale of the Self-Report Habit Index*. International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity, 9, 102. DOI: [10.1186/1479-5868-9-102](https://doi.org/10.1186/1479-5868-9-102).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed original measurement research.

**What it contributes:** The paper supports separating frequency or past behavior from a measure intended to capture automaticity. This is central to the reel’s checklist: what did the study count, what did participants report, and what was the outcome measure?

## Evidence categories and safety

The identity source is used for construct distinction; the van der Weiden paper is used for longitudinal original research; Gardner et al. is used for measurement parsimony. The reel will use “associated with,” “self-report,” “habit-strength measure,” and “longitudinal pattern.” It will not use clinical labels, moral judgments, fixed identity claims, mind-reading, personalized treatment, prescriptions, or guarantees.

# Reel 0027 browser findings

On 2026-08-25, two public PMC full-text pages were opened and read.

1. Kang et al., “Neural Mechanisms of Observational Learning: A Neural Working Model” (PMC8100516), reviews methodological approaches and distinguishes simple imitation tasks from observational learning in which an observer learns stimulus-response or action-outcome information. It describes neural evidence as part of a working model, not as a direct readout of learning content.

2. Fryling, Johnston, and Hayes, “Understanding Observational Learning: An Interbehavioral Approach” (PMC3139552), reviews classic behavioral research and discusses behavior change after observing a model, including the role of consequences and later demonstrations. The article’s DOI is 10.1007/BF03393102.

These findings support a reel that separates observation, retention, later performance, and interpretation. They do not justify diagnosing a viewer, claiming that imitation equals complete learning, or treating a neural activation pattern as proof of a private mental state.

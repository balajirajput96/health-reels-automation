# Reel 0057 — Working memory: what studies measure

## Research question

When a study reports “working-memory capacity,” what was actually measured? This reel separates performance on a particular span or updating task from self-reported strategy and from broader theoretical claims about working memory.

## Operational categories used in the reel

**Task performance:** span length, accuracy, response time, updating, recognition, or recall on a specified procedure. These are observable outputs under task instructions.

**Self-report/coded strategy:** what a participant says they did, sometimes coded by independent raters. This is a report about strategy, not a complete transcript of cognition.

**Psychometric/latent estimate:** a factor or composite inferred from several indicators. It can separate shared construct variance from task-specific and measurement error variance, but it remains an estimate.

**Theory:** working memory is a broad construct involving temporary maintenance and processing/control. A single task result does not automatically establish general memory ability, intelligence, learning potential, or everyday functioning.

## Safe conclusion

A working-memory study may measure performance on one span, n-back, updating, or recall procedure; it may also measure self-reported strategy or fit a latent model. These measures can relate, but they are not interchangeable and none alone is a complete readout of a person’s general memory.

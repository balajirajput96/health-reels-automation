# REEL-0075 Source Validation: Meaningful Hobbies and Everyday Recovery Support

## Editorial question

How can a reel present enjoyable hobbies as possible recovery-supporting activities without claiming that a specific hobby treats stress, causes health improvements, or works for everyone?

## Claim register

| Claim | Evidence class | Support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enjoyable leisure includes voluntary activities such as hobbies, social activities, nature, sports, or quiet time. | Peer-reviewed definition | Pressman et al. define enjoyable leisure broadly as pleasurable activities people choose during free time and measure several categories [1]. | No single category is required, superior, or universally enjoyable. |
| More frequent aggregate enjoyable leisure was associated with more positive psychosocial states and lower negative affect in a multi-study sample. | Observational association | Pressman et al. combined four studies with 1,399 participants and reported associations between higher PEAT scores and psychosocial and physiological measures [1]. | The design is associational; do not say hobbies caused better mood, lower cortisol, lower blood pressure, or disease prevention. |
| Enjoyable activities are discussed as possible “breathers” or “restorers” during or after stress. | Theoretical mechanism | Pressman et al. describe restoration and recovery as theoretical explanations for why leisure may help replenish resources [1]. | Present this as a proposed explanation, not a guaranteed biological process. |
| Meaningful participation and belonging/connectedness were associated with well-being in a survey of healthy working-age adults. | Cross-sectional survey association | Haim-Litevsky et al. studied 121 adults in Israel and found associations among meaningful participation, belonging/connectedness, and well-being [2]. | Convenience sample, self-report, cross-sectional design, and limited generalizability mean causality cannot be inferred. |
| Choosing a personally meaningful, accessible activity may be a reasonable everyday recovery cue. | Practical synthesis | The sources support voluntary, enjoyable, meaningful participation as a plausible recovery-supporting idea [1] [2]. | This is not a prescription. Do not require a fixed duration, productivity outcome, social setting, or expensive hobby. |

## Editorial boundaries

The reel will use cautious language: “may support recovery,” “is associated with,” and “for some people.” It will emphasize choice, accessibility, and the difference between a restorative activity and another obligation. It will not claim that hobbies cure stress, depression, trauma, burnout, or physical illness.

The reel will not imply that people with significant symptoms should solve them through hobbies. Persistent or serious concerns should be discussed with a qualified professional. No universal “best hobby” or fixed daily schedule will be prescribed.

# R0076 Evidence Record: टालमटोल — अध्ययन वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Research question
When a study says it measured procrastination, did it measure a person’s self-report, an observed delay in a task, an outcome such as mood or grades, or a theoretical correlate? This reel uses the angle that **the instrument and operational definition matter**.

## Evidence category
This is a measurement-literacy reel grounded in peer-reviewed research and a systematic review. It distinguishes self-report, behavioral timing, task completion/pacing, affective well-being, academic outcomes, association/prediction, and theory. No claim is made that any instrument directly reads an inner state, diagnoses a condition, or identifies a universal cause.

## Source and measurement table

| Source | Population/design | What was measured | Bounded finding used here | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vangsness et al. (2022) [1] | 242 students; psychometric and predictive comparisons across 10 self-report measures | Questionnaire scores; days to study completion; deadline-action pacing | Self-report instruments had strong internal reliability and moderate concurrent validity; some predicted days to completion, but none predicted deadline-action pacing; several measures had poor fit | Findings concern these instruments, participants, and behavioral operationalizations; they do not make self-report and behavior interchangeable |
| Steel (2007) [2] | Meta-analytic/theoretical review drawing on correlational, experimental, and qualitative work; 691 correlations | Correlates/predictors and theoretical accounts of procrastination | Task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy, impulsiveness, and conscientiousness-related facets were reported as strong and consistent correlates/predictors; temporal motivation theory was presented as an integrative account | Meta-analytic association/prediction and theory are not proof of one universal causal mechanism |
| Krause & Freund (2014) [3] | 162 undergraduates; 16 measurement occasions over 8 weeks | Self-reported state procrastination; planned-versus-actual study-time difference; affective well-being | Self-report and behavioral measures correlated only moderately; self-report better predicted affective well-being than the behavioral measure in this design | Different measures may relate to different outcomes; this does not establish that self-report is always superior |
| Turner & Hodis (2023) [4] | Systematic review of recent intervention literature; 21 included studies | Intervention effects on academic procrastination and gaps in mechanisms/boundary conditions | 17 included studies reported significant reductions; the review identified a need for mediation/moderation analyses and better understanding of mechanisms and boundary conditions | A review of intervention reports does not imply all interventions work for everyone or that all studies used one identical measure |

## Safe synthesis
A study may operationalize procrastination as questionnaire responses about delaying or discomfort, actual/planned-versus-actual timing, days to completion, or deadline pacing. Those indicators overlap only partly and may relate differently to mood and other outcomes. Therefore, a headline such as “procrastination is measured” is incomplete unless it says **which measure, in what task or domain, in which population, and against which outcome**.

## Claims allowed in the reel

1. “Studies procrastination को एक ही तरीके से नहीं मापतीं” is supported by the different operationalizations across [1] and [3], and by the review evidence in [4].
2. “Questionnaire score और observed delay समान नहीं” is supported by the moderate or inconsistent relationships reported in [1] and [3].
3. “कुछ measures completion days या mood जैसे outcomes से अलग तरह जुड़ सकते हैं” is supported by [1] and [3].
4. “Task aversiveness, self-efficacy, impulsiveness और conscientiousness-related traits associations/predictors के रूप में study हुए हैं” is bounded to the meta-analytic review [2].
5. “Association or prediction universal cause नहीं” is a safety-preserving interpretation of the designs and should be stated explicitly.

## Claims excluded
The reel must not say that procrastination is laziness, that one score diagnoses depression/ADHD or a personality type, that a specific neural mechanism has been proven, that an intervention guarantees results, or that the viewer can infer another person’s private motives from a delay.

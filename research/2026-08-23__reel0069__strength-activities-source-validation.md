# REEL-0069 Source Validation: Strength Activities in a Weekly Movement Routine

## Editorial question

How can a reel explain the role of muscle-strengthening activity in a weekly movement routine without turning public-health guidance into an individualized prescription or promising outcomes for everyone?

## Claim register

| Claim | Evidence class | Support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| WHO and CDC include muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups in adult activity guidance. | Public-health guidance | WHO describes muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups on 2 or more days per week for adults; CDC gives the same broad recommendation alongside aerobic activity [1] [2]. | These are population-level guidelines, not a personalized plan. Frequency, intensity, exercise selection, and progression depend on the person and context. |
| Strength training can improve physical functioning in adults. | Overview of systematic reviews | El-Kotob et al. reviewed 11 systematic reviews representing 364 primary studies and 382,627 unique participants; the overview reported improvement in physical functioning, while evidence certainty varied by outcome [3]. | This does not mean every exercise, dose, or person gets the same result. The reel should not promise a specific gain. |
| Resistance exercise may improve some cardiometabolic measures in adults. | Systematic review and meta-analysis, with low/very-low certainty for outcomes | Ashton et al. included 173 randomized trials and reported improvements in some blood-pressure and insulin-related measures, but rated the quality of evidence low or very low for all outcomes [4]. | Do not make disease-management, prevention, weight-loss, or biomarker promises in a one-minute reel. This evidence is separate from the public-health recommendation itself. |
| Bodyweight or elastic-resistance movements can count as resistance training. | Definition and intervention examples | The overview describes resistance as muscles contracting against an external force; the included interventions used weight machines, free weights, bodyweight, elastic bands, and related formats [3] [4]. | The reel should not imply that one informal movement automatically meets a guideline or is appropriate for every body. |
| A new strengthening routine should be adjusted for ability, symptoms, access, and safety. | Safety/editorial boundary | The reviewed evidence included varied populations and protocols, and adverse events were not consistently monitored or reported in the overview [3]. | Avoid telling viewers to push through pain. Accessibility and professional advice may matter, especially with injury, symptoms, disability, pregnancy, or chronic conditions. |

## Editorial boundaries

The reel will distinguish the WHO/CDC population-level guideline from research on structured resistance-training programs. It will not claim that strength activities assurance better balance, cognition, mood, metabolism, weight, longevity, or disease outcomes for every viewer. It will not prescribe a specific load, exercise, rep count, or schedule for an individual.

The practical cue will be modest: consider how major muscle groups could be included in a weekly routine, using an accessible option and gradual progression when appropriate. A viewer should stop for pain or concerning symptoms and seek individualized guidance when needed.

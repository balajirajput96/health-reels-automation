# Reel 0002 source validation — sleep environment

## Topic

Sleep environment: bedroom darkness, noise, temperature, and comfort as contextual supports for sleep.

## Sources reviewed

1. CDC / NIOSH archived guidance, “Creating a Good Sleep Environment.” https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/emres/longhourstraining/environment.html
   - The page is archived and notes it may be outdated; it was last reviewed April 1, 2020.
   - It recommends a sleep environment that is very dark, quiet, cool, and comfortable.
   - Specific suggestions include blocking light, reducing noise and nonessential alerts, keeping temperatures cool, and avoiding work, reading, or television in the sleeping area.

2. Sleep Foundation, “Bedroom Environment: What Elements Are Important?” https://www.sleepfoundation.org/bedroom-environment
   - Updated July 10, 2025; written by Logan Foley and medically reviewed by Anis Rehman, MD.
   - Summarizes evidence that temperature, noise, light, and comfort influence the sleep environment.
   - Notes that many experts use approximately 65°F / 18.3°C as a reference point, but personal comfort and bedding matter; it is not universal.
   - Describes noise disturbances as capable of fragmenting sleep and notes that evidence for added background noise is not uniform; masking sound is optional and individual.
   - Describes natural light/darkness as connected with circadian timing and says evening artificial light can delay sleep onset in some contexts; this does not mean every screen affects everyone identically.

## Evidence boundary

The reel can say that a dark, quiet, cool, comfortable room is a generally supported sleep-hygiene context. It must not claim that bedroom changes cure insomnia, guarantee restful sleep, or replace evaluation for persistent sleep problems. Temperature, bedding, masking sound, and light sensitivity vary by person. The CDC page is archived, so current guidance is framed as general education rather than a current clinical directive.

## Practical, low-risk suggestions

Try one reversible change at a time: dim or block intrusive light, silence nonessential alerts, reduce avoidable noise, and adjust bedding or ventilation for comfort. Keep a clear path to the bathroom if making the room dark. Persistent or severe sleep difficulty warrants qualified professional support.

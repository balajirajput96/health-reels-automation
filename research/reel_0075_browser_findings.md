# R0075 Browser Research Findings

## Source 1 — Official Self-Determination Theory overview

URL: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/

The official theory overview describes Self-Determination Theory as a framework for studying human motivation and personality. It identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness as basic psychological needs. It says conditions supporting these experiences are argued to foster more volitional forms of motivation and engagement, including performance, persistence, and creativity, while thwarting is associated with non-optimal functioning. Safe interpretation: these are theoretical constructs about experienced needs and motivational regulation, not a direct meter of a person’s whole inner state.

## Source 2 — Martela, Ryan, & Steger (2021), measurement paper

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8264600/

The paper argues that comprehensive well-being assessment should include indicators of psychological need satisfaction, especially autonomy, competence, and relatedness. It describes autonomy as a sense of volition/internal causality, competence as mastery/effectance/efficacy, and relatedness as connection with others. The paper emphasizes measurement and construct coverage, treating need satisfaction as one dimension alongside other well-being indicators. Safe interpretation: scales can capture reported experiences of need satisfaction in a specified context; they do not diagnose someone or replace broader outcome measurement.

## Current evidence boundary

The reel should explain that SDT studies may measure perceived autonomy, competence, relatedness, and forms of motivation such as intrinsic or more/less autonomous regulation, often with questionnaires or domain-specific instruments. Researchers may then relate these measures to engagement, persistence, performance, well-being, or other outcomes. Need satisfaction, motivational regulation, and observed outcomes must remain distinct; associations and theory claims should not be narrated as direct mind-reading or guaranteed individual predictions.

## Source 3 — Ryan & Deci (2020), Contemporary Educational Psychology

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0361476X20300254

The review presents SDT as a framework for factors that facilitate or undermine intrinsic motivation, autonomous extrinsic motivation, and psychological wellness. It distinguishes intrinsic motivation as doing an activity for inherent interest or enjoyment and discusses more autonomous forms of extrinsic motivation. It links these forms to educational outcomes and to support for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, while noting that research is conducted across defined educational levels and cultural contexts. Safe interpretation: motivational regulation types and outcomes are studied as differentiated constructs; the review does not justify collapsing them into a single universal motivation reading.

## Source 4 — Official Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction Scales documentation

URL: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/basic-psychological-need-satisfaction-scales/

The official scale page says the general-life Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction Scale has 21 items assessing competence, autonomy, and relatedness. It also lists domain-specific versions, including a 9-item relationship-domain scale and a 21-item work-domain scale. Safe interpretation: SDT measurement can be domain-specific and item-based; a general-life score and a work or relationship score are not interchangeable, and a scale captures reported need satisfaction rather than direct private motives or diagnosis.

## Evidence-bounded conclusion

R0075 should explain that SDT research may measure three experienced needs—autonomy, competence, relatedness—along with motivational regulation such as intrinsic or autonomous versus controlled forms. It commonly uses questionnaires or domain-specific scales and may relate them to engagement, persistence, performance, and well-being. Need satisfaction, regulation type, and observed outcomes are separate evidence categories. Results are theory- and context-bound, not direct mind-reading or guaranteed individual forecasts.

# Reel 0055 browser/research findings

## Scope
Topic: uncertainty — what studies actually measure. Unique angle: the word “uncertainty” can refer to different constructs and study measures; a result should be labeled by the measure used rather than generalized into a person-level trait.

## Source 1 — Franssen et al. (2026)
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1844980
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1844980
Title: Psychological uncertainty in human experience and behavior: a systematic review on measurements, induction, and effects.

The systematic review synthesized 27 peer-reviewed articles explicitly addressing uncertainty. It reports that uncertainty is not a single unified construct and definitions vary by context. It distinguishes psychological uncertainty as an experiential state from uncertainty in a decision environment or from related terms such as unpredictability, uncontrollability, and ambiguity. Its review found that measurements most frequently used Likert or multiple-choice self-report. It also describes induction methods such as recalling uncertain events, varying or withholding information, and altering predictability. The measure boundary is important: a self-report score is a report of experienced uncertainty in that task/context, not a direct readout of a stable personality essence.

## Source 2 — Brunyé & Giles (2023)
DOI: 10.3389/frvir.2023.951435
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/frvir.2023.951435
Title: Methods for eliciting and measuring behavioral and physiological consequences of stress and uncertainty in virtual reality.

The methods article describes a scenario that manipulates stimulus clarity to induce transient uncertainty during perceptual decisions. It separates the manipulation from outcomes: task performance can include accuracy, discriminability (d-prime), decision criterion, response time, and confidence ratings. It also records physiological and biochemical signals, including heart rate, pupil diameter, autonomic responses, salivary alpha-amylase, and cortisol, primarily as stress-related responses. Recognition memory, spatial orienting, threat classification, and decision-making are distinct task outcomes. A result on one outcome or signal should not be treated as direct evidence about all other outcomes.

## Source 3 — Anderson et al. (2019)
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02504
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02504
Title: The Relationship Between Uncertainty and Affect.

This review notes that uncertainty is often used inconsistently and distinguishes it from mere ignorance: uncertainty is the subjective awareness of not knowing. It describes probability/risk, ambiguity, and complexity as different sources of uncertainty. It reviews theoretical links between uncertainty and affect, while explicitly identifying knowledge gaps and noting that its treatment is not a systematic review of all empirical work. This supports cautious language: theories and associations should not be narrated as universal causal laws.

## Source 4 — Windschitl & Wells (1996)
DOI: 10.1037/1076-898X.2.4.343
URL: https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.2.4.343
Title: Measuring psychological uncertainty: Verbal versus numeric methods.

The paper compares verbal and numeric ways of eliciting uncertainty and argues that alternatives to traditional numeric methods may have advantages. It is used here only for the measurement-method point: the response format can influence what is observed. The reel must not claim that verbal scales are universally superior.

## Evidence and safety boundary
Established evidence: the cited papers describe operational definitions, manipulations, self-report, behavioral performance, confidence, physiological/biochemical measures, and theoretical distinctions. Association/theory: links between uncertainty and affect are not equivalent to universal causation. Measurement boundary: task performance, self-report, response time, confidence, physiological signals, and model-derived quantities are different observables. No diagnosis, treatment, individual assessment, prediction guarantee, or personal advice.

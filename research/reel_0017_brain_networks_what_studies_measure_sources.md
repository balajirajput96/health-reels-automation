## Verified source record

### [1] Yao, Hu, Xie, Moore & Zheng (2015), *A review of structural and functional brain networks: small world and atlas*

- DOI: `10.1007/s40708-015-0009-z`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40708-015-0009-z
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4883160/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed review article.
- Relevant boundary: structural networks can be constructed from anatomical measurements or white-matter tractography; functional networks represent temporal dependencies between separated regions. Brain regions are often defined through an atlas or parcellation, and the choice of atlas can materially affect quantified network properties.

### [2] Sporns (2018), *Graph theory methods: applications in brain networks*

- DOI: `10.31887/DCNS.2018.20.2/osporns`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2018.20.2/osporns
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6136126/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed methods review.
- Relevant boundary: graph analysis represents nodes and edges in a connection matrix. Measures can be local, such as degree or strength, or global, such as path length and efficiency; modules and hubs are mathematical summaries whose meaning depends on the data representation and null model. Functional graphs encode statistical dependencies and are not automatically physical communication routes.

### [3] Babaeeghazvini, Rueda-Delgado, Gooijers, Swinnen & Daffertshofer (2021), *Brain Structural and Functional Connectivity: A Review of Combined Works of Diffusion Magnetic Resonance Imaging and Electro-Encephalography*

- DOI: `10.3389/fnhum.2021.721206`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.721206
- Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.721206/full
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed multimodal review.
- Relevant boundary: structural connectivity is related to anatomical fiber organization, while functional connectivity is a statistical dependence between activity time series. EEG provides high-temporal-resolution electrical signals; fMRI BOLD provides an indirect, hemodynamically mediated measure of neural functioning. The review highlights variability across paradigms and analysis choices.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Measurement layer | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Structural connectivity | Tracts, diffusion-derived white-matter measures, or anatomical relations | A model of anatomical organization or tissue properties | That a tract is active during a specific thought or that structure alone proves function |
| Functional connectivity | Statistical dependence between regional time series | Co-variation or association under a defined task or resting condition | A physical wire, information flow, or causation by itself |
| Task activation | Signal change linked to an experimental task | A task-related contrast under that design | A single region as the sole cause of a complex behavior |
| EEG/MEG synchronization | Time-resolved electrical or magnetic relationships | Temporal dynamics and statistical coupling at the measured scale | Exact deep-brain activity or a complete whole-brain explanation |
| fMRI/BOLD network | Correlated hemodynamic time series | Indirect network-level association | A direct readout of neural firing or private mental content |
| Graph summary | Degree, strength, modularity, efficiency, hubness, or other derived metric | A quantitative description of the chosen graph | A universal ranking of brain regions or a clinical conclusion without validation |
| Atlas/parcellation choice | Definition of nodes and regions of interest | The selected spatial representation for analysis | An atlas-independent ground truth |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel presents brain networks as data representations built from selected nodes, edges, signals, tasks, and analysis choices. “Functional connectivity” is described as statistical dependence, not a literal cable or proof of causal communication. Structural, functional, task, and graph measures are kept distinct. The reel does not diagnose a brain disorder, infer a viewer’s intelligence or personality, or claim that a scan directly reads thoughts.

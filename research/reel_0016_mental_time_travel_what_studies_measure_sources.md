### [1] Collaro, Barton, Ainge & Easton (2024), *Measuring episodic memory and mental time travel: crossing the species gap*

- DOI: `10.1098/rstb.2023.0406`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2023.0406
- Full text: https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/379/1913/20230406/109563/Measuring-episodic-memory-and-mental-time-travel
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed review article.
- Relevant boundary: mental time travel concerns projection into the personal past or possible future and relates to experiential aspects of episodic memory and episodic future thinking. The review emphasizes that mental time travel and episodic memory are not synonymous, and that behavioral outputs require careful interpretation.

### [2] Quoidbach, Hansenne & Mottet (2008), *Personality and mental time travel: a differential approach to autonoetic consciousness*

- DOI: `10.1016/j.concog.2008.04.002`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2008.04.002
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18508283/
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed empirical study.
- Relevant boundary: the study examined relations between personality measures, episodic memory, and episodic future thinking using participant reports. Its abstract supports a relation between remembering the past and projecting into the future, but it does not make a report a direct recording of a private mental event.

### [3] Suddendorf, Addis & Corballis (2009), *The evolution of foresight: What is mental time travel, and is it unique to humans?*

- DOI: `10.1016/j.behavbrainres.2008.12.011`
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.behavbrainres.2008.12.011
- Evidence type: peer-reviewed theoretical review.
- Relevant boundary: the review frames mental time travel as the capacity to mentally project oneself backward or forward in time, and discusses the difficulty of inferring subjective experience from behavioral evidence, especially in nonhuman animals. Behavioral flexibility is evidence about performance under a task, not a direct window into phenomenology.

## Evidence map for the reel

| Research window | Typical observable | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autobiographical recall | Narrative or rating about a personally experienced past event | Reported detail, specificity, vividness, emotion, or source information | That the report is a complete or perfectly accurate replay |
| Episodic future thinking | Description or rating of a possible personal future event | Content, detail, temporal distance, or subjective qualities of the imagined event | That the imagined event will happen or is a literal prediction |
| Remember/know or source judgments | Whether recognition includes contextual/source recollection | A distinction between richer source recollection and familiarity in that task | A universal test of subjective consciousness |
| Behavioral task | Choices, flexible planning, or use of what–where–when information | Performance that is consistent with memory or projection under defined conditions | Private phenomenology without additional assumptions |
| Self-report scales | Confidence, vividness, emotional valence, or frequency ratings | The participant’s reported experience under the wording and time window | A direct recording free from memory, language, or demand effects |
| Neural/physiological correlate | Signal during remembering or imagining | Association with a task and measured report | Literal time travel, mind-reading, or a diagnosis |

## Source and safety boundary

The reel uses “mental time travel” as a scientific construct linked to remembering personal past episodes and imagining possible personal futures. It distinguishes objective task performance from subjective report and avoids claiming that memories are exact recordings or that imagined futures are predictions. It does not diagnose memory disorders, infer personality, or give advice based on a viewer’s ability to remember or imagine.

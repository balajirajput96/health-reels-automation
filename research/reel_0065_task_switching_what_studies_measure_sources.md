# Reel 0065 — Task switching: what studies measure

## Research question

When a study reports a “task-switching cost,” which controlled comparison produced it, and which cognitive processes can or cannot be inferred from that comparison?

## Safe operational categories

**Switch cost:** reaction-time or accuracy difference between a switch trial and a repeat trial in a specified design.

**Mixing cost:** contrast between performance in a mixed-task block and a pure or single-task condition, when the design includes that comparison.

**Cueing and preparation:** effects of advance cues, cue-switches, and preparation intervals on readiness for the next task.

**Task-set reconfiguration and interference:** proposed processes involved in activating a new task rule and overcoming residual activation or interference from a prior task set.

**Response and context effects:** response repetition, feature binding, context repetition, priming, and trial-order effects that can alter the apparent cost.

**Model-derived components:** diffusion-model or latent-variable estimates that attempt to separate processes such as evidence accumulation, non-decision time, response caution, or preparation. These are estimates conditional on a model, not directly observed mental quantities.

**Behavioral outcomes:** reaction time, accuracy, errors, omissions, and variability under controlled instructions. They are not direct readouts of thought content or a complete measure of daily life multitasking.

## Safe conclusion

Task-switching studies measure how performance changes when people shift between specified task sets, cues, responses, and contexts. The resulting contrasts can inform task preparation, reconfiguration, interference, retrieval, and response selection. Because several components can contribute to the same contrast, interpretation must name the exact design and comparison. One switch-cost score cannot diagnose a person, rank their worth, or guarantee superior multitasking outside the task.

## Safety and editorial boundaries

This is public education, not diagnosis, treatment, prescription, or individualized cognitive assessment. Do not label a person inflexible, unintelligent, lazy, or clinically impaired from one switch cost. Do not claim that a small laboratory cost proves effortless real-world multitasking or that a large cost proves a fixed deficit.

## Evidence boundaries

This reel is general education about reading a U.S.-style Nutrition Facts label. It is not individualized dietary, medical, weight-loss, diabetes, or allergy advice. Serving size is a label reference based on typical consumption, not a recommended amount. The 5%/20% guide and the 50 g Daily Value are general label references tied to the FDA framework and a 2,000-calorie reference diet; individual needs can differ. The narration must avoid “sugar is poison,” “this label proves a food is unhealthy,” or assuranced-outcome language.

## Source notes

The FDA’s added-sugars page states that the Daily Value for added sugars is 50 grams per day based on a 2,000-calorie diet and gives 5% DV or less as low and 20% DV or more as high. The FDA’s broader label guide states that nutrient amounts and %DV refer to the serving size, that serving size is not a recommendation of how much to eat, and that total sugars include naturally occurring and added sugars. The CDC page states that serving size reflects what people are likely to consume and is not necessarily a recommended portion; it also distinguishes naturally occurring sugars from added sugars.

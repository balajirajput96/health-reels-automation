# Reel 0003 source validation — implementation intentions

## Editorial question

Can a specific “if–then” plan help translate an intention into action, and what should viewers not infer from that evidence?

## Verified sources

1. **Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006). _Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes._ Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38, 69–119.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1
   - The review analyzes 94 independent tests.
   - Its abstract reports a positive medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65) for implementation intentions that specify when, where, and how goal-directed action will begin.
   - Proposed processes include making specified opportunities more accessible and automating the corresponding goal-directed response.
   - Editorial limitation: this is evidence about goal-striving effects across tested contexts, not a assurance that any if–then plan will create a lasting habit or overcome every barrier.

2. **Gollwitzer (1999), _Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans._ American Psychologist.** Accessible teaching copy: https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf
   - The core format is “If situation Y is encountered, then I will initiate goal-directed behavior X.”
   - Keep the reel focused on the planning mechanism, not on exaggerated productivity claims.

3. **National Cancer Institute, Division of Cancer Control & Population Sciences, _Implementation Intentions_.** https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/research/constructs/implementation-intentions
   - Provides an institutional explanation that forming if–then plans can promote translating intention into action.
   - Use as explanatory context, not as a substitute for the peer-reviewed meta-analysis.

4. **Adriaanse et al. (2011), _Breaking Habits With Implementation Intentions: A Test of Underlying Processes._ Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167211399102
   - Specific replacement plans have been studied for responding differently in critical situations; this supports a cautious example about preparing an alternative response, not a universal habit-breaking resolution.

5. **Trenz (2024), _Promoting new habits at work through implementation intentions._ Journal of Occupational and Organizational Psychology.** https://doi.org/10.1111/joop.12540
   - Search results identify a habit-formation application in work settings. Use only after checking the full article when making any detailed claim; do not invent sample sizes or effect estimates from the snippet.

## Claim classification

| Claim | Classification | Allowed wording |
|---|---|---|
| An if–then plan specifies a cue and a response | Established construct definition | “अगर X situation आए, तो Y action शुरू करूँगा।” |
| Implementation intentions improved goal attainment across 94 tests in the 2006 meta-analysis | Peer-reviewed meta-analytic evidence | “Meta-analysis ने positive average effect report किया।” |
| If–then planning assurances a habit | Unsupported universal claim | Do not say “काम पक्का होगा” or “habit automatically बन जाएगी।” |
| A plan can make the cue and intended response more accessible | Proposed/empirically supported process in the review | Use “help कर सकता है,” not certainty. |
| The method works equally for everyone and every goal | Unsupported | Explicitly reject universal assurances. |

## Safety boundary

This is general behavioural-science education, not medical, psychiatric, or individualized management advice. Avoid productivity shame, clinical assessment, or claims that planning alone defeats structural barriers, disability, illness, or severe distress.

## Full-page verification notes

The NCI page defines implementation intentions as if–then plans linking situational cues with responses intended to attain goals, and explicitly distinguishes them from goal intentions. It also describes the intention–action gap and says accumulated evidence supports improved goal attainment, while noting that strong goal intentions alone do not assurance action. The page states that it is a reference page no longer maintained, so it is used only for definitions and context, not as the primary effect estimate.

The ScienceDirect preview confirms that Gollwitzer and Sheeran’s 2006 chapter is a review article in *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, volume 38, pages 69–119. Its abstract reports 94 independent tests and a positive medium-to-large average effect (d = .65) on goal attainment, with proposed processes including increased accessibility of specified opportunities and automated goal-directed responses. It also explicitly says strong goal intention by itself does not assurance goal achievement.

The SAGE abstract for Adriaanse et al. (2011) reports three experiments on counterhabitual implementation intentions. It found that such plans removed the cognitive accessibility advantage of a habitual response over an alternative response in the tested task, but did not immediately create automatic activation of the alternative; actual departure from habits depended on strong alternative goal intentions. This supports cautious language about flexibility and response selection, not a claim of assuranced habit-breaking.

A PMC browser page was protected by reCAPTCHA during direct navigation, so no unsupported details were extracted from it. The accessible full-page sources above are sufficient for the planned reel’s claims.

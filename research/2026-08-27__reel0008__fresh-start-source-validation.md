# Reel 0008 — Fresh-start effect: source validation

**Validation date:** 2026-08-27  
**Topic:** Temporal landmarks, fresh-start moments, and goal initiation  
**Language/output:** Hindi (India), approximately 60–75 seconds, general education  
**Status:** Validated for production with bounded claims

## Research conclusion

The evidence supports a **fresh-start effect** in which salient temporal landmarks can increase motivation for, or engagement in, goal-related initiation and aspirational behaviour. The relevant landmarks include the beginning of a week, month, year, or semester, birthdays, holidays, and other personally meaningful transitions. The studies do **not** establish that a temporal landmark guarantees goal completion, sustained motivation, or durable habit formation. Reel 0008 therefore frames a landmark as a possible **temporary motivation window**, not as a brain law, treatment, or magic mechanism.

## Source 1 — 2014 field-study paper

**Citation:** Dai, H., Milkman, K. L., & Riis, J. (2014). *The Fresh Start Effect: Temporal Landmarks Motivate Aspirational Behavior*. **Management Science, 60(10)**. DOI: 10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901.

**Verified page:** [INFORMS publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)

The publisher page identifies the three authors, the journal, volume 60, issue 10, publication date 23 June 2014, and the article title. Its abstract states that across three archival field studies, Google searches for “diet,” gym visits, and commitments to pursue goals increased following temporal landmarks such as the start of a new week, month, year, or semester, a birthday, or a holiday. The abstract proposes that landmarks create new mental-accounting periods, relegate past imperfections to a previous period, induce a broader view of life, and motivate aspirational behaviour.

**Interpretation used:** This source supports the existence of field-observed associations around salient temporal landmarks and the authors’ proposed explanation. Because the abstract describes archival field studies, the reel does not treat every result as proof that the landmark alone caused lasting behaviour change.

## Source 2 — 2015 causal laboratory paper

**Citation:** Dai, H., Milkman, K. L., & Riis, J. (2015). *Put Your Imperfections behind You: Temporal Landmarks Spur Goal Initiation When They Signal New Beginnings*. **Psychological Science, 26(12), 1927–1936**. DOI: 10.1177/0956797615605818. PMCID: PMC4839284.

**Verified page:** [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839284/)

The open-access article identifies the final publication details and reports five laboratory experiments. Its abstract states that the authors provide causal evidence that emphasizing a temporal landmark marking the beginning of a new time period increases people’s intentions to initiate goal pursuit. It also reports that the strengthened motivation may originate partly from psychological disassociation between the person’s present self and past, imperfect self.

The introduction defines temporal landmarks as days that stand out from ordinary events. It gives examples including holidays, the start of a new week, month, year, or semester, first experiences, developmental milestones, and birthdays. The article notes that how strongly a landmark feels like a new beginning can depend on cultural, occupational, religious, or personal meaning.

The article’s Study 1 reports randomized framing of otherwise similar dates. In one experiment, describing a date as the first day of spring increased the selection of a goal reminder compared with describing it as an ordinary day; in another, describing a date as the first day of a university summer break increased reminder selection compared with an administrative day. Study 2 reports greater engagement with goal-related websites after participants reflected on the personal significance of New Year’s compared with an ordinary framing. These outcomes concern goal reminders, intentions, and goal-related engagement rather than guaranteed completion or long-term habit retention.

**Interpretation used:** This source supports careful causal language about **goal initiation and intentions when a landmark signals a new beginning**. It does not license the claim that temporal landmarks automatically create a stable habit or ensure success.

## Source 3 — 2019 review

**Citation:** Dai, H., & Li, C. (2019). *How Experiencing and Anticipating Temporal Landmarks Influence Motivation*. **Current Opinion in Psychology, 26, 44–48**. DOI: 10.1016/j.copsyc.2018.04.012. PMID: 29753243.

**Verified page:** [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29753243/)

The PubMed record identifies this as a 2019 review in *Current Opinion in Psychology*. Its abstract states that temporal landmarks structure people’s perceptions and use of time. It reviews evidence that experiencing a temporal landmark may produce a fresh-start effect and make people feel more motivated to pursue goals right after the landmark. It also says that anticipating a future landmark may affect current motivation, reviews psychological separation between past, current, and future selves as one explanation, and discusses circumstances under which landmark effects may stop being motivating or may even harm motivation.

**Interpretation used:** This review is used to reinforce the caveat that the effect is conditional and may not always help. It supports the reel’s distinction between a possible short-term motivational lift and durable behaviour change.

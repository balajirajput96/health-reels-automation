# R0070 Browser Research Findings: Dehumanization — What Studies Measure

## Source 1: Simon & Gutsell (2021), Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8094996/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsab014

The face-to-face dyadic study operationalized dehumanization as variation in how human participants evaluated one another. It also measured empathic accuracy in interaction, cooperation on a task, and EEG mu-suppression as an index of neural mirroring during action observation. The abstract reports that participants showed stronger neural mirroring toward partners they evaluated as more human, were marginally more empathically accurate with those partners, and performed better with them on a cooperative task. These are separate behavioral and neural outcomes in a defined interaction design.

The paper also states that recognition of another person’s humanity is contextually contingent and that the study measured ordinary face-to-face interactions between strangers. Safe interpretation: a rating, cooperation score, empathic-accuracy outcome, or EEG correlate in this design does not directly reveal a stable universal attitude or private mental state. Associations between measures do not by themselves establish a general trait or causality.

## Source 3: Pizzirani, Karantzas, & Mullins (2019), Frontiers in Psychology
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6908499/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02754

This study developed and validated the Dehumanization in Romantic Relationships Scale (DIRRS). The abstract and results describe self-report measurement of dehumanization perpetration and targeting within close relationships, with factor-analytic and criterion-validity work across studies. The paper’s construct is tied to relationship behaviors and appraisals in romantic relationships, not a universal measure of attitudes toward every person or group.

Safe interpretation: a validated scale can measure a defined construct in a defined population and context. Its factor structure, reliability, and associations with relationship outcomes do not automatically establish a general dehumanization trait, direct access to private beliefs, or causation.

## Working evidence boundary

Dehumanization research may measure explicit humanness or humanity ratings, self-reported treatment or experience in a relationship, behavioral outcomes such as cooperation or empathic accuracy, implicit association or categorization performance, and physiological or neural correlates. These methods answer different questions. The reel should avoid presenting an implicit association score, an EEG index, a target rating, or a relationship questionnaire as a complete readout of a person’s mind or moral character.

# Reel 0037 — implementation intentions: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence question

An implementation intention is an advance if-then plan that links a specified situation to a goal-directed response. It is different from a goal intention such as “I want to exercise” or “I intend to reach a result.” Research can measure whether a person formed the plan, whether the critical cue becomes more accessible, whether the planned response starts, whether behavior is repeated or maintained, and whether a physiological or neural correlate changes. These outcomes should not be collapsed into one score.

## Measurement layers

| Layer | Example operationalization | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Goal intention | Stated commitment or desired outcome | An intention or commitment, not proof of later action |
| If-then plan | Participant specifies when, where, and how to act | Plan formation is an intervention or construct, not behavior itself |
| Cue processing | Lexical decision, attention, memory, or response-initiation task | A cognitive process measure; not necessarily real-world adherence |
| Immediate goal attainment | Coupon pickup, task completion, or target response | Behavior in the tested situation and timeframe |
| Longer-term behavior | Follow-up self-report, records, or adherence checks | Depends on follow-up length, measurement quality, and attrition |
| Physiology/neural correlates | Heart rate, cortisol, eye movements, EEG, or fMRI | A process correlate; not a guarantee of successful behavior |
| Moderators | Goal type, obstacle, context, participant characteristics, and flexibility costs | Effects may vary across tasks and people |

## Vetted scholarly sources

1. Wieber, F., Thürmer, J. L., & Gollwitzer, P. M. (2015). “Promoting the translation of intentions into action by implementation intentions: behavioral effects and physiological correlates.” *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 9, 395. DOI: [10.3389/fnhum.2015.00395](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00395); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500900/). The review states that strong intentions do not guarantee subsequent action and describes if-then plans as specifying when, where, and how to act. It surveys behavioral measures of cue accessibility, response initiation, goal attainment, and prospective memory, as well as heart rate, cortisol, eye movements, EEG, and fMRI. These are distinct process and outcome measures.

2. Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006). “Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes.” *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 38, 69–119. DOI: [10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1); [University of Konstanz record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f). The repository abstract reports 94 independent tests and a positive average effect on goal attainment (d=.65), with effects described for initiating goal striving, shielding it from unwanted influences, disengaging from failing courses, and preserving capability. This pooled estimate is not a guarantee for an individual or every behavior.

3. Bieleke, M., Keller, L., & Gollwitzer, P. M. (2021). “If-then planning.” *European Review of Social Psychology*, 32(1), 88–122. DOI: [10.1080/10463283.2020.1808936](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2020.1808936); [full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10463283.2020.1808936). The review distinguishes goal intentions from implementation intentions, discusses strategic automaticity, flexible versus tenacious goal striving, behavior in non-planned situations, moderators, and unanswered empirical questions. It supports a conditional research framing rather than a magic habit-button claim.

## Claim boundaries

The reel will say that implementation intentions can help translate an intention into action under some tested conditions, while emphasizing that plans, cue processing, immediate task performance, longer-term adherence, and physiology are different measurements. It will not promise habit formation, infer motivation from brain or heart signals, prescribe a behavioral plan to the viewer, or imply that one study applies to every goal. “d=.65” will be presented as the reported meta-analytic pooled estimate, not as a personal forecast.

# REEL-0071 Source Validation: Starting Physical Activity with Small, Sustainable Steps

## Editorial question

How can a reel encourage inactive adults to begin physical activity gradually without turning a population-level guideline into a personal prescription or promising assuranced long-term adherence?

## Claim register

| Claim | Evidence class | Support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Some physical activity is better than none, and activity can be accumulated in smaller chunks. | Public-health guidance | WHO states that some physical activity is better than none; CDC says adults can spread activity across the week and break it into smaller chunks [1] [2]. | This is general guidance, not a assurance that a particular small amount will produce a specific health outcome. |
| People who are inactive can start with small amounts and gradually increase frequency, intensity, and duration. | WHO good-practice guidance | The WHO guideline recommendation text advises starting with small amounts and gradually increasing frequency, intensity, and duration over time [3]. | “Start small” is not a fixed universal dose. The appropriate progression depends on ability, symptoms, age, environment, and access. |
| CDC recommends starting slowly and working toward more time or more challenging activity. | Public-health guidance | CDC’s getting-started page explicitly advises people to start slowly and work up to more time or more challenging activities [2]. | Do not convert this into a rigid 5-, 10-, or 30-minute prescription for every viewer. |
| Adult guidelines commonly describe 150 minutes of moderate activity per week plus muscle strengthening on 2 days. | Population-level guideline | CDC’s adult overview describes those broad recommendations, and WHO provides comparable adult recommendations [1] [4]. | The reel should not imply that viewers must jump immediately to the full target or that meeting it assurances health outcomes. |
| Choosing an enjoyable, accessible activity and placing it into a routine may make starting more practical. | Practical synthesis | CDC suggests choosing activities, locations, and times people enjoy and setting aside specific times [2]. | This is a behavior-design suggestion, not proof of assuranced adherence or motivation. |

## Editorial boundaries

The reel will frame gradual progression as a general starting principle, not individualized medical or exercise advice. It will avoid exact beginner prescriptions, assuranced outcomes, weight-loss promises, and claims that small steps alone meet any person’s complete guideline target.

The script will state that people with chronic conditions may want to speak with a healthcare professional about suitable types and amounts of activity, consistent with CDC guidance. It will not instruct viewers to push through pain or concerning symptoms.

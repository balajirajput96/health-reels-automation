# Reel 0031 source-retrieval findings

On 2026-08-25, public text extraction was used after a PMC page presented a reCAPTCHA.

1. Matthews and Simpson, “Evaluation of Behavior Change Interventions,” in *The Handbook of Behavior Change* (Cambridge University Press, 2020), DOI 10.1017/9781108677318.022, states that rigorous evaluation should distinguish efficacy, real-world effectiveness, how an intervention works, and how it interacts with context. It describes experimental, quasi-experimental, and nonexperimental designs and stresses choosing relevant primary and secondary outcomes while considering implementation and wider context.

2. Middleton, Anton, and Perri, “Long-Term Adherence to Health Behavior Change,” *American Journal of Lifestyle Medicine* (2013), DOI 10.1177/1559827613488867, reviews the challenge of maintaining behavior changes over the long term and distinguishes adherence, relapse, and maintenance from initial response. This source is used only to support the general measurement distinction; the reel makes no personal health recommendation.

3. Conner and Norman, “Attitudes, Intentions, and Behavior Change,” *Annual Review of Psychology* (2026), DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-013125-042110, reports that attitudes and intentions have small-to-medium relationships with behavior change, while experimental changes in attitudes or intentions produce smaller changes in behavior. The reel uses this to explain the intention–behavior gap, not to predict an individual.

4. The Michie et al. 2018 behavior-change-technique evaluation source was attempted via PMC but returned a reCAPTCHA page. No unsupported detail from that page is used; the Cambridge chapter and openly extracted reviews provide the methods basis.

# R0072 Browser Research Findings: Intrinsic Motivation — What Studies Measure

## Source 1: Di Domenico & Ryan (2017), The Emerging Neuroscience of Intrinsic Motivation
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364176/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00145

The review describes intrinsic motivation as interest in and inherent satisfaction from an activity, contrasted with engagement for separable rewards. It states that research assesses intrinsic motivation behaviorally through freely pursued activities, experientially through self-report questionnaires about reasons for engagement and states such as interest, curiosity, and fun, and in some laboratory work through exploratory or manipulatory behaviors and facial displays of interested engagement. The review also describes emerging neuroscience research on motivational processes and large-scale networks. Safe interpretation: a free-choice or persistence outcome is a behavioral indicator under a specific protocol; a questionnaire measures reported experience or reasons; a neural pattern is a correlate of a process, not a direct readout of a person’s private motive.

## Source 2: Luther et al. (2018), A meta-analytic review of self-reported, clinician-rated, and performance-based motivation measures
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6083441/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2018.04.001

This meta-analysis compared self-reported, clinician-rated, and performance-based motivation measures in a clinical literature. Its abstract reports modest convergence between clinician-rated measures and self-report or performance-based measures, while the available self-report versus performance-based comparison was negligible and nonsignificant but underpowered. The authors conclude that these methods should not be used interchangeably and should be described as the specific motivational construct or domain they capture. Safe interpretation: different streams can diverge because they measure different aspects, contexts, or observables; a single score does not establish a stable universal motivation trait.

## Source 3: Intrinsic Motivation Inventory documentation
URL: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/intrinsic-motivation-inventory/

The documentation identifies the Interest/Enjoyment subscale as the self-report measure of intrinsic motivation itself. Other subscales address perceived choice, perceived competence, effort, value/usefulness, pressure/tension, and relatedness; these are related constructs, predictors, or context-specific dimensions rather than interchangeable parts of one score. The items are often modified for the particular activity, and the site cautions that the inventory should be interpreted for a particular activity rather than as a universal trait. It also notes that correlations between self-report and behavioral indices can be modest and influenced by ego involvement and self-presentation. Safe interpretation: enjoyment reported during one activity is not proof of the same motivation in every activity or setting.

## Working evidence boundary

Intrinsic-motivation studies can measure reported interest and enjoyment, perceived choice or competence, effort and value, free-choice engagement or persistence, exploratory behavior, performance, or neural correlates. Each is tied to a construct and protocol. A behavioral choice may reflect opportunity, task design, or context; a self-report reflects the participant’s reported experience; a neural signal is a correlate. These streams should not be collapsed into a universal motivation meter.

# Reel 0047 — रिट्रीवल प्रैक्टिस: studies वास्तव में क्या मापते हैं?

## Evidence-bounded claim

Retrieval practice is an observable attempt to recall information, but studies can measure several different things: opportunities assigned, questions answered, correctness, delayed retention, final-test performance, or transfer to new formats. These variables are related but not interchangeable. Participation in a quiz is not automatically successful retrieval, and one test score is not a complete measure of learning.

## Source 1 — Applied classroom study

**Citation:** Azzam, M. B., & Easteal, R. A. (2021). *Retrieval Practice for Improving Long-Term Retention in Anatomical Education: A Quasi-Experimental Study*. Medical Science Educator, 31, 1305–1310. DOI: [10.1007/s40670-021-01298-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40670-021-01298-8).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed quasi-experimental classroom research.

**Design and exposure:** The study included 248 undergraduate students in a Gross Anatomy course. Retrieval practice occurred during the first ten minutes of lectures, with six to ten questions per lecture and 252 questions across the semester. Correct-answer feedback was provided one day after each session. A final examination occurred approximately three weeks after the course ended.

**Operational measures:** De-identified platform data counted the number of questions each student answered, regardless of correctness. Students were split at an 85% participation cut-off into higher- and lower-participation groups. The outcome was final-exam performance, including 67 questions considered congruent with the retrieval-practice content.

**Reported result:** The higher-participation group had higher average scores on congruent final-exam questions. Participation correlated moderately and positively with final-exam performance. The study concludes that classroom retrieval practice was associated with improved performance and long-term retention in this anatomy context.

**Limitations:** This was not randomized. The authors note that conscientiousness, interest, prior preparation, and other factors may confound the association. Students may also have answered questions together, so a platform response does not always prove an individual retrieval attempt. The finding is therefore bounded to one course, one population, and the tested outcome.

## Source 2 — Transfer meta-analysis

**Citation:** Pan, S. C., & Rickard, T. C. (2018). *Transfer of Test-Enhanced Learning: Meta-Analytic Review and Synthesis*. Psychological Bulletin, 144(7), 710–756. DOI: [10.1037/bul0000151](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed meta-analysis.

**Scope:** The review synthesized 192 transfer effect sizes from 122 experiments and 67 published or unpublished articles, totaling 10,382 participants. A random-effects model found a positive average transfer effect relative to a non-testing re-exposure control: d = 0.40, 95% CI [0.31, 0.50].

**Moderators:** Transfer was stronger across some test formats and for application or inference questions under particular conditions. It was weaker for some rearranged stimulus-response items, untested materials, and worked examples. Response congruency, elaborated retrieval practice, and initial test performance influenced the likelihood of positive transfer.

**Limitations:** The average effect is not a promise that retrieval practice transfers to every new task. The abstract reports that publication-bias adjustments reduced some intercept predictions and often indicated no positive transfer when the named moderators were absent. Transfer depends on the material, new task, test format, feedback or elaboration, and initial performance.

## Source 3 — Self-initiated study behavior

**Citation:** Karpicke, J. D., Butler, A. C., & Roediger, H. L. (2009). *Metacognitive strategies in student learning: Do students practise retrieval when they study on their own?* Memory, 17(4), 471–479. DOI: [10.1080/09658210802647009](https://doi.org/10.1080/09658210802647009).

**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed original memory research.

**Use in this reel:** This source is included to distinguish a learner’s self-reported or observed study strategy from a later performance outcome. The reel does not add numerical claims from the paper beyond that conceptual distinction.

**Measurement boundary:** What learners say they do, what a platform logs, and what they later recall are separate data streams. A study strategy label is not the same as verified learning or transfer.

## Language and safety controls

Use “attempted recall,” “participation count,” “answer correctness,” “delayed test,” “congruent questions,” and “transfer under specified conditions.” Say “associated with” for the quasi-experimental classroom result. Do not say “quizzes guarantee memory,” “a log proves retrieval,” “the effect transfers everywhere,” or “one score measures intelligence.” No diagnosis, individualized educational prescription, ability label, or performance guarantee will be given.

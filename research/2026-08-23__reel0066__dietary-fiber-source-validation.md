# REEL-0066 evidence register — dietary fiber and varied eating patterns

## Editorial question

How can a Hindi reel explain the role of dietary fiber in a varied eating pattern without turning population guidance or emerging microbiome research into a personalized diet prescription?

## Primary and secondary sources

### [1] World Health Organization — Healthy diet

URL: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet

Evidence type: Established public-health guidance.

The WHO describes a diversified, balanced and healthy diet as dependent on individual characteristics, cultural context, locally available foods and dietary customs. It identifies diversity as including a wide variety of nutritious foods within and across food groups. The page says dietary carbohydrates should come primarily from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses, with examples such as oats, wheat, brown rice, lentils, chickpeas, beans and dried peas. It states that people older than 10 years should aim for at least 25 grams per day of naturally occurring dietary fibre from foods, while noting different amounts for younger children. It also says fresh, frozen and canned fruits and vegetables can be good choices when they do not contain added sugars or excess sodium.

Boundary: This is population-level guidance, not a universal personal target. The exact make-up of a healthy diet varies by individual characteristics and context.

### [2] Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health — Fiber

URL: https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/carbohydrates/fiber/

Evidence type: University educational synthesis.

Harvard explains that fiber is a type of carbohydrate the body cannot digest and identifies whole grains, whole fruits and vegetables, legumes and nuts as useful food sources. It distinguishes soluble and insoluble fiber and discusses different physiological properties. The page’s practical guidance supports gradual changes and attention to tolerance and fluids rather than an abrupt large increase.

Boundary: This educational explainer does not justify claiming that fiber alone prevents or treats a disease in every person.

### [3] Fu et al. — Dietary Fiber Intake and Gut Microbiota in Human Health

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9787832/

Citation: Fu J, Zheng Y, Gao Y, Xu W. Dietary Fiber Intake and Gut Microbiota in Human Health. *Microorganisms*. 2022;10(12):2507. doi:10.3390/microorganisms10122507. PMCID: PMC9787832. PMID: 36557760.

Evidence type: Peer-reviewed review; mechanistic and translational synthesis.

The review explains that gut microbiota can ferment dietary fiber from plant foods and produce metabolites including short-chain fatty acids. It reports that effects vary with fiber type, amount, duration, population and intervention context. It also describes shifts in microbial composition in some high-fiber patterns and interventions while noting inconsistent findings for measures such as alpha diversity.

Boundary: The reel may present the gut-microbe connection as a research-supported biological pathway and active research area. It must not claim that one food assurances a particular microbiome change, weight outcome, disease prevention or management.

## Claim classification for the reel

| Planned claim | Classification | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| Varied eating patterns can include whole grains, fruits, vegetables and pulses/legumes | Established public-health guidance | “WHO guidance includes…” |
| These foods can provide naturally occurring dietary fiber | Established nutrition fact | “ये foods fiber के sources हो सकते हैं” |
| Fiber is not digested like ordinary digestible carbohydrate | Established educational explanation | “Fiber शरीर में उसी तरह digest नहीं होता” |
| Fiber interacts with gut microbes | Supported mechanism and review evidence | “Gut microbes fiber को ferment कर सकते हैं; यह research का active area है” |
| Increasing fiber gradually may be easier to tolerate | Practical guidance | “धीरे-धीरे बढ़ाना helpful हो सकता है” |
| A fixed gram target is right for everyone | Not supported as a universal claim | Do not say this |
| Fiber assurances weight loss, disease prevention or management | Not supported for this reel | Do not say this |

## Safety boundary

This is general education only. It does not diagnose, address or prescribe a diet. People with digestive disease, significant symptoms, dietary restrictions, pregnancy-related needs, or clinician-directed nutrition plans should consult a qualified clinician or registered dietitian before making substantial changes.

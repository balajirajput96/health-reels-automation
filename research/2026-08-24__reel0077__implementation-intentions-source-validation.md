# REEL-0077 Source Validation: If–Then Planning for Habits

## Editorial question

How can a reel explain implementation intentions as a planning strategy without presenting an if–then plan as a guaranteed habit-maker, medical intervention, or substitute for adapting to real-world constraints?

## Claim register

| Claim | Evidence class | Support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| An implementation intention links a situational cue with a goal-directed response in an if–then format. | Recognized behavioral-science framework | The NCI reference page and Wieber et al. define the structure as “If situation Y occurs, then I will initiate action Z” [1] [2]. | The plan should specify a realistic cue and feasible action; it is not a magic phrase. |
| Strong goals alone do not guarantee that intentions become behavior. | Review/meta-analytic summary | The NCI page and Wieber et al. describe an intention–behavior gap [1] [2]. | Do not state a universal percentage or imply that planning removes all obstacles. |
| Across varied studies, implementation intentions were associated with better goal attainment than goal intentions alone. | Review/meta-analysis summary | Wieber et al. summarize a prior meta-analysis of 94 studies and more than 8,000 participants with a medium-to-large average effect [2]. | This is an average across different tasks and populations. It does not guarantee success for a specific person or habit. |
| A possible process is that the specified cue becomes more accessible and the linked response easier to initiate. | Mechanistic review | Wieber et al. discuss cue accessibility and stronger cue–response links [2]. | Use “proposed process” or “may make the cue easier to notice,” not permanent brain rewiring or automatic behavior for everyone. |
| A practical example can connect a routine cue with a small action. | Practical synthesis | The framework supports examples such as linking dinner with preparing for a walk [1] [2]. | Keep the action optional, safe, accessible, and adaptable. No exercise, weight-loss, medication, or medical prescription should be inferred. |

## Editorial boundaries

The reel will contrast a vague goal—“I want to walk more”—with a concrete plan—“अगर dinner के बाद walk करनी है, तो shoes पहले से door के पास रखूँगा।” It will say the plan may reduce remembering or starting friction, but it cannot guarantee a habit or overcome every barrier.

The reel will not claim that if–then plans rewire the brain, create automatic discipline, improve health markers, or work equally well for every goal. It will state that a plan should be revised when the cue is unrealistic, the action is unsafe, or circumstances change.

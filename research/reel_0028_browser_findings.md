# Reel 0028 browser findings

On 2026-08-25, two public sources were opened.

1. Legros and Cislaghi, “Mapping the Social-Norms Literature: An Overview of Reviews” (*Perspectives on Psychological Science*, DOI 10.1177/1745691619866455; PMC6970459), was read in full-text view. The review describes social norms as a multifaceted and sometimes contradictory literature, with recurring questions about what norms are, how norms relate to behavior, how norms evolve, and which actors or reference groups matter. This supports careful distinction between constructs instead of treating “the norm” as a single observable fact.

2. Chang and Sanfey, “Great expectations: neural computations underlying the use of social norms in decision-making” (*Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*, DOI 10.1093/scan/nsr094), was extracted from a public page. The study combined a social bargaining task, computational modeling, and fMRI, reporting that a model based on social expectations explained behavior and that expectation violations were tracked in anterior cingulate cortex. This supports the wording “task-specific computational and neural correlate,” not direct reading of a person’s beliefs or universal norm detection.

A browser captcha blocked direct access to parts of the Oxford page, but its extracted article text included the title, DOI, abstract, methods focus, and limitations relevant to this evidence record.
